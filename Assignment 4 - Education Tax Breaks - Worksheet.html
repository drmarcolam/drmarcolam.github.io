Education tax breaks — the Browns

In 2026, Margery and Philip Brown have three children attending college full-time and they claim all three as dependents (all three children are over age 17). They also have education-related expenses for Philip. They paid for the following expenses:

1. Their daughter, Amy, is a Masters student in both Spring 2026 and Fall 2026 at Wake Forest where she received a scholarship for part of her tuition. Her parents paid a total of tuition of $13,000 and room & board of $12,000 (all paid in 2026).

2. Their son, Brad, is a freshman in Spring 2026 and a sophomore in Fall 2026 at Appalachian State University. His parents paid a total of tuition and fees of $8,400 and room & board of $10,000 (all paid in 2026).

3. Their daughter, Elizabeth, started at UNC-CH as a freshman in Fall 2026. She receives a scholarship that covers her tuition, but her parents paid required fees of $2,000. Her parents also paid room & board of $7,000 and textbooks for $1,200.

4. Philip is a self-employed lawyer. He completed continuing education courses (not taken at a qualified higher education institution) that are required to meet the requirements of North Carolina State law and cost $4,200. (You can ignore any effects on self-employment tax.)

5. Philip is also paying off student loans from law school. He paid $3,200 of interest in 2026.

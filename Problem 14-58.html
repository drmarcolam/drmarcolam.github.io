Reference: rental use of a home (§280A)

Tamar’s personal use is more than 14 days and more than 10% of the rental days, so the condo is a residence with significant rental use: expenses are allocated between rental and personal use, and the rental deductions cannot exceed the gross rental income.

Allocating the expenses — IRS method
  • Expenses that relate to the property as a whole are allocated by rental days ÷ total days of use (rental days + personal days). Days the home is vacant do not count.
  • Expenses that relate only to the rental activity (advertising, rental agency fees) are not allocated — they belong 100% to the rental.
  • Under the Tax Court method, mortgage interest and property taxes are instead allocated by rental days ÷ 365. This worksheet uses the IRS method.

Rental ratio = rental days ÷ (rental days + personal days)

The three tiers and the gross-income limit
  • Tier 1 — direct rental expenses (advertising) and the rental share of mortgage interest and real property taxes.
  • Tier 2 — the rental share of the other operating expenses (insurance, repairs and maintenance, utilities), deductible only to the extent of the balance of rental income remaining.
  • Tier 3 — the rental share of depreciation, deductible only to the extent of the balance remaining after tiers 1 and 2.
  • Deductions are taken in that order and may not exceed the gross rental income; the disallowed amounts carry forward to next year.
What goes on Schedule A, and basis
  • The personal share of the mortgage interest (qualified residence interest) and real property taxes is an itemized deduction, subject to the 2026 state and local tax limit of $40,400.
  • Tier 1 interest and taxes that exceed the gross rental income are also deductible on Schedule A, because they are deductible whether or not the home is rented.
  • Only the depreciation actually deducted reduces the basis of the property.

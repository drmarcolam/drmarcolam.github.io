LO 5-1 Revenue Recognition

Revenue recognition at Pfizer

From Pfizer's revenue recognition policy note: "We record revenues from product sales when there is a transfer of control of the product from us to the customer. We determine transfer of control based on when the product is shipped or delivered and title passes to the customer . . . Our gross product revenues are subject to a variety of deductions, which generally are estimated and recorded in the same period that the revenues are recognized. Such variable consideration represents chargebacks, rebates, sales allowances, and sales returns."

Chargebacks, rebates, sales allowances, and sales returns are deducted from GROSS REVENUE.

Four issues drive the analysis of revenue for a company like Pfizer:

  • Revenue recognition timing
  • Sales and related allowances
  • Deferred revenue — advance payments from customers
  • Foreign currency exchange rates

Sales on credit

  • Many sales are on credit, meaning the customer has agreed to pay the company in the future.
  • The company recognizes revenue when the good or service is transferred to the customer, and records an account receivable to be collected later.
  • Revenue recognition is unaffected by the delayed receipt of cash if the company has fulfilled its performance obligation.

The five-step revenue recognition rule

StepWhat it requires
1. Identify the contract(s) with the customerParties to the contract should be identifiable; terms of the sale should be specified.
2. Identify the performance obligation(s) in the contractA performance obligation is a contractual promise to transfer a good or service to the customer. For contracts with more than one good or service, the company must identify separate performance obligations for each contractual promise.
3. Determine the transaction priceIf the selling price is variable, estimate revenue using the expected selling price.
4. Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligation(s)With more than one performance obligation, allocate the transaction price to each at its fair value (standalone selling price). If standalone prices are not available, use a reasonable estimate.
5. Recognize revenue as / when each performance obligation is satisfiedA performance obligation is satisfied when the customer obtains control of the goods or services. Obligations satisfied over a period of time are recognized as revenue over time.

Performance obligations satisfied over time: the cost-to-cost method
Percentage complete = Costs incurred to date ÷ Total estimated costs at completion
Revenue recognized = Contract price × Percentage complete
  • Revenue is recognized over the life of a long-term contract in amounts that track the percentage of completion.
  • If a company incurs 15% of the total expected cost in the current period, it recognizes revenues equal to 15% of the contract amount.

Raytheon's 2022 disclosure: "For performance obligations satisfied over time, revenue is recognized on a percentage of completion basis generally using costs incurred to date relative to total estimated costs at completion to measure progress."

LO 5-2 Sales Allowances

Why allowances exist

  • Many companies offer customers a variety of sales allowances: rights of return, sales discounts for volume purchases, and retailer promotions (point-of-sale price markdowns and other promotions).
  • These reduce the amount of cash the company receives.
  • Under GAAP, companies must report the amount of cash expected to be received (NET sales), deducting from GROSS sales the expected sales returns and other allowances.

LO 5-3 Unearned (Deferred) Revenue

  • In some industries it is common to receive cash before recording revenue. Deposits or advance payments are not recorded as revenue until the company performs the services owed or delivers the goods.
  • The company must record a liability (unearned revenue) because it is obligated to deliver those products and services.
  • When the good is provided or the service rendered, the unearned revenue liability is reduced and revenue is recognized.
  • Unearned revenue is particularly common among companies that receive advance payments, sell gift cards, or sell memberships or subscriptions.

Walmart's policy note illustrates all three: net sales are recognized net of sales taxes and estimated sales returns at the time merchandise or services are sold; membership fee revenue is recognized over the term of the membership, typically 12 months, with deferred membership fee revenue included in accrued liabilities; gift cards are not recognized as sales until the card is redeemed; and revenue from financial and other services is recognized when the service is performed.

Analysis of unearned revenue

  • If deferred revenue liabilities decrease, we infer that current reported revenue was collected from customers in a prior period — there have been fewer new prepayments for which revenue will be recognized in the future. Such a trend could predict future declines in revenue and profit.
  • Growth in deferred revenue could predict future increases in revenue and profit.
  • In either case, pay attention to the rate of increase or decrease as well as to the dollar amount of the change during the period.

LO 5-4 Foreign Currency Exchange Rates

Accounting for foreign currency effects

  • Companies routinely conduct business in foreign currencies. Foreign subsidiaries transact almost entirely in their home currencies and maintain their accounting records in those currencies.
  • Before the subsidiaries' financial statements are consolidated, they must be translated into $US.
  • As the $US strengthens against other world currencies, each unit of foreign currency buys less in $US terms. When a foreign subsidiary's income statement is translated, every income statement item shrinks — reported revenues and expenses are smaller than before the $US strengthened. The balance sheet shrinks as well, giving lower $US values for assets, liabilities, and equity.

Foreign currency effects at Pfizer

Pfizer's MD&A reported: "Revenues increased 23% in 2022 reflecting an operational increase of 30%, as well as an unfavorable impact of foreign exchange of 7%." We can infer that the $US dollar strengthened during the year.

Pfizer also discloses that 58% of its total 2022 revenues were derived from international operations, including 26% from Europe and 20% from Japan, China, and the rest of the Asia Pacific region.

Analysis of A/R — magnitude
Accounts receivable turnover = Sales ÷ Average accounts receivable
Days sales outstanding (DSO) = 365 days ÷ Accounts receivable turnover

DSO reveals the number of days, on average, that accounts receivable are outstanding before they are paid. DSO can be:

  • Compared with the company's established credit terms to investigate whether customers are conforming to those terms
  • Computed over several years for the same company to investigate trends
  • Compared with peer companies

Interpretation of A/R ratios. Evidence that A/R have grown more quickly than sales shows up as a lower A/R turnover ratio, a higher percentage of accounts receivable to sales, and a lengthening DSO. Generally such a trend is not favorable, for these possible reasons:

  • The company is becoming more lenient in granting credit to its customers
  • Credit quality is deteriorating
  • The mix of products sold has changed toward products or customer contracts with longer payment terms

Research and development expense

R&D costs broadly consist of: salaries and benefits for researchers and developers; supplies needed to conduct the research; licensing fees for intellectual property or software used in the R&D process; third-party payments to collaborators at other firms and universities; laboratory and other equipment; and property and buildings used as research facilities.

R&D costs are expensed as incurred, except for general-purpose PP&E assets, which are capitalized and depreciated as usual.

R&D intensity varies enormously by industry. Median S&P 500 R&D expense to revenue in 2022: chemicals 1.1%, machinery 1.2%, pharmaceuticals/biotechnology/life sciences 6.7%, technology hardware and equipment 8.5%, software and services 10.8%, semiconductors and semiconductor equipment 13.4%.

Provision (benefit) for taxes on income

  • Tax expense is the income taxes the company has paid and expects to pay to federal, state, municipal, and foreign tax authorities.
  • The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) made sweeping changes that affected taxes and income statements:
  • Reduced the corporate tax rate from 35% to 21%
  • Imposed tax on all future foreign earnings, even if the cash profits remain abroad
  • Reduced the repatriation tax on prior foreign earnings to 15.5% (from 35%)
  • Many companies, including Pfizer, experienced tax benefits (a negative tax expense) in 2017. Effects continued into 2018 as companies responded to the new rules by realigning operations and repatriating past profits.

Why segregate discontinued operations?

  • Discontinued operations are segregated because they represent a transitory item. Transitory items will not recur and are therefore largely irrelevant to predicting future performance.
  • Investors tend to focus on income from continuing operations, because that is the level of profitability likely to persist into the future.
  • To be classified as a discontinued operation, the disposal of the business unit must represent a strategic shift for the company and have a major effect on the company's financial results.
Manager Insight — which number to use
Several numbers could be used to analyze company performance; which one we use depends on the question we seek to answer.
To compare to the prior two years, and to calculate ROA, use "net income from continuing operations before allocation to noncontrolling interest" ($31,407 million in 2022). The appropriate number for ROE is net income attributable to Pfizer ($31,372 million in 2022). This measure reflects results measured on a comparable basis over the three years, and is accordingly also appropriate for forecasting.

LO 5-7 Pro Forma and Non-GAAP Disclosures

Pro forma income reporting

  • Pro forma income statements are non-GAAP numbers that company management believes provide a better measure of their financial performance.
  • The SEC requires that companies reconcile non-GAAP information to GAAP numbers (Regulation G).
  • Remember that a company's purpose in making a non-GAAP disclosure is to portray its financial performance the way management would like us to analyze it. Unscrupulous companies might attempt to present results in the best possible light (opportunism).

Pro forma disclosure at Pfizer. Pfizer's 2022 earnings release reported full-year revenues of $100.3 billion reflecting 30% operational growth (excluding Paxlovid and Comirnaty, revenues grew 2% operationally), and full-year reported diluted EPS of $5.47, up 42%, alongside adjusted diluted EPS of $6.58, up 62%.

  • Adjusted diluted EPS is not a GAAP metric.
  • Pfizer made a number of deductions and additions to its GAAP numbers because management believes doing so provides a better measure of financial performance.
  • Adjusted income statements are sometimes referred to as pro forma or non-GAAP numbers.

SEC warning on pro forma information

The SEC cautions public companies on the use of "pro forma" financial information and alerts investors to its potential dangers. Its key points:

  • In this context the term has no defined meaning and no uniform characteristics.
  • Pro forma information can serve useful purposes — focusing investors' attention on critical components of results, providing meaningful comparison to prior periods, or emphasizing the results of core operations. There is no prohibition on publishing interpretations or summaries of GAAP results.
  • Nonetheless, pro forma information can mislead investors if it obscures GAAP results. Because it departs from traditional accounting conventions, its use can make it hard to compare an issuer's information with other reporting periods and with other companies.
  • The SEC's advice: compare any summary or pro forma presentation with the results reported on GAAP-based financials by the same company. "Read before you invest; understand before you commit."

Disclosures and Market Assessments

For a thorough reading of the GAAP financials:

  • Read the external audit report and note any deviation from boilerplate language
  • Peruse notes on accounting policies and compare with industry peers
  • Examine changes in accounting policies
  • Compare key ratios over time
  • Review ratios of competitors and consider how macroeconomic conditions have shifted ratios over time
  • Identify nonrecurring items and separately assess their impact on company performance and position
  • Recast financial statements as necessary to reflect accounting policies more in line with competitors or that better reflect economically relevant numbers

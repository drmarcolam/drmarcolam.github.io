1. The profit formula and the contribution format

Laurel Ammons asked how safe the $4.25 million profit is. That starts with a statement that separates variable from fixed costs.

Key ideas
  • Profit = price × units − variable cost per unit × units − fixed costs.
  • The contribution format shows net sales − variable costs = contribution margin, then − fixed costs = operating income.
  • CVP assumes costs are fixed or variable, relationships are linear within the relevant range, and the sales mix is constant.
Worked example: Cullowhee

Cullowhee FY2026 ($ thousand):

$ thousand
Net sales55,200
Total variable costs(34,700)
Contribution margin20,500
Total fixed costs(16,250)
Operating income4,250

Contribution margin ratio = 20,500 ÷ 55,200 = 37.1%.

2. Break-even in units and in sales dollars

Break-even is the volume at which Cullowhee earns zero profit.

Key ideas
  • Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit.
  • Break-even sales dollars = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio (or break-even units × price).
Worked example: Cullowhee

Cullowhee overall and the Balsam E1 line (FY2026):

CalculationResult
Company break-even sales: 16,250 ÷ 0.371443,756
Balsam E1 break-even units: 5,400,000 ÷ 9605,625

The units are in the E1 line; the dollars are $ thousand for the company.

3. Target profit and margin of safety

Break-even is the floor. Managers plan for a target and want to know their cushion.

Key ideas
  • Target units = (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution margin per unit.
  • Margin of safety = actual (or planned) sales − break-even sales, in units, dollars or as a percent of sales.
Worked example: Cullowhee

Cullowhee FY2026:

CalculationResult
Margin of safety: (55,200 − 43,756) ÷ 55,20020.7%
Balsam E1 margin of safety in units: 10,000 − 5,6254,375
Units needed for a 6,000,000 E1 segment margin: (5,400,000 + 6,000,000) ÷ 96011,875

4. Operating leverage

Cullowhee's profit is a small slice of a large contribution margin, so a small change in sales moves profit a lot.

Key ideas
  • Degree of operating leverage = contribution margin ÷ operating income.
  • % change in operating income ≈ DOL × % change in sales.
  • High operating leverage means a high share of fixed costs: more profit on the way up, more risk on the way down.
Worked example: Cullowhee

Cullowhee FY2026:

CalculationResult
DOL: 20,500 ÷ 4,2504.82
Sales up 10%: operating income up about 4.82 × 10%48.2%
Sales down 10%: operating income down about48.2%

That is why a 10% drop in sales would remove nearly half of the profit.

5. Multi-product break-even and sales mix

Cullowhee sells four lines with different margins. The mix of sales changes the break-even point.

Key ideas
  • Weighted-average contribution margin per unit = sum of (unit CM × share of units).
  • Break-even total units = fixed costs ÷ weighted-average CM, split by the mix.
  • If the mix shifts toward a lower-margin product, the average falls and break-even rises.
Worked example: Cullowhee

Two products, 3 to 1 mix: A has CM $10, B has CM $50. Weighted average = (3 × 10 + 1 × 50) ÷ 4 = $20. With fixed costs of $25,000, break-even is 25,000 ÷ 20 = 1,250 units: 938 of A and 312 of B.

6. Target profit after tax

Owners care about profit after tax, but break-even formulas work with pre-tax profit.

Key ideas
  • Pre-tax profit = after-tax profit ÷ (1 − tax rate).
  • Put the pre-tax figure into the target profit formula: units = (fixed costs + pre-tax profit) ÷ CM per unit.
Worked example: Cullowhee

Tax rate 30%, after-tax profit wanted $600 thousand: pre-tax profit = 600 ÷ 0.70 = $857 thousand. Taxes = 257, after-tax = 600.

7. What-if: tariffs, prices, costs and mix

Laurel Ammons asked what a motor tariff would do. CVP turns the question into numbers.

Key ideas
  • A higher variable cost lowers contribution margin per unit and raises break-even.
  • Lower fixed costs lower break-even and raise operating income dollar for dollar.
  • A shift toward low-margin products lowers profit at the same total volume.
Worked example: Cullowhee

Balsam E1, 10% tariff on a $320 imported motor:

Result
Extra cost per E1$32
New CM per unit: 960 − 32$928
New break-even units: 5,400,000 ÷ 9285,819
E1 segment margin at 10,000 units$3,880 thousand

Break-even rises from 5,625 to 5,819 units, and the segment margin falls by 320 thousand.

Scenario. Summit Custom Frames, Inc. is a small frame builder in Asheville that supplies Summit Cycle Works. Naomi Okafor wants to see how ordinary transactions flow through the financial statements. Consider the effects of the following independent transactions on the company’s balance sheet, statement of cash flows, income statement and statement of stockholders’ equity.
  1. Owners invested cash in the company in exchange for shares of common stock.
  2. The company paid the monthly rent for its assembly space.
  3. The company purchased a welding robot for frame production and paid with cash.
  4. The company built a batch of custom frames and paid cash for materials and labor.
  5. The company paid cash dividends to the owners.
  6. The company sold the batch of frames from transaction d to a customer for cash, at a price above their cost.
  7. The company received cash from the bank for a loan.
How to answer. For every cell choose + if the account increases, − if it decreases, and none if the transaction does not affect that line. Each transaction is independent of the others except where one explicitly refers to another. Every cell counts, and a blank cell is not complete, so answer none deliberately rather than leaving it empty.
Required. For each transaction, indicate the effect on every line of the four statements. Work down one transaction at a time. The balance sheet must still balance, and any change in retained earnings on the balance sheet must appear again on the statement of stockholders’ equity.

Explanation. (a) Stock issued: cash and contributed capital up; a financing inflow. (b) Rent is an expense: cash, retained earnings and net income down; an operating outflow. (c) Equipment is an asset, so cash falls and noncash assets rise; an investing outflow, no expense. (d) Cash spent on inventory still owned buys an asset, so nothing reaches the income statement; an operating outflow. (e) Dividends reduce cash and retained earnings but are not an expense; a financing outflow. (f) The sale brings in cash and revenue, and the cost of the frames leaves inventory as an expense; the profit raises net income and retained earnings, and cash is an operating inflow. (g) The loan raises cash and liabilities; a financing inflow.

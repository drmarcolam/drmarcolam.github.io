Scenario. Summit Trail Builders, Inc., a Summit Cycle Works affiliate, contracted with Buncombe County to build a mountain-bike park at a contract price of $240 million. The schedule of expected (which equals actual) cash collections and contract costs follows.

How to answer. Do not round intermediate calculations. Enter dollar amounts in millions to two decimal places (e.g., 44.00) and percentage of completion as a percentage to two decimal places (e.g., 20.00). Percentage of completion is cumulative (costs to date ÷ total expected costs); revenue, expense and net income are the amounts for that year alone. Revenue to date = percentage complete × contract price; revenue for the year = revenue to date − revenue recognized in prior years; expense = cost incurred that year. Cash collections play no part in the calculation.
Required. (a) Calculate revenue, expense and net income for each year and for all three years combined using the cost-to-cost method. (b) Assess whether the method measures performance well on this contract.

(b) Does the method measure performance well?

Choose the statement that best assesses the cost-to-cost method for this contract.

  1. No. Revenue should be recognized only as cash is collected, since the collections schedule is the one objectively verifiable measure of what the customer accepted.
  2. No. The method recognizes the entire profit as soon as the contract is signed, which overstates income in the earliest year.
  3. Yes. Revenue is recognized as the work is performed, with costs incurred as the measure of progress, so income follows the effort expended rather than the timing of cash collections. Its weakness is its dependence on the estimate of total cost: if the estimate is wrong, the pattern of reported income is wrong with it.
  4. Yes. The method assigns an equal share of the profit to each year regardless of activity, which smooths income and makes the years comparable.

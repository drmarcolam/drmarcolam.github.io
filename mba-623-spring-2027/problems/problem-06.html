Scenario. Elena Park started Summit Mobile Tune-Up, LLC, a mobile bike-repair business serving the Asheville area and a start-up partner of Summit Cycle Works, on May 1. The company created these accounts for May: Cash; Accounts receivable; Supplies; Prepaid van lease; Equipment; Notes payable; Accounts payable; Common stock; Dividends; Service revenue; Wages expense; Advertising expense; and Fuel expense. Naomi Okafor, Summit’s CFO, wants to see how the first month looks in the financial statement effects template.
How to answer. Enter whole dollars with no cents. Enter decreases as negative numbers, either −2,850 or (2,850). Enter an expense as a positive amount in the Expenses column; net income is revenue minus expenses. On the second line of each transaction, name the account behind each amount you entered. For revenue and expense events, name the same income statement account in Earned capital. Leave a cell empty if the transaction does not affect it: empty cells are not scored, but anything typed into a cell that should be empty is marked wrong. Cash and net income need no account title.
Required. Record each May transaction in the template. Work across one transaction before starting the next, and check that the change in cash plus the change in noncash assets equals the change in liabilities plus contributed capital plus earned capital.

Explanation. Every transaction keeps the accounting equation in balance. Cash flows change only the asset side (or move an asset against a liability or capital). Revenue and expenses each reach the balance sheet through earned capital, which is why each amount in the Revenue or Expenses column has a matching amount in Earned capital. Dividends reduce earned capital but are not an expense, so they never reach net income. The net income for May is $7,800 − $640 − $3,960 − $1,085 = $2,115.

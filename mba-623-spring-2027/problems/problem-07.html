How to answer. Enter whole dollars with no cents. Enter decreases as negative numbers, either −135 or (135). Enter an expense as a positive amount in the Expenses column. Required. Record each adjustment needed on January 31 in the template. Every one was prompted by the calendar, not by a payment or a receipt. If you find yourself entering an amount in the cash column, look again.

Information for the adjustments

  1. Prepaid insurance represents a 2-year premium paid on January 1.
  2. Supplies of $790 were still on hand on January 31.
  3. Equipment is expected to last 10 years (120 months), with no salvage value.
  4. On January 1 the park collected $14,400 for 12 months of memberships paid in advance, which earn $1,200 per month.
  5. Salaries of $520 have been earned by employees but not yet recorded as of January 31.

The account list, in full: Accounts receivable · Accumulated depreciation · Cash · Depreciation expense · Equipment · Insurance expense · Membership revenue · Prepaid insurance · Salaries expense · Salaries payable · Supplies · Supplies expense · Unearned membership revenue.

Explanation. (a) $5,400 ÷ 24 months = $225 of insurance used up in January. (b) $1,860 − $790 on hand = $1,070 of supplies used. (c) $18,000 ÷ 120 months = $150 of depreciation, recorded against accumulated depreciation, which reduces the net equipment balance. (d) One month of the advance collection is now earned: $14,400 ÷ 12 = $1,200 moves from unearned revenue (a liability) to revenue. (e) Employees have earned $520 that the park owes but has not paid, so a liability and an expense are recognized. No adjustment touches cash. Total effect on net income: +1,200 − 225 − 1,070 − 150 − 520 = −$765.

Write one or two sentences: do the business models (the mix of products, pricing and how each company serves customers) help explain the differences?

Suggested discussion. Net income margin reflects pricing power and cost structure. The home-improvement chain earns the highest margin on each sales dollar. Discount and supercenter formats sell lower-margin, high-volume goods, so a small margin on a very large sales base is normal. The department store, with branded merchandise and higher prices, sits in between.

Does the ranking match part (a)? Name at least one reason the two rankings can differ (think about noncash expenses such as depreciation, and changes in inventory and payables).

Suggested discussion. Operating cash flow exceeds net income for every company because depreciation and other noncash expenses are added back, and the gap is largest for capital-intensive chains with many stores and distribution centers. Differences from the part (a) ranking also come from working capital: a retailer that stretches payables to suppliers, or turns inventory quickly, converts more of its profit into cash. Here the discount chain moves up (third in part a, second here) and the department store moves down. The supercenter still ranks last, but it turns each dollar of profit into several dollars of operating cash, because depreciation on its huge store base is large relative to its thin income.

(c) Investing cash flow as a percentage of sales

CompanyInvesting cash flow ÷ sales (%)Rank (1 = highest)

Investing cash flow is usually negative for a growing retailer (cash goes out for stores, equipment and acquisitions). A positive number means the company took in more cash from selling assets or investments than it spent. Does this ranking match part (a)? Suggest a reason.

Suggested discussion. Investing outflows measure how aggressively a company is building stores, remodeling and buying equipment relative to its sales. High-margin companies do not automatically invest the most. The discount and supercenter chains, expanding quickly, spend the most relative to sales. The electronics chain is the only one with positive investing cash flow: it sold more investments or assets than it bought, which is typical of a company shrinking its store base or liquidating securities.

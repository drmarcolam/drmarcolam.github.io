Reading the Financial Statements: Chapter Summary

A quick review of the key ideas, with Cullowhee examples · Not graded · Print or save as PDF if you like

Use this as a refresher before the Gate Quiz or while you work through the walkthrough. Nothing here is submitted. Textbook: Chapter 2. The short audio overview and review handout for this module are in the Canvas module.

Formulas to know

  • Ending retained earnings = beginning + net income − dividends
  • Net change in cash = operating + investing + financing
  • Ending cash = beginning cash + net change in cash
  • Common-size % = line item ÷ net sales (income statement) or total assets (balance sheet)
  • Growth rate = (this year − last year) ÷ last year
  • Operating cash flow (indirect) = net income + noncash expenses ± changes in working capital

1. How the four statements link

Nora Crisp wants you fluent in how the statements fit together before you analyze them.

Key ideas
  • Net income increases retained earnings (less dividends). That flows to stockholders’ equity on the balance sheet.
  • Ending cash on the statement of cash flows must equal cash on the balance sheet.
  • The balance sheet must balance: assets = liabilities + equity.
  • Every item has a primary home: revenues and expenses on the income statement; assets, liabilities and equity balances on the balance sheet; cash flows by activity on the cash flow statement; changes in equity accounts on the statement of stockholders’ equity.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee FY2026 ($ thousand):

  • Retained earnings: 10,979 + 2,679 − 0 dividends = 13,658.
  • Cash: 4,989 + 8,003 net increase = 12,992 on both the cash flow statement and balance sheet.
  • Total assets 58,282 = liabilities 26,414 + equity 31,868.

2. Retained earnings and equity rollforwards

A rollforward explains how a balance moved from last year to this year. It is also a strong way to check your work.

Key ideas
  • Ending retained earnings = beginning + net income − dividends.
  • Ending total equity = beginning + net income + shares issued + stock-based compensation − dividends − share repurchases.
  • Selling shares increases contributed capital (common stock and paid-in capital), not retained earnings.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee FY2026 equity ($ thousand): 19,849 + 2,679 net income + 9,000 share sale + 340 stock-based compensation = 31,868. There were no dividends and no repurchases in FY2026 (the repurchase was in FY2025).

3. Operating cash flow: the indirect method

Nora asked why operating cash flow is higher than net income. The indirect method answers it line by line.

Key ideas
  • Start with net income.
  • Add back noncash expenses: depreciation, amortization, stock-based compensation.
  • Subtract increases in operating assets (receivables, inventories, prepaid expenses). A decrease is added.
  • Add increases in operating liabilities (payables, accrued liabilities, warranty, deferred revenue). A decrease is subtracted.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee FY2026 ($ thousand):

$ thousand
Net income2,679
Depreciation, amortization, stock compensation and bond discount3,315
Net change in working capital(1,430)
Cash provided by operating activities4,564

Working capital used 1,430: receivables (1,380), inventories (2,300) and prepaid expenses (150) were uses; payables 900, accrued liabilities 500, warranty 400 and deferred revenue 600 were sources.

4. Why operating cash flow differs from net income

Net income and cash from operations measure different things. The gap is a clue about the quality of earnings.

Key ideas
  • Accrual accounting recognizes revenue when earned and expense when incurred, whatever the timing of cash.
  • Noncash expenses make cash from operations higher than net income.
  • Growth in receivables and inventories makes cash from operations lower: the sale is recorded, but the cash has not arrived.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee FY2026: net income 2,679 and operating cash 4,564. The noncash add-backs (3,315) outweighed the cash tied up in working capital (1,430). Inventory growth (2,300) was the largest use. At the same time sales grew 18.7%, but net receivables grew 29.6% and inventories grew 31.5%: cash is being tied up faster than sales are growing.

5. Net change in cash

The three sections of the cash flow statement must add up to the change in cash.

Key ideas
  • Net change in cash = operating + investing + financing.
  • Ending cash = beginning cash + net change.
  • Investing outflows are negative; financing inflows from borrowing and share sales are positive.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee FY2026 ($ thousand): 4,564 − 12,850 + 16,289 = 8,003 increase. Ending cash = 4,989 + 8,003 = 12,992.

6. Common-size and growth comparisons

Ridgeline Bikes has about 1.5 times Cullowhee’s sales. Dollars mislead; percentages compare.

Key ideas
  • Common-size: express each line as a percent of net sales (income statement) or total assets (balance sheet).
  • Growth rate = (this year − last year) ÷ last year.
  • Compare companies of different size with percentages, and compare them over time with growth rates.

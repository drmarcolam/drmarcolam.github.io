Formulas to know

  • Accounting equation: assets = liabilities + stockholders’ equity
  • ROA = net income ÷ average total assets
  • ROE = net income ÷ average stockholders’ equity
  • Profit margin = net income ÷ net sales
  • Asset turnover = net sales ÷ average total assets
  • ROA = profit margin × asset turnover

1. Operating, investing and financing activities

Laurel Ammons needs to explain how Cullowhee paid for its growth. That starts with sorting every cash flow into three groups.

Key ideas
  • Operating: the daily work of selling bikes and paying for parts, wages and other costs.
  • Investing: buying or selling long-lived assets (equipment, an assembly line) and other businesses.
  • Financing: money from or to lenders and owners: borrowing, repaying debt, selling shares, repurchasing shares and paying dividends.
  • The statement of cash flows has one section for each, and the three sections add up to the change in cash.

2. The four statements, their users, and disclosure

Different people read Cullowhee’s statements for different reasons. A good analyst matches the statement and the question to the reader.

Key ideas
  • Income statement: performance for a period. Balance sheet: what the company owns and owes at a date. Statement of stockholders’ equity: changes in the owners’ interest. Statement of cash flows: where cash came from and went.
  • Lenders and suppliers ask: can the company pay? Investors and boards ask: what return does it earn, and how fast can it grow?
  • Disclosure has benefits (a lower cost of capital, better supplier and recruiting relationships) and costs (preparing and auditing the statements, competitive disadvantage, litigation and political cost).
Cullowhee example

Cullasaja Mountain Bank is deciding whether to renew the revolver. It reads the cash flow statement and the balance sheet for evidence that Cullowhee can pay interest and principal. Cowee Growth Partners reads ROA and ROE to judge the return on its $9.0 million. The same company, two readers, two sets of questions.

3. The accounting equation and account types

Everything in the statements rests on one equation. If it does not balance, something is wrong.

Key ideas
  • Assets = liabilities + stockholders’ equity. Every event keeps the equation in balance.
  • Assets are resources the company controls: cash, receivables, inventories, prepaid expenses, equipment, goodwill.
  • Liabilities are amounts owed to non-owners: payables, accrued liabilities, warranty and deferred revenue, debt.
  • Equity is the owners’ claim: common stock, paid-in capital and retained earnings.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee at December 31, 2026 ($ thousand):

$ thousand
Total assets58,282
Total liabilities26,414
Total stockholders’ equity31,868
Liabilities + equity58,282

If you know any two, you can find the third: equity = 58,282 − 26,414 = 31,868.

4. Return on assets (ROA)

Cowee Growth Partners wants to know what its money earned. ROA starts with what the whole company earned on all the money invested in it.

Key ideas
  • ROA = net income ÷ average total assets.
  • Use the average of beginning and ending assets, because assets change during the year.
  • ROA measures the return on all financing, from lenders and owners together.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee, FY2026 ($ thousand):

  • Average total assets = (36,549 + 58,282) ÷ 2 = 47,415.5
  • ROA = 2,679 ÷ 47,415.5 = 5.65%

For comparison, FY2025 ROA was about 8.7%, so it fell sharply as assets grew faster than income.

5. Return on equity and financial leverage

The owners care about the return on their investment, which is a different number from ROA.

Key ideas
  • ROE = net income ÷ average stockholders’ equity.
  • ROE is higher than ROA when liabilities finance part of the assets. This is financial leverage: the owners earn on a smaller base.
  • New shares enlarge equity. Unless the new money raises income in proportion, ROE falls.
Cullowhee example

Cullowhee, FY2026 ($ thousand):

  • Average equity = (19,849 + 31,868) ÷ 2 = 25,858.5
  • ROE = 2,679 ÷ 25,858.5 = 10.36%, compared with ROA of 5.65%.
  • FY2025 ROE was about 16.0%. The $9.0 million share sale in April 2026 enlarged equity while income did not grow, so ROE fell.

6. Profit margin, asset turnover and strategy

ROA can be split into two questions: how much profit does each sale earn, and how hard do the assets work?

Key ideas
  • Profit margin = net income ÷ net sales. Asset turnover = net sales ÷ average total assets.
  • ROA = profit margin × asset turnover.
  • A premium, low-volume business earns a high margin with low turnover. A value, high-volume business earns a thin margin and needs high turnover to earn a comparable ROA.

Why this matters

Exam 2 asks you to use Modules 14 to 19 together, the way a manager does. Each Block Check showed you one block at a time. The exam asks you to connect them: how cost behavior, break-even, relevant costs and activity-based costing change what Cullowhee should do. A few focused hours on your weakest topics will help more than rereading everything.

Step 3. Try a mixed practice case

Cullowhee is looking at its accessories line. Use the facts below for every question. You can check each answer as you go, and the worked answer opens after you check.

Facts for the accessories line.
  • The selling price is 400 per unit and the variable cost is 240 per unit.
  • Fixed costs of the line are 480,000 a year. Expected sales are 4,000 units.
  • Two years ago Cullowhee paid 90,000 for special tooling that cannot be sold or used for anything else.
  • Full cost per unit at 4,000 units is 360 (variable cost 240 plus fixed cost 120).
Question 7 (multiple choice)

Two years ago Cullowhee paid 90,000 for special tooling that cannot be sold or used for anything else. Is the 90,000 relevant to this year’s make-or-buy decision?

Question 8 (explain your reasoning)

A manager says, “We should reject the retailer’s offer of 300, because it is below our full cost of 360 per unit.” Using your answers, what does the 360 leave out, and what would you tell her? Write 3 to 5 sentences.

See what a strong answer includes

A strong answer notes that the 360 includes fixed cost per unit (480,000 ÷ 4,000 = 120) that does not change if the order is accepted with idle capacity, so only the 240 variable cost is relevant and the order adds 60 per unit (30,000 in total). It also asks whether the plant really has idle capacity (otherwise the lost sales are an opportunity cost), whether regular customers will learn of the lower price, and whether the order is one-time. It should end with a specific question or next step.

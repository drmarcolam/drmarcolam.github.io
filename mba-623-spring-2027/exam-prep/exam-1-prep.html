Exam 1 Preparation: Cullowhee’s Financial Story

Financial accounting, Modules 1–8 · Cullowhee Cycle Works · Not graded

What this page is. It is a short, guided way to get ready, and nothing here is scored or sent to your instructor. You will (1) check where you stand in each module, (2) pick what to work on and when, (3) try one practice case that mixes several modules, and (4) save your plan as a PDF for yourself. Nothing you type is stored on this site, so save the PDF before you leave.

Why this matters

Exam 1 asks you to use the first eight modules together, the way a manager does. Each Block Check showed you one block at a time. The exam asks you to connect them: how a sale, a loan or a dividend flows through the statements and what it tells you about Cullowhee. A few focused hours on your weakest topics will help more than rereading everything.

What to expect. Exam 1 opens Tue Mar 16 and closes Sun Mar 21 at 11:59 PM ET. It covers Modules 1–8 and uses Cullowhee. It has two parts, and you can take them in either order as long as both are finished by the due date: a case like the Block Checks, with an “Explain your reasoning” part, and a proctored quiz in Canvas with numbers that differ for each student. The rules for AI and other help differ by part, and the instructions on each part tell you exactly what is allowed. Using AI to study before the exam, including this page and the tutors, is fine.

Step 1. Where do I stand?

Rate each skill honestly. Choose Need work if you could not do it without help, Getting there if you can do it with a reference or you make slips, and Solid if you could do it now. Your Gate Quiz scores can help you decide.

Module 1: Funding the Climb

I can classify a business event as an operating, investing or financing activity.
I can explain how the four financial statements link across time.

Module 2: Reading the Financial Statements

I can read a balance sheet and sort items into assets, liabilities and equity.
I can compute net working capital and the cash conversion cycle.

Module 3: Recording Business Events with the FSET

I can show a transaction’s effects on the statements with the FSET.
I can make the four types of accounting adjustments (prepaid, unearned, accrued expenses, accrued revenues).

Module 4: Sales Up, Returns Down

I can compute ROE and split it with the DuPont disaggregation (ROA and financial leverage).
I can break ROA into profitability and productivity ratios and say what changed.

Module 5: Revenues, Receivables and Operating Expenses

I can apply the five-step revenue recognition rule and account for sales allowances and unearned revenue.
I can estimate bad debts and explain the allowance for uncollectible accounts.

Module 6: Inventories, Payables and Long-Term Assets

I can compute cost of goods sold and ending inventory under FIFO, LIFO and average cost.
I can compute depreciation and explain how an asset sale or impairment changes the statements.

Module 7: Borrowing to Build

I can estimate and record a warranty liability and roll it forward.
I can record accrued interest and explain how a bond is priced and reported.

Module 8: Pay a Dividend or Split the Stock?

I can record stock issuances, repurchases, cash dividends and stock splits.
I can compute earnings per share and book value per share.

Step 3. Try a mixed practice case

Cullowhee had a busy year. Amounts are in $000. Use the facts below for every question. You can check each answer as you go, and the worked answer opens after you check.

Facts for the year.
  • Sales on account were 4,800 and cost of goods sold was 2,880. Beginning inventory was 600 and purchases were 3,000.
  • Management estimates bad debts at 2% of credit sales.
  • Warranty expense is 3% of sales. The warranty liability began at 40, and repairs paid during the year were 70.
  • On October 1 Cullowhee borrowed 2,000 at 6% a year. No interest has been paid.
  • Other operating expenses were 900. The board declared and paid a cash dividend of 150.
Question 7 (multiple choice)

The board declared and paid a cash dividend of 150. What does it do to this year’s income before income tax?

Question 8 (explain your reasoning)

The CEO says, “The loan only cost us 30 in interest this year, so borrowing is cheap.” Using your answers, what does the 30 leave out, and what would you tell her? Write 3 to 5 sentences.

See what a strong answer includes

A strong answer notes that the loan was outstanding only 3 months (October 1 to December 31), so 30 is a quarter of a year. A full year at 6% on 2,000 is 120, four times as much. It also notes that interest is only part of the cost of borrowing (principal must be repaid, and the cash is committed), and compares the cost with what the money earns. It should end with a specific question or next step, for example how much the money earns, or when the principal is due.

Contribution margin

Contribution Margin and CM Ratio

Blue Ridge Bike Works in Asheville sells a trail bike for $250. Variable cost is $150 per bike (parts, assembly labor, sales commission). Fixed costs are $150,000 per year.

  • Contribution margin (CM) per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit
  • CM ratio = CM per unit ÷ Selling price
Each unit sold contributes its CM first toward covering fixed costs, and after that toward profit.
Example 1 — CM per Unit

What is the contribution margin per bike?

$
Solution

$250 − $150 = $100 per bike.

CM per bike = $100

Example 2 — CM Ratio

What is the CM ratio? Enter it as a percent (for example, 25 for 25%).

Need a hint?
CM per unit ÷ selling price, then multiply by 100.
%
Solution

$100 ÷ $250 = 0.40, or 40%.

CM ratio = 40%

Every sales dollar produces 40 cents of contribution margin. The CM ratio is handy when you only know sales dollars.
Break-even

Break-Even Analysis

  • Break-even in units = Fixed expenses ÷ CM per unit
  • Break-even in sales dollars = Fixed expenses ÷ CM ratio
At break-even, net operating income is zero: total contribution margin equals fixed expenses.
Example 1 — Break-Even Units

How many bikes must Blue Ridge sell to break even?

Need a hint?
Fixed expenses ÷ CM per unit.
bikes
Solution

$150,000 ÷ $100 = 1,500 bikes.

Break-even = 1,500 bikes

Example 2 — Break-Even Sales Dollars

What is break-even in sales dollars?

Need a hint?
You can use the CM ratio, or multiply break-even units by the selling price.
$
Solution

$150,000 ÷ 0.40 = $375,000.
Check: 1,500 bikes × $250 = $375,000.

Break-even sales = $375,000

Example 3 — Direction of Change

For each change (considered alone), will break-even units go up or down?

Solution

Break-even = fixed costs ÷ CM per unit. Higher fixed costs raise the numerator, so break-even rises. A lower variable cost raises CM per unit, so break-even falls. A lower selling price cuts CM per unit, so break-even rises.

Example 4 — A Price Cut

Suppose Blue Ridge cuts its price to $230 with variable cost still $150. How many bikes are needed to break even now?

Need a hint?
New CM per unit first.
bikes
Solution

New CM = $230 − $150 = $80. $150,000 ÷ $80 = 1,875 bikes.

New break-even = 1,875 bikes

Target profit

Target Profit Analysis

Required units = (Fixed expenses + Target profit) ÷ CM per unit. Required sales dollars = (Fixed expenses + Target profit) ÷ CM ratio.
Example 1 — Units for a Target Profit

Blue Ridge wants a net operating income of $80,000. How many bikes must it sell?

Need a hint?
Add the target profit to fixed expenses before dividing.
bikes
Solution

($150,000 + $80,000) ÷ $100 = $230,000 ÷ $100 = 2,300 bikes.

Required sales = 2,300 bikes

Example 2 — Dollar Sales for a Target Profit

Using the CM ratio, what sales dollars are required for the same target profit?

$
Solution

$230,000 ÷ 0.40 = $575,000.
Check: 2,300 × $250 = $575,000.

Required sales = $575,000

Margin of safety & leverage

Margin of Safety and Operating Leverage

Suppose Blue Ridge actually sells 2,500 bikes ($625,000 in sales), so net operating income is 2,500 × $100 − $150,000 = $100,000.

  • Margin of safety (dollars) = Total sales − Break-even sales. As a percentage = Margin of safety ÷ Total sales.
  • Degree of operating leverage = Contribution margin ÷ Net operating income. A given % change in sales changes net operating income by that % × the degree of operating leverage.
Example 1 — Margin of Safety (Dollars)

What is the margin of safety in dollars?

$
Solution

$625,000 − $375,000 = $250,000.

Margin of safety = $250,000

Example 2 — Margin of Safety (Percent)

Express the margin of safety as a percent of sales.

%
Solution

$250,000 ÷ $625,000 = 40%. Sales could fall 40% before Blue Ridge starts losing money.

Margin of safety = 40%

Example 3 — Degree of Operating Leverage

Total contribution margin is 2,500 × $100 = $250,000. What is the degree of operating leverage?

Need a hint?
Contribution margin ÷ net operating income.
Solution

$250,000 ÷ $100,000 = 2.5.

Degree of operating leverage = 2.5

Example 4 — Predicting the Effect of a Sales Increase

If sales rise 10% (and fixed costs do not change), what will net operating income be? Use the degree of operating leverage.

Need a hint?
% change in net operating income = degree of operating leverage × % change in sales.
$
Solution

NOI increases by 2.5 × 10% = 25%. $100,000 × 1.25 = $125,000.
Check: 2,750 bikes × $100 = $275,000 − $150,000 = $125,000.

Net operating income = $125,000

Because fixed costs stay the same, a 10% sales increase produced a 25% profit increase. Operating leverage works in reverse too: a 10% sales drop would cut profit by 25%.
Sales mix

Break-Even with More Than One Product

Blue Ridge adds a second model. Fixed costs for the company are now $230,000. Sales are expected to be 75% Trail bikes and 25% Summit bikes (by units).

Product data
PriceVariable costCM per unit
Trail$250$150$100
Summit$400$240$160
With a constant sales mix, compute the weighted-average CM per unit, then divide fixed costs by it to get total break-even units. Multiply by each product's share to get its break-even units.
Example 1 — Average CM per Unit

What is the weighted-average contribution margin per bike?

Need a hint?
Weight each product's CM by its share of units sold.
$
Solution

(75% × $100) + (25% × $160) = $75 + $40 = $115.

Average CM = $115 per bike

Example 2 — Break-Even Units

How many bikes in total (both models) must be sold to break even?

bikes
Solution

$230,000 ÷ $115 = 2,000 bikes.
Trail: 75% × 2,000 = 1,500. Summit: 25% × 2,000 = 500.

Total break-even = 2,000 bikes

Example 3 — Break-Even Sales Dollars

At that mix, average revenue per bike is (75% × $250) + (25% × $400) = $287.50. What is break-even in sales dollars?

$
Solution

2,000 bikes × $287.50 = $575,000.
Check with the CM ratio: $115 ÷ $287.50 = 40%, and $230,000 ÷ 0.40 = $575,000.

Break-even sales = $575,000

The break-even point changes if the mix changes. Shifting toward the product with the lower CM ratio raises the break-even point.
Wrap-up

Chapter 2 at a Glance

  • CM per unit = price − variable cost; CM ratio = CM ÷ price.
  • Break-even = fixed costs ÷ CM (per unit or ratio). Target profit: add the profit to fixed costs first.
  • Margin of safety = sales − break-even sales. Operating leverage = CM ÷ NOI.
  • Sales mix: use the weighted-average CM, and assume the mix stays constant.

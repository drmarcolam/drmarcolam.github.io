Two costing methods

Absorption vs. Variable Costing: Unit Product Cost

Pisgah Pottery in Asheville makes ceramic dinnerware. Year 1 data: selling price $40; direct materials $8, direct labor $4, variable manufacturing overhead $2 per unit; variable selling expense $3 per unit sold; fixed manufacturing overhead $120,000; fixed selling and administrative $90,000. The company produced 12,000 units and sold 10,000.

  • Absorption costing: unit cost includes direct materials, direct labor, variable overhead and fixed manufacturing overhead.
  • Variable costing: unit cost includes only the variable manufacturing costs. Fixed manufacturing overhead is a period expense.
Selling and administrative costs are period costs under both methods.
Example 1 — Variable Costing Unit Cost

What is the unit product cost under variable costing?

$
Solution

$8 + $4 + $2 = $14.

Variable costing unit cost = $14

Example 2 — Absorption Costing Unit Cost

What is the unit product cost under absorption costing? Fixed overhead is spread over the 12,000 units produced.

Need a hint?
Fixed overhead per unit = fixed overhead ÷ units produced.
$
Solution

Fixed overhead per unit: $120,000 ÷ 12,000 = $10.
Unit cost: $14 + $10 = $24.

Absorption unit cost = $24

Example 3 — Reconciling the Difference

The two methods differ by $20,000. Compute the fixed manufacturing overhead deferred in inventory: units added to inventory × fixed overhead per unit.

$
Solution

(12,000 − 10,000) × $10 = 2,000 × $10 = $20,000.

Fixed overhead deferred = $20,000

Absorption costing carries $20,000 of fixed overhead into ending inventory instead of expensing it, so its income is higher. When production exceeds sales, absorption income is higher than variable costing income.
Example 4 — Which Is Higher?

Complete each statement.

Solution

Production > sales: fixed overhead is deferred in inventory under absorption costing (absorption income higher). Sales > production: prior-period deferred overhead is released to expense under absorption costing (variable income higher). Equal: no change in inventory, so no difference.

In Year 2 Pisgah produced 10,000 units and sold 12,000, using up the 2,000 units in beginning inventory (costed at $24 each under absorption costing). Prices and costs are unchanged. Fixed overhead per unit produced is now $12, so absorption unit cost for Year 2 production is $26.

Segment reporting

Segment Reporting

Pisgah also reports by sales channel. A segment margin shows how much a segment contributes toward common costs.

  • Contribution margin = Sales − Variable expenses
  • Segment margin = Contribution margin − Traceable fixed expenses
  • Company net operating income = Total segment margins − Common fixed expenses
A traceable fixed cost would disappear if the segment were eliminated. A common fixed cost would continue.

Example 1 — Classify the Fixed Cost

Is each fixed cost traceable or common?

Solution

Costs that exist because of a specific segment (its manager, its warehouse) are traceable. Costs that support the company as a whole (the president) are common and are not allocated to segments.

Example 2 — Retail Segment Margin

What is the segment margin for Retail?

$
Solution

CM: $300,000 − $120,000 = $180,000. Segment margin: $180,000 − $70,000 = $110,000.

Retail segment margin = $110,000

$

Wrap-up

Chapter 4 at a Glance

  • Variable costing treats fixed manufacturing overhead as a period cost; absorption treats it as a product cost.
  • Absorption income − variable income = change in inventory (units) × fixed overhead per unit.
  • Segment margin = contribution margin − traceable fixed expenses. Common costs are not allocated.

Sales budget

Where the Master Budget Starts: The Sales Budget

Hiwassee Hammocks in Hayesville, North Carolina, sells a camping hammock for $30. Budgeted unit sales by quarter are: Q1 10,000, Q2 12,000, Q3 14,000, Q4 16,000. Customers pay 70% in the quarter of sale and 30% in the following quarter.

The master budget is a set of linked budgets. Everything starts with the sales budget, because production, purchasing, staffing and cash flows all depend on expected sales.
Example 1 — Where Does It Begin?

Which budget is the starting point of the master budget?

Solution

Sales volume drives everything else: how much to produce, how many materials and labor-hours are needed, and when cash arrives. The cash budget comes last because it pulls together the other budgets.

Example 2 — Q1 Budgeted Sales

What are budgeted sales revenues for Q1?

$
Solution

10,000 × $30 = $300,000.

Q1 sales = $300,000

Production budget

The Production Budget

Hiwassee wants ending inventory equal to 20% of the next quarter's unit sales. Beginning inventory for Q1 is 2,000 units.

Required production = Budgeted unit sales + Desired ending inventory − Beginning inventory

How many hammocks must be produced in Q2? Q2's beginning inventory equals Q1's ending inventory.

Direct materials & labor

Direct Materials, Direct Labor and Overhead Budgets

Each hammock needs 3 yards of fabric at $2.00 per yard and 0.5 direct labor-hours at $18 per hour. Desired ending fabric inventory is 10% of the next quarter's production needs, and beginning inventory is 10% of Q1's production needs. Variable manufacturing overhead is $3 per direct labor-hour; fixed manufacturing overhead is $40,000 per quarter, including $10,000 of depreciation (not a cash cost).

What is the budgeted cash manufacturing overhead for Q1 (exclude depreciation)?

Need a hint?
Variable overhead plus fixed overhead other than depreciation.
$
Solution

Depreciation is a noncash expense, so it belongs in the overhead budget for costing but not in the cash budget.
Cash budget

Cash Collections and the Cash Budget

Accounts receivable at the start of Q1 total $60,000 and will be collected in Q1.

Example 1 — Q1 Cash Collections

What are budgeted cash collections in Q1?

Need a hint?
70% of Q1 sales plus the beginning receivables.
$
Solution

70% × $300,000 = $210,000. Plus beginning A/R $60,000. Total = $270,000.

Q1 collections = $270,000

Example 2 — Q2 Cash Collections

Q2 sales revenue is $360,000. What are budgeted cash collections in Q2?

Need a hint?
70% of Q2 sales plus 30% of Q1 sales.
$
Solution

70% × $360,000 = $252,000. 30% × $300,000 = $90,000. Total = $342,000.

Q2 collections = $342,000

Now build Q1's cash budget. Beginning cash is $25,000. Cash disbursements: fabric purchases (paid in the quarter), direct labor, cash manufacturing overhead, selling and administrative expenses of $80,000, and an equipment purchase of $40,000. The company wants at least $20,000 of ending cash and borrows in multiples of $1,000.

$

Ending cash = $20,200

The cash budget is where management learns whether it will need to borrow, and how much, long before the shortage actually occurs.
Wrap-up

Chapter 8 at a Glance

  • The sales budget drives the master budget.
  • Production = sales + desired ending inventory − beginning inventory. Purchases follow the same logic for materials.
  • The cash budget uses only cash items: collections, cash payments and financing. Noncash expenses such as depreciation are excluded.

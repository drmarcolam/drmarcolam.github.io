Balanced scorecard

The Balanced Scorecard

Brevard Brewing in Brevard, North Carolina, is building a balanced scorecard. A balanced scorecard translates a company's strategy into performance measures in four categories:

  • Learning and growth: employee capabilities and motivation (the foundation).
  • Internal business processes: how well operations run: quality, speed, cost.
  • Customer: how customers see the company: satisfaction, retention, referrals.
  • Financial: the results: profit, return, growth.
Example 1 — Classify the Measure

Choose the scorecard category for each measure.

Solution

Training hours build employee capability (learning and growth). Defect-free output and delivery cycle time describe how well internal processes perform. Defection rate and referrals describe customer behavior. Return on assets is a financial result.

Example 2 — Cause and Effect

A scorecard links measures with if-then hypotheses. Which category sits at the bottom of the scorecard, driving improvements in the categories above it?

Solution

The typical chain reads upward: if employees receive more training (learning and growth), then defects fall (internal processes); if defects fall, then customer satisfaction rises (customer); if satisfaction rises, then sales and profit grow (financial). If a hypothesis is false, management learns the strategy isn't working.

Cost of quality

The Cost of Quality

  • Prevention costs: keep defects from occurring (training, quality engineering).
  • Appraisal costs: find defects (inspection, testing).
  • Internal failure costs: defects caught before shipment (scrap, rework).
  • External failure costs: defects reaching customers (warranty claims, recalls, returns).
Example 1 — Classify the Quality Cost

Choose the quality-cost category.

Solution

Prevention and appraisal costs are incurred to keep poor quality from reaching the customer. Internal and external failure costs are incurred because poor quality has occurred, before or after shipment.

Total cost of quality = $600,000

Example 3 — External Failure Share

What percent of last year's total cost of quality was external failure cost?

%
Solution

$240,000 ÷ $600,000 = 40%.

External failure = 40% of the total

Example 4 — Change in Total Cost of Quality

By how much did total cost of quality decrease this year? Total this year is the sum of the four categories.

$
Solution

This year: $200,000 + $110,000 + $100,000 + $130,000 = $540,000.
Decrease: $600,000 − $540,000 = $60,000.

Decrease = $60,000

Example 5 — Invest More, Save More

By how much did prevention-plus-appraisal spending increase?

$
Solution

This year ($310,000) − last year ($210,000) = $100,000.

Increase in prevention and appraisal = $100,000

That extra $100,000 cut internal and external failure costs by $160,000, a net improvement of $60,000. Spending more on prevention and appraisal is the best way to lower the total cost of quality.
Delivery cycle time & MCE

Throughput Time, Delivery Cycle Time and MCE

Brevard's average times for a keg order, in days: wait time before production starts 6.0; process time 2.4; inspection time 0.6; move time 0.8; queue time 4.2.

  • Throughput time = Process time + Inspection time + Move time + Queue time
  • Delivery cycle time = Wait time + Throughput time
  • Manufacturing cycle efficiency (MCE) = Value-added time (process time) ÷ Throughput time
Only process time adds value; inspection, move and queue time do not.
Example 1 — Throughput Time

What is the throughput time, in days?

days
Solution

2.4 + 0.6 + 0.8 + 4.2 = 8.0 days.

Throughput time = 8.0 days

Example 2 — Manufacturing Cycle Efficiency

What is MCE? Enter it as a percent.

%
Solution

2.4 ÷ 8.0 = 0.30, or 30%. Seventy percent of throughput time is non-value-added.

MCE = 30%

Example 3 — Delivery Cycle Time

What is the delivery cycle time, in days?

days
Solution

6.0 + 8.0 = 14.0 days.

Delivery cycle time = 14.0 days

Example 4 — Eliminate Queue Time

If lean production eliminated all queue time, what would the new MCE be (as a percent, to one decimal)?

Need a hint?
Throughput time without queue time = 2.4 + 0.6 + 0.8.
%
Solution

New throughput time: 2.4 + 0.6 + 0.8 = 3.8 days. MCE = 2.4 ÷ 3.8 = 63.2%.

New MCE = 63.2%

Cutting non-value-added time raises MCE sharply and shortens delivery cycle time, which is why lean production focuses on queue time.
OEE

Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE)

A bottling machine at Brevard had 8,000 minutes available this week and actually ran 5,600 minutes. Its ideal run rate is 5 units per minute, and its actual run rate was 4.5 units per minute. Defect-free output was 22,680 units.

  • Utilization rate = Actual run time ÷ Machine time available
  • Efficiency rate = Actual run rate ÷ Ideal run rate
  • Quality rate = Defect-free output ÷ Total output
  • OEE = Utilization rate × Efficiency rate × Quality rate
Example 1 — Utilization Rate

What is the utilization rate? Enter it as a decimal.

Solution

5,600 ÷ 8,000 = 0.70.

Utilization rate = 0.70

Example 2 — Efficiency Rate

What is the efficiency rate?

Solution

4.5 ÷ 5 = 0.90.

Efficiency rate = 0.90

Example 3 — Quality Rate

Total output was 5,600 minutes × 4.5 units per minute = 25,200 units. What is the quality rate?

Solution

22,680 ÷ 25,200 = 0.90.

Quality rate = 0.90

Example 4 — OEE

What is overall equipment effectiveness? Enter it as a decimal to three places.

Solution

0.70 × 0.90 × 0.90 = 0.567.
Check: defect-free output ÷ maximum possible output = 22,680 ÷ (8,000 × 5) = 22,680 ÷ 40,000 = 0.567.

OEE = 0.567

Only about 57% of the machine's potential output was good product. The biggest single loss is utilization: 2,400 idle minutes × 5 units = 12,000 units of lost output.
Wrap-up

Chapter 12 at a Glance

  • A balanced scorecard has four categories and links measures with if-then hypotheses that reflect the company's strategy.
  • Cost of quality: prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure. Invest in prevention and appraisal to cut failure costs.
  • Throughput time = process + inspection + move + queue; delivery cycle time adds wait time; MCE = process time ÷ throughput time.
  • OEE = utilization × efficiency × quality.

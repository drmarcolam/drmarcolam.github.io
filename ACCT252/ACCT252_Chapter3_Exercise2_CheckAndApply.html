Recall card: formulas for this exercise
  • Overhead applied for the year = Predetermined rate × Actual amount of the base for the year.
  • Underapplied = actual overhead > applied overhead (increase cost of goods sold). Overapplied = applied > actual (decrease cost of goods sold).
  • Jobs are costed during the year with the predetermined rate. The actual rate is not known until the year ends.
  • Departmental rate = Department’s estimated overhead ÷ Department’s estimated base. Overhead applied to a job = each department’s rate × the job’s activity in that department, added together.
  • Total job cost = Direct materials + Direct labor + Overhead applied. Unit product cost = Total job cost ÷ Units produced.

Part 1

Over- and Underapplied Overhead

At year-end, Sylva Mountain Millwork shows actual manufacturing overhead of $192,500 and 12,500 actual direct labor-hours. Its predetermined rate was $15.00 per DLH.

Question 1 — Overhead Applied for the Year

How much overhead was applied to jobs during the year?

Need a hint?
Predetermined rate × actual hours for the year.
$
Solution

12,500 DLH × $15.00 = $187,500.

Applied overhead = $187,500

Question 2 — The Difference

What is the difference between actual overhead and applied overhead? Enter the amount only; the next question asks about direction.

Need a hint?
Compare actual overhead with the applied overhead from Question 1.
$
Solution

Actual $192,500 − applied $187,500 = $5,000.

Difference = $5,000

Question 3 — Which One?

Is overhead underapplied or overapplied?

Need a hint?
If actual overhead is more than applied overhead, jobs were charged too little.
Solution

Applied overhead ($187,500) was less than actual overhead ($192,500), so too little overhead was charged to jobs. Overhead is underapplied by $5,000.

Question 4 — Year-End Adjustment

Almost all of the year’s jobs have been sold. What adjustment is made at year-end?

Need a hint?
Think about what underapplied overhead says about the costs already recorded for the jobs.
Solution

Underapplied overhead means jobs were under-costed. The $5,000 is added to cost of goods sold, which reduces income.

If overhead were overapplied, cost of goods sold would be decreased instead.

Find the Mistake

After year-end, a new bookkeeper recomputed the cost of Job 118 ($7,200 of direct materials and 160 direct labor-hours at $20 per hour) and wrote this:

Bookkeeper's work for Job 118
LineBookkeeper’s work
Overhead rate$192,500 ÷ 12,500 DLH = $15.40 per DLH
Overhead applied to Job 118160 DLH × $15.40 = $2,464
Direct materials$7,200
Direct labor160 hrs × $20 = $3,200
Total manufacturing cost$12,864

Use the predetermined rate of $15.00 per DLH when you correct the work.

Question 1 — The Error

What did the bookkeeper do wrong?

Need a hint?
Look at how the $15.40 rate was calculated.
Solution

The bookkeeper divided actual overhead by actual hours ($15.40). Jobs are costed with the predetermined rate ($15.00), which was set before the year started.

Question 2 — Why Not the Actual Rate?

Why can’t a company use the actual rate to cost jobs during the year?

Solution

The actual rate can only be computed after the year ends. A predetermined rate lets the company cost jobs and quote prices as the work is done.

Part 3

Departmental Overhead Rates

Sylva is considering separate rates for its two departments instead of one plantwide rate. Estimated data:

Departmental overhead data
DepartmentAllocation baseEstimated overheadEstimated base
MillingMachine-hours (MH)$96,0004,000 MH
FinishingDirect labor-hours (DLH)$84,0007,000 DLH

Job 118 (8 cabinets) used 50 machine-hours in Milling and 90 direct labor-hours in Finishing. Direct materials ($7,200) and direct labor ($3,200) are unchanged.

Question 1 — Milling Rate

What is the predetermined rate per machine-hour in Milling?

Need a hint?
Milling’s estimated overhead ÷ Milling’s estimated machine-hours.
$ per MH
Solution

$96,000 ÷ 4,000 MH = $24.00 per MH.

Milling rate = $24.00 per MH

Question 2 — Finishing Rate

What is the predetermined rate per direct labor-hour in Finishing?

Need a hint?
Finishing uses direct labor-hours as its base.
$ per DLH
Solution

$84,000 ÷ 7,000 DLH = $12.00 per DLH.

Finishing rate = $12.00 per DLH

Question 3 — Overhead Applied to Job 118

How much overhead is applied to Job 118 using the two departmental rates?

Need a hint?
Apply each department’s rate to the job’s activity in that department, then add.
$
Solution

Milling: 50 MH × $24.00 = $1,200.
Finishing: 90 DLH × $12.00 = $1,080.
Total: $2,280.

Overhead applied = $2,280

Question 4 — New Unit Cost

What is Job 118’s unit product cost per cabinet using departmental rates?

Need a hint?
Add materials, labor and the new overhead, then divide by the number of cabinets.
$
Solution

Total: $7,200 + $3,200 + $2,280 = $12,680. ÷ 8 cabinets = $1,585 per cabinet.

Unit product cost = $1,585

Under the single plantwide rate, the unit cost was $1,600. Job 118 uses little machine time, so departmental rates charge it less overhead.

Exercise 2 at a Glance

  • Applied for the year = rate × actual base. Underapplied = actual > applied (cost of goods sold goes up); overapplied = applied > actual (cost of goods sold goes down).
  • Jobs are costed with the predetermined rate, not the actual rate, because actual figures are not known until year-end.

Problem

Chimney Rock Sporting Goods, Inc.

Chimney Rock Sporting Goods, Inc., located in Chimney Rock, North Carolina, manufactures basketballs. Last year the company sold 34,000 balls at a selling price of $25 per ball. Variable expenses were $15.00 per ball, of which 60% was direct labor cost.

Fixed expenses$232,400

For reference, last year's results in a contribution format income statement (not graded):

ItemAmount
Sales$850,000
Variable expenses$510,000
Contribution margin$340,000
Fixed expenses$232,400
Net operating income$107,600
Required:
  1. Compute (a) last year's CM ratio and break-even point in balls, and (b) the degree of operating leverage at last year's sales level.
  2. The sales manager expects that the cost of direct labor next year will increase by $3 per ball. Assuming the selling price remains at $25 per ball, what will next year's CM ratio and break-even point in balls be?
  3. Refer to the data in (2) above. If the expected direct labor cost increase takes place, how many balls will have to be sold next year to earn the same net operating income, $107,600, as last year?
  4. Refer again to the data in (2) above. The president feels the company must raise the selling price of its basketballs. If Chimney Rock Sporting Goods wants to maintain the same CM ratio as last year (requirement 1a), what selling price per ball must it charge next year to cover the increased labor cost?
  5. Refer to the original data. The company is considering the construction of a new, automated plant. The new plant would slash variable expenses per ball by 40%, but it would cause fixed expenses per year to double. If the new plant is built, what would be the company's new CM ratio and new break-even point in balls?
  6. (a) Refer to the data in (5) above. If the new plant is built, how many balls will have to be sold next year to earn the same net operating income, $107,600, as last year? (b) Assume the new plant is built and that next year the company manufactures and sells this same number of balls, 34,000.

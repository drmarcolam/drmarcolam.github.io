Return on equity

Computing ROE

Facebook (Meta) 2018 ($ millions): net income $22,112; revenue $55,838. Total assets were $97,334 (2018) and $84,524 (2017); total liabilities were $13,207 and $10,177.

ROE = Net income ÷ Average stockholders' equity. Equity = Total assets − Total liabilities.

Example 1 — Average Equity

Compute Facebook's average stockholders' equity, in $ millions.

DuPont disaggregation

ROE = ROA × Financial Leverage

ROE reflects both company performance (ROA) and how assets are financed (financial leverage).

  • ROA = Net income ÷ Average total assets
  • Financial leverage (FL) = Average total assets ÷ Average equity
  • ROE = ROA × FL, and ROA = Profit margin × Asset turnover.
Example 1 — Facebook's ROA

Compute ROA. Average total assets are ($97,334 + $84,524) ÷ 2 = $90,929 million.

%
Solution

$22,112 ÷ $90,929 = 0.2432, or 24.3%.

ROA = 24.3%

Example 2 — Facebook's Financial Leverage

Compute financial leverage (two decimals).

Solution

$90,929 ÷ $79,237 = 1.15.

FL = 1.15

Check: 24.3% × 1.15 = 27.9%, the ROE from the prior section.
Example 3 — Profit Margin

Compute Facebook's profit margin (percent, one decimal).

%
Solution

$22,112 ÷ $55,838 = 39.6%.

Profit margin = 39.6%

Example 4 — Asset Turnover

Compute Facebook's asset turnover (three decimals).

Solution

$55,838 ÷ $90,929 = 0.614.

Asset turnover = 0.614

39.6% × 0.614 = 24.3% = ROA.
Example 5 — Leverage and Risk

Holding assets and profitability constant, what happens if a company finances more of its assets with debt and less with equity?

Solution

Less equity for the same assets raises FL and therefore ROE. But debt is a contractual obligation, so greater leverage means higher risk.

Working capital

The Cash Conversion Cycle

CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO: days inventory outstanding, plus days sales outstanding, minus days payable outstanding. A lower (or negative) CCC means less cash tied up in operations.

DIO = Average inventory ÷ COGS × 365 · DSO = Average receivables ÷ Revenue × 365 · DPO = Average payables ÷ COGS × 365

Example 1 — Pfizer's CCC

Pfizer holds inventory 96 days (DIO), collects receivables in 41 days (DSO) and pays suppliers in 66 days (DPO). Compute its CCC in days.

Need a hint?
DIO + DSO − DPO.
days
Solution

96 + 41 − 66 = 71 days.

CCC = 71 days

Example 2 — Halliburton's DSO

Halliburton ($ millions): revenue $23,995; cost of sales $21,009; average receivables $5,135; average inventory $2,712; average payables $2,786. Compute days sales outstanding (365 days, one decimal).

days
Solution

$5,135 ÷ $23,995 × 365 = 78.1 days.

DSO = 78.1 days

Example 3 — Halliburton's DIO

Compute Halliburton's days inventory outstanding (one decimal).

days
Solution

$2,712 ÷ $21,009 × 365 = 47.1 days. Inventory and payables use cost of sales.

DIO = 47.1 days

Example 4 — Halliburton's CCC

Halliburton's DPO is $2,786 ÷ $21,009 × 365 = 48.4 days. Compute its cash conversion cycle.

days
Solution

47.1 + 78.1 − 48.4 = 76.8 days.

CCC = 76.8 days

Operating focus

Net Operating Assets and Net Nonoperating Obligations

Companies create value mainly through core operations, so analysts separate operating from nonoperating items.

  • NOA = Operating assets − Operating liabilities
  • NNO = Nonoperating liabilities − Nonoperating assets (borrowings minus cash and investments)
Example 1 — Operating or Nonoperating?

Classify each balance sheet item.

Solution

Receivables, PP&E and accounts payable arise from operations. Cash, marketable securities and interest-bearing debt are financing items.

Example 2 — Net Operating Assets

Home Depot (Feb. 3, 2019, $ millions): operating assets $42,225; operating liabilities $16,679. Compute net operating assets.

$
Solution

$42,225 − $16,679 = $25,546.

NOA = $25,546 million

Example 3 — Net Nonoperating Obligations

Home Depot's nonoperating liabilities are $29,202 and nonoperating assets are $1,778. Compute net nonoperating obligations.

$
Solution

$29,202 − $1,778 = $27,424.

NNO = $27,424 million

Check: NOA $25,546 = NNO $27,424 + equity (−$1,878). Home Depot's buybacks leave its equity negative, which inflates ROE.
Operating profit

NOPAT: Net Operating Profit After Tax

Home Depot ($ millions): net operating profit before tax (NOPBT) $15,530; pretax net nonoperating expense $974; reported tax expense $3,435; statutory tax rate 22%.

Interest and other nonoperating expenses create a tax shield. To get taxes on operating profit, add the tax shield back to reported tax expense. NOPAT = NOPBT − tax on operating profit.

Example 1 — Tax Shield

Compute the tax shield on Home Depot's nonoperating expense (one decimal).

$
Solution

$974 × 22% = $214.3.

Tax shield = $214.3 million

Example 2 — NOPAT

Compute Home Depot's NOPAT, in $ millions.

Need a hint?
Add the tax shield to reported tax expense first.
$
Solution

Tax on operating profit = $3,435 + $214.3 = $3,649.3.
NOPAT = $15,530 − $3,649.3 = $11,880.7.

NOPAT = $11,881 million

RNOA

Return on Net Operating Assets

Netflix 2018 ($ thousands): revenue $15,794,341; NOPAT $1,506,681; NOA $11,804,340 (2018) and $7,258,593 (2017).

  • RNOA = NOPAT ÷ Average NOA
  • NOPM (net operating profit margin) = NOPAT ÷ Revenue
  • NOAT (net operating asset turnover) = Revenue ÷ Average NOA
  • RNOA = NOPM × NOAT
Example 1 — RNOA

Compute Netflix's RNOA (percent, one decimal).

%
Solution

Average NOA = ($11,804,340 + $7,258,593) ÷ 2 = $9,531,467. RNOA = $1,506,681 ÷ $9,531,467 = 15.8%.

RNOA = 15.8%

Example 2 — NOPM

Compute Netflix's net operating profit margin (percent, one decimal).

%
Solution

$1,506,681 ÷ $15,794,341 = 9.5%.

NOPM = 9.5%

Example 3 — NOAT

Compute Netflix's net operating asset turnover (two decimals).

Solution

$15,794,341 ÷ $9,531,467 = 1.66.

NOAT = 1.66

9.5% × 1.66 = 15.8%, which confirms RNOA = NOPM × NOAT. Companies can earn a high RNOA with a high margin, high turnover, or both.
Nonoperating return

ROE = Operating Return + Nonoperating Return

With NCIR = 1: ROE = RNOA + Nonoperating return, where nonoperating return = FLEV × Spread, FLEV = Average NNO ÷ Average equity, and Spread = RNOA − net nonoperating expense percent (NNEP).

Example 1 — Pfizer's Nonoperating Return

Pfizer 2022: FLEV = 0.12748 and Spread = 32.244%. Compute the nonoperating return (percent, two decimals).

%
Solution

0.12748 × 32.244% = 4.11%.

Nonoperating return = 4.11%

Pfizer's ROE of 36.3% = RNOA 32.1% + nonoperating return 4.1% (≈); about 88.5% of ROE came from operations.
Example 2 — Apple's Negative Leverage

Apple's RNOA is 864.9% but its ROE is only 175.5%.

Solution

Apple holds more nonoperating assets (cash and investments) than nonoperating obligations, so net nonoperating obligations are negative. Idle cash earns less than operating assets, so nonoperating return is negative and ROE falls below RNOA.

Vertical and horizontal analysis

Common-Size and Trend Analysis

Vertical analysis expresses statements as percents (income statement items ÷ sales; balance sheet items ÷ total assets). Horizontal analysis compares the same line item across periods.

Example 1 — Vertical or Horizontal?

Which type of analysis does each task describe?

Solution

Vertical (common-size) analysis scales every line by one base amount, which adjusts for size and currency. Horizontal analysis tracks change over time.

Wrap-up

Module 4 at a Glance

  • ROE = ROA × Financial leverage; ROA = Profit margin × Asset turnover. Higher leverage raises ROE and risk.
  • CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO: fewer days of cash tied up in operations is better.
  • Separate operating from nonoperating items: NOA, NNO, NOPAT; RNOA = NOPAT ÷ Average NOA = NOPM × NOAT.
  • ROE = RNOA + (FLEV × Spread) (with NCIR = 1); use vertical and horizontal analysis to compare across firms and years.

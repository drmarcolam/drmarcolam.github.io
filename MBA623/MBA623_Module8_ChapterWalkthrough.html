Stockholders' equity

Contributed Capital, Earned Capital and Book Value per Share

Equity has contributed capital (what owners invested) and earned capital (profits retained and other comprehensive items).

Book value per share = (Total equity − Preferred equity) ÷ Common shares outstanding.

Example 1 — Classify the Account

Is each account contributed or earned capital?

Solution

Stock and paid-in capital come from owners. Retained earnings and AOCI accumulate from the company's own results.

Example 2 — Book Value per Share

A company has total stockholders' equity of $1,200,000, no preferred stock and 150,000 common shares outstanding. Compute book value per share.

$
Solution

$1,200,000 ÷ 150,000 = $8.00.

Book value per share = $8.00

Stock issuances

Issuing Preferred and Common Stock

When stock is issued, cash increases by the proceeds; the stock account increases by par value and additional paid-in capital (APIC) by the excess.

Example 1 — Preferred Issuance: Cash

A company issues 8,000 shares of $50 par preferred stock at $84 per share. How much cash does it receive?

$
Solution

8,000 × $84 = $672,000.

Cash = $672,000

Example 2 — Preferred Issuance: APIC

In the same issuance, how much is credited to additional paid-in capital?

$
Solution

Preferred stock at par = 8,000 × $50 = $400,000. APIC = $672,000 − $400,000 = $272,000.

APIC = $272,000

Example 3 — Common Issuance: APIC

A company issues 19,000 shares of $1 par common stock at $10 per share. How much is credited to APIC?

$
Solution

Proceeds = $190,000. Common stock at par = $19,000. APIC = $190,000 − $19,000 = $171,000.

APIC = $171,000

Treasury stock

Repurchases and Resales

Repurchased shares held by the company are treasury stock, a deduction from equity at cost. No gain or loss is reported on the income statement.

Example 1 — Cost of Treasury Stock

J&J repurchases 200 shares at $120 per share. What is the cost of the treasury stock?

$
Solution

200 × $120 = $24,000, reported as a deduction in equity; cash decreases by $24,000.

Treasury stock = $24,000

Example 2 — Resale Above Cost

J&J later resells the 200 shares at $125 per share. By how much does additional paid-in capital increase?

$
Solution

Proceeds = 200 × $125 = $25,000; cost = $24,000. The $1,000 excess increases APIC (not income).

APIC increases by $1,000

Example 3 — Average Repurchase Price

J&J has repurchased 506,246,000 shares for a cumulative cost of $41,694 million. What is the average price per share (two decimals)?

$
Solution

$41,694 million ÷ 506.246 million shares = $82.36.

Average price = $82.36

Stock-based compensation

Expense and Cash Effects

Companies grant stock, options and discounted stock purchases to align employee and shareholder interests. The company estimates fair value at grant and expenses it over the service period.

Example 1 — Stock Purchase Plan Expense

Employees buy 25,000 shares at a 15% discount from the $78.94 market price. What compensation expense is recognized?

$
Solution

25,000 × $78.94 × 15% = $296,025, the value of the discount.

Compensation expense = $296,025

Example 2 — Is It a Cash Cost?

Stock-based compensation is added back on the statement of cash flows.

Solution

The expense is noncash, so it is added back in operating cash flow. But buying shares in the market to offset dilution from employee awards uses cash, so there is an economic cost.

Dividends and splits

Dividends, Stock Splits and Retained Earnings

Cash dividends reduce cash and retained earnings. A stock split increases the number of shares and lowers par and price per share; total equity does not change.

Dividend payout = Dividends ÷ Net income. Dividend yield = Dividend per share ÷ Stock price.

Example 1 — Ending Retained Earnings

Springwerth Company began the year with retained earnings of $537,000, earned net income of $203,000, declared a $46,000 stock dividend and paid $55,000 of cash dividends. Compute ending retained earnings.

$
Solution

$537,000 + $203,000 − $46,000 − $55,000 = $639,000. A stock dividend reduces retained earnings and increases contributed capital.

Ending retained earnings = $639,000

Example 2 — Declaring a $910.8 Million Dividend

When the board declares the dividend (before payment), what is the effect?

Solution

Declaration creates a liability and reduces retained earnings. Cash falls only when the dividend is paid.

Example 3 — Shares After a Split

Apstein Company has 300,000 shares of $9 par stock and declares a 3-for-2 split. How many shares are outstanding after the split?

Solution

300,000 × 3 ÷ 2 = 450,000 shares.

Shares after split = 450,000

Example 4 — Par Value After the Split

What is the par value per share after the split?

$
Solution

Total par value stays $2,700,000: $2,700,000 ÷ 450,000 shares = $6.00 per share.

New par value = $6.00

A split changes shares and par per share but not total equity; companies split to reduce the share price and improve liquidity.
Example 5 — Dividend Yield

A stock trading at $60 pays annual dividends of $2.40 per share. What is the dividend yield (percent)?

%
Solution

$2.40 ÷ $60 = 4.0%.

Dividend yield = 4.0%

Accumulated other comprehensive income

AOCI and Comprehensive Income

AOCI holds cumulative unrealized gains and losses (foreign currency translation, certain securities) that bypass net income.

Example 1 — True or False?

Decide whether each statement is true or false.

Solution

AOCI is often negative (as for J&J). Changes in these items are recorded in other comprehensive income, not net income, and are unrealized until the asset is sold or the liability settled.

Earnings per share

Basic and Diluted EPS

Wells Fargo ($ millions, shares in millions): net income $22,393; preferred dividends $1,556; common dividends $7,955; average common shares 4,800; diluted average shares 4,838.

  • Basic EPS = (Net income − Preferred dividends) ÷ Average common shares
  • Diluted EPS assumes options and convertible securities are exercised or converted, so it is usually lower.
Example 1 — Basic EPS

Compute basic EPS (two decimals).

Need a hint?
Subtract preferred dividends from net income first.
$
Solution

($22,393 − $1,556) ÷ 4,800 = $20,837 ÷ 4,800 = $4.34.

Basic EPS = $4.34

Example 2 — Diluted EPS

Compute diluted EPS (two decimals).

$
Solution

$20,837 ÷ 4,838 = $4.31.

Diluted EPS = $4.31

Example 3 — Dividend Payout

Compute the common dividend payout ratio (percent, one decimal): common dividends ÷ net income available to common.

%
Solution

$7,955 ÷ $20,837 = 38.2%.

Payout = 38.2%

Wrap-up

Module 8 at a Glance

  • Equity = contributed capital (stock + APIC − treasury stock) plus earned capital (retained earnings + AOCI).
  • Issuances credit par and APIC; treasury stock is carried at cost and resale gains go to APIC.
  • Stock-based compensation is a noncash expense with real economic cost; splits change shares, not total equity; dividends reduce retained earnings when declared.
  • Basic EPS = (NI − preferred dividends) ÷ average shares; diluted EPS uses a larger share count.

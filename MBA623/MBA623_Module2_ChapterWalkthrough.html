Balance sheet

Classifying Balance Sheet Accounts

The balance sheet reports assets, liabilities and stockholders' equity at a point in time: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

  • Assets are owned or controlled resources that arise from past transactions and are expected to provide future benefits.
  • Liabilities are future economic sacrifices (amounts that must be repaid or settled).
  • Equity is capital invested by owners plus retained earnings.
Example 1 — Asset, Liability or Equity?

Classify each balance sheet account.

Solution

Cash and equipment are resources (assets). Wages payable and long-term debt are obligations (liabilities). Common stock and retained earnings are owner financing (equity).

Example 2 — Current or Long-Term?

Classify each item as a current asset, long-term asset, current liability or long-term liability.

Solution

Items expected to be converted to cash (or settled) within a year are current. Inventories and accounts payable are current; PP&E and 10-year bonds are long-term. Unearned revenue is cash received in advance, so it is a current liability.

Analyzing the balance sheet

Net Working Capital and Common-Size Analysis

Abercrombie & Fitch ($ thousands): current assets $1,335,950; long-term assets $1,049,643; total assets $2,385,593; current liabilities $558,917.

  • Net working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities.
  • Common-size balance sheet: divide every line by total assets so companies of different size (or currency) can be compared.
Example 1 — Net Working Capital

Compute Abercrombie & Fitch's net working capital, in $ thousands.

Need a hint?
Current assets minus current liabilities.
$
Solution

$1,335,950 − $558,917 = $777,033.

Net working capital = $777,033 thousand

Example 2 — Common-Size Current Assets

Express current assets as a percent of total assets (one decimal).

%
Solution

$1,335,950 ÷ $2,385,593 = 0.560, or 56.0%.

Current assets = 56.0% of total assets

Retailers carry large inventories, so current assets are a high share of total assets.
Book vs. market value

Book Value vs. Market Value

Blue Ridge Outfitters has 4,000,000 common shares outstanding trading at $38.50. Its stockholders' equity (book value) is $96,000,000.

Market value (market cap) = Shares outstanding × Stock price. Book value is based on historical cost and excludes assets that cannot be reliably measured, so book value ≠ market value.

Example 1 — Market Capitalization

Compute Blue Ridge Outfitters' market capitalization, in $ millions.

Need a hint?
Shares × price per share.
$
Solution

4,000,000 × $38.50 = $154,000,000.

Market value = $154.0 million

Example 2 — Book vs. Market

Complete the statement.

Solution

$154.0 million exceeds $96.0 million. Market value reflects expected future performance and unrecorded assets such as brand and management talent; GAAP records most assets at historical cost.

Income statement

Recognizing Revenues and Expenses

The income statement reports revenues earned and expenses incurred during a period. Under accrual accounting, recognition does not depend on when cash moves.

  • Revenue recognition: recognize revenue when the company has done what it promised in the sales contract.
  • Expense recognition (matching): recognize expenses when incurred to generate that revenue.
Example 1 — Cash vs. Revenue Timing

For each situation, which comes first?

Solution

Cash received in advance creates unearned revenue, with revenue recognized later. A credit sale is recognized when delivered, before the cash arrives. A cash sale at the register is recognized when cash is received.

Analyzing the income statement

Gross Profit Margin and Net Profit Margin

($ thousands) Abercrombie & Fitch: sales $3,590,109; cost of goods sold $1,430,193; net income $78,808. TJX: sales $38,972,934; gross profit $11,141,757.

Gross profit margin = Gross profit ÷ Sales; net profit margin = Net income ÷ Sales. A common-size income statement expresses every line as a percent of sales.

Example 1 — Gross Profit Margin: Abercrombie

Compute Abercrombie & Fitch's gross profit margin (percent, one decimal).

Need a hint?
First find gross profit = sales − cost of goods sold.
%
Solution

Gross profit = $3,590,109 − $1,430,193 = $2,159,916. Margin = $2,159,916 ÷ $3,590,109 = 60.2%.

Gross profit margin = 60.2%

Example 2 — Gross Profit Margin: TJX

Compute TJX's gross profit margin (percent, one decimal).

%
Solution

$11,141,757 ÷ $38,972,934 = 28.6%.

Gross profit margin = 28.6%

Example 3 — Net Profit Margin: Abercrombie

Compute Abercrombie & Fitch's net profit margin (percent, one decimal).

%
Solution

$78,808 ÷ $3,590,109 = 2.2%.

Net profit margin = 2.2%

Abercrombie keeps 60.2 cents of each sales dollar after product cost but only 2.2 cents after all expenses, so operating expenses are large relative to its gross profit.
Statement of stockholders' equity

Retained Earnings Roll-Forward

Retained earnings are updated each period: Beginning + Net income − Dividends (− other reductions) = Ending.

Statement of cash flows

Operating, Investing and Financing Cash Flows

The statement of cash flows explains the change in cash by activity.

  • Operating: cash from the company's day-to-day transactions.
  • Investing: acquisitions and sales of long-term assets and investments.
  • Financing: borrowing, repaying debt, issuing or repurchasing stock, and paying dividends.
Example 1 — Classify the Cash Flow

Choose the section of the statement of cash flows for each item.

Solution

Customer and supplier cash is operating. Equipment purchases are investing. Dividends and stock repurchases are financing.

Financial statement linkages

How the Statements Connect

The statements articulate: net income from the income statement increases retained earnings on the balance sheet, and the statement of cash flows reconciles beginning and ending cash.

Example 1 — Effects of a Credit Sale

Blue Ridge Outfitters sells $500 of goods on credit. What is the effect of the sale (ignore cost of goods sold)?

Solution

Accounts receivable (an asset) rises by $500 and revenue raises net income, and so retained earnings, by $500. No cash is collected yet, so cash and operating cash flow are unchanged.

Public information

SEC Filings and Other Sources

Beyond the financial statements, investors use SEC filings, analyst reports and credit reports.

Example 1 — Which SEC Form?

Choose the form that matches each description.

Solution

The 10-K is the annual report and contains the MD&A (Item 7). The 10-Q is quarterly. The 8-K reports significant events such as executive departures within four days.

Wrap-up

Module 2 at a Glance

  • Assets = Liabilities + Equity; current items are expected to turn to cash or be settled within a year; net working capital = current assets − current liabilities.
  • Accrual accounting recognizes revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of cash timing.
  • Common-size statements and margins allow comparison across years and companies.
  • Retained earnings roll forward: Beginning + Net income − Dividends = Ending; the four statements are linked through retained earnings and cash.

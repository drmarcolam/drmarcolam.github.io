Financial vs. managerial accounting

Two Accounting Systems, Two Audiences

Financial accounting communicates results to outside decision makers. Managerial accounting gives internal managers the information they need to plan, direct and control.

  • Financial: external users, GAAP, highly aggregated, long periods, past results.
  • Managerial: internal users, no required format, detailed (by product, customer, segment), short periods, future-oriented, monetary and nonmonetary.
Example 1 — Which System?

Choose the accounting system each description fits.

Solution

Financial accounting is a reporting system for external users under GAAP. Managerial accounting is a decision-making tool for internal users, with flexible, detailed, forward-looking information.

Mission, goals and strategy

Mission, Goals and Strategy

A mission states the basic purpose of the organization; a goal is a definable, measurable target; a strategy is a course of action to achieve goals.

  • Cost leadership: low costs let the company win on price or profit.
  • Differentiation: a perceived unique product earns a premium price.
  • Market niche: serve a narrow, specific segment.
Example 1 — Mission, Goal or Strategy?

Classify each statement.

Solution

The mission describes purpose; a goal is measurable and time-bound; a strategy is the route to reach goals.

Example 2 — Strategic Positioning

Which strategic positioning does each company illustrate?

Solution

Low cost and low price mark cost leadership; a perceived unique product that commands a premium is differentiation; a narrow segment is a niche.

Planning and control

Line and Staff Positions and Performance Reports

Managers plan, organize, direct and control. Line positions are directly involved in the organization's primary operations; staff positions support them.

Example 1 — Line or Staff?

Classify each position.

Solution

Line managers (production, sales) are tied directly to the company's main activities. Accounting, HR and IT support them.

Example 2 — Cost Variance

A performance report shows a budgeted cost of $50,000 and an actual cost of $54,500. What is the variance (enter the amount)?

$
Solution

$54,500 − $50,000 = $4,500. Spending exceeded budget, so the variance is unfavorable.

Variance = $4,500 unfavorable

Performance reports compare budgeted and actual results so managers can investigate differences and take corrective action.
Value chain

The Value Chain

The value chain is the set of value-producing activities from raw materials to the final consumer. Within a company, primary activities include inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, and service, supported by administration.

Example 1 — Which Activity?

Place each activity in the internal value chain.

Solution

Each activity adds value in sequence. Managerial accounting analyzes costs across the whole chain, not only inside the company.

Business trends

Performance Dimensions and Technology Trends

Competition is global and driven by cost, quality and time. Technology produces vast data that managerial accountants analyze.

Example 1 — Key Dimensions

Which dimension of competition does each measure reflect?

Solution

Cost is usually monetary; quality and time are often nonmonetary measures that managers also track.

Example 2 — Which Trend?

Match each description to a trend.

Solution

Big data supports prediction, RPA automates repetitive work, and ERM (as defined by COSO) identifies and manages risk across the organization.

Ethics and ESG

Ethics, Governance and ESG

Ethics goes beyond what is legal. The IMA's standards of ethical conduct include competence, confidentiality, integrity and credibility.

Example 1 — IMA Standards

Match each behavior to an IMA standard.

Solution

These four IMA standards guide management accountants through ethical dilemmas, which often start as a series of small compromises.

Example 2 — True or False?

Decide whether each statement is true or false.

Solution

Ethics goes beyond legality. SOX responded to corporate scandals. ESG asks companies to balance profit with environmental, social and governance responsibilities, not to abandon profit.

Wrap-up

Module 14 at a Glance

  • Financial accounting serves external users under GAAP; managerial accounting serves internal decision makers with detailed, future-oriented information.
  • Mission → goals → strategy (cost leadership, differentiation, niche); budgets and performance reports support control.
  • The value chain runs from inbound logistics through service; managers track cost, quality and time.
  • Management accountants follow IMA standards (competence, confidentiality, integrity, credibility) and consider ESG.

Relevant costs

Relevant vs. Irrelevant Costs and Revenues

A cost or revenue is relevant if it is a future amount that differs among the alternatives. Sunk costs are never relevant; opportunity costs are always relevant.

Example 1 — Relevant or Irrelevant?

Classify each item for a replace-or-keep decision.

Solution

Only future costs and revenues that differ among alternatives matter. Past (sunk) costs and depreciation of past purchases cannot be changed; disposal values, opportunity costs and differing operating costs can.

Changes in profit plans

Evaluating Alternatives Using Contribution Margin

HP sells adapters for $20 with variable costs of $10 per unit (CM $10) and currently sells 4,000 per month. Management considers three changes.

Example 1 — Alternative 1: Advertising

Spend $1,000 more on advertising and sell 300 more units. What is the change in monthly profit?

$
Solution

300 × $10 = $3,000 added contribution − $1,000 advertising = +$2,000.

Profit change = +$2,000

Example 2 — Alternative 2: Raise the Price $1

Raise the price by $1 and lose 300 units. What is the change in monthly profit?

Need a hint?
Remember the price increase applies only to units still sold.
$
Solution

Lost contribution: 300 × $10 = $(3,000). Extra revenue on the 3,700 units still sold: 3,700 × $1 = $3,700. Net +$700.

Profit change = +$700

Example 3 — Choose the Best

Complete the statement.

Solution

Alternative 1 (+$2,000) beats Alternative 2 (+$700) and Alternative 3 (+$300).

Example 4 — Discontinuing a Product

Suppose HP discontinues the adapter (4,000 units a month, CM $10 per unit, no fixed costs saved). How much monthly contribution is lost?

$
Solution

($20 − $10) × 4,000 = $40,000 per month that no longer helps cover fixed costs.

Contribution lost = $40,000

Example 5 — Dropping a Custom Line

A custom product line has contribution margin of $20,000 and traceable fixed costs of $10,000 that would be eliminated if it is dropped. What is the net change in profit from dropping it (enter the decrease as a positive amount)?

$
Solution

Lost contribution margin $20,000 − avoided fixed costs $10,000 = $10,000 decrease. Keep the line.

Profit decrease = $10,000

Special orders

Accepting a One-Time Special Order

A customer offers to buy 800 units at $16. HP has capacity of 5,000 units and normally produces 4,200. The order saves 25% of variable selling and administrative costs, so variable cost is $3 + $4 + $2 + $0.75 = $9.75 per unit.

Relevant: the special-order price, variable production costs and additional costs. Fixed costs are irrelevant when there is idle capacity.

Example 1 — Profit from the Special Order

How much does profit change if HP accepts the order using idle capacity?

$
Solution

Revenue 800 × $16 = $12,800 − variable cost 800 × $9.75 = $7,800 = +$5,000.

Profit increase = $5,000

Example 2 — At Full Capacity

Now HP is at capacity and the order would displace 800 regular units that earn $10 of contribution each. What is the net change in profit? (enter a decrease as a negative number)

Need a hint?
Subtract the contribution lost on regular sales.
$
Solution

$5,000 − opportunity cost 800 × $10 = $8,000 → net −$3,000. Reject the order.

Net change = −$3,000

Outsourcing

Make or Buy

HP uses 4,200 units a month (50,400 per year). It can buy the part for $9 or make it with materials $3, labor $4, variable overhead $2 per unit and a supervisor's fixed salary of $12,000 a year that is avoidable.

There are no relevant revenues in outsourcing. Compare the cost to buy with the avoidable cost to make, including opportunity costs.

Example 2 — With an Opportunity Cost

If the manufacturing space could instead be rented for $15,000 per year, what is the advantage of buying?

$
Solution

Sell or process further

Sell as Is or Process Further?

A boat maker can sell a hull for $9,000 or add masts, sails and rigging costing $2,500 and sell the complete sailboat for $10,500.

Process further only if the additional revenue exceeds the additional cost. The cost of making the hull is a sunk joint cost and is irrelevant.

Example 1 — Advantage of Further Processing

What is the advantage (disadvantage) of completing the sailboat? Enter a disadvantage as a negative number.

$
Solution

Additional revenue = $10,500 − $9,000 = $1,500. Additional cost = $2,500. Advantage = $1,500 − $2,500 = −$1,000. Sell the hull as is.

Advantage = $(1,000), a disadvantage

Limited resources

Using a Scarce Resource and the Theory of Constraints

Wilson Sports has only 24,000 board feet of white ash. A deluxe bat has CM $41 and needs 3.4 board feet; a basic bat has CM $29 and needs 2.2 board feet.

With one constraint, rank products by contribution margin per unit of the scarce resource.

Example 1 — CM per Board Foot: Basic

Compute the contribution margin per board foot for basic bats (two decimals).

$
Solution

$29 ÷ 2.2 = $13.18. (Deluxe: $41 ÷ 3.4 = $12.06.) Produce basic bats first.

CM per board foot = $13.18

Example 2 — Basic Bats with a Deluxe Minimum

Wilson must make at least 2,000 deluxe bats. After reserving wood for them, how many basic bats can it make with the remaining wood?

Solution

Wood for deluxe: 3.4 × 2,000 = 6,800. Remaining: 24,000 − 6,800 = 17,200. Basic bats = 17,200 ÷ 2.2 = 7,818.

Basic bats = 7,818

Example 3 — Total Contribution Margin

What total contribution margin does Wilson earn from that mix?

$
Solution

Deluxe: $41 × 2,000 = $82,000. Basic: $29 × 7,818 = $226,722. Total = $308,722.

Total CM = $308,722

Example 4 — Theory of Constraints

Decide whether each statement is true or false.

Solution

Throughput is limited by the bottleneck. Keeping non-bottleneck resources busy just builds inventory.

Wrap-up

Module 17 at a Glance

  • Relevant = future and differs among alternatives; sunk costs are never relevant, opportunity costs always are.
  • Use differential analysis for replacement, profit plans, dropping a line, and special orders (watch for capacity and opportunity costs).
  • Make or buy: compare the cost to buy with avoidable costs to make plus opportunity cost; sell or process further: incremental revenue vs. incremental cost.
  • With a scarce resource, rank by CM per unit of the constraint; focus on the bottleneck.

Business activities

The Three Business Activities

Every company plans and then carries out three kinds of activities. Financial statements report the results of all three.

  • Financing: how the company pays for its resources (borrowing and selling stock).
  • Investing: buying and selling the resources the company needs (equipment, buildings, investments).
  • Operating: the day-to-day work of buying inventory, selling to customers and paying expenses.
Example 1 — Classify the Activity

Choose the type of business activity for each transaction.

Solution

Borrowing and issuing stock raise the money to run the company (financing). Buying equipment is a purchase of long-term resources (investing). Selling products is part of day-to-day business (operating).

Accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Microsoft Corporation reports total assets of $364,840 million and total stockholders' equity of $166,542 million. Liabilities are not given.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity for every company at every point in time.

  • Liabilities are financing from nonowners (banks, suppliers, creditors).
  • Equity is financing from owners (stockholders).
Example 1 — Solve for Liabilities

Compute Microsoft's total liabilities, in $ millions.

Solution

Liabilities = Assets − Equity = $364,840 − $166,542 = $198,298.

Example 2 — Percent Owner-Financed

What percent of Microsoft's assets is financed by owners? Enter a percent, such as 12.3.

Need a hint?
Divide equity by total assets.
%
Solution

Equity ÷ Assets = $166,542 ÷ $364,840 = 0.4565, or 45.6%.

Owner financing = 45.6%

The other 54.4% ($198,298 ÷ $364,840) is nonowner financing.
Example 3 — Who Finances More?

Complete each statement about Microsoft.

Solution

Liabilities of $198,298 million exceed equity of $166,542 million, so more of Microsoft's financing comes from nonowners.

Four financial statements

Which Statement Reports It?

The four statements link together. The balance sheet reports a point in time; the other three report a period of time.

  • Balance sheet: assets, liabilities and equity at a date.
  • Income statement: revenues − expenses = net income for a period.
  • Statement of stockholders' equity: changes in equity accounts, including retained earnings.
  • Statement of cash flows: cash from operating, investing and financing activities.
Example 1 — Match the Statement

Choose the statement where each item is primarily reported.

Solution

Assets are on the balance sheet. Net income is the bottom line of the income statement. Operating cash flow is the first section of the statement of cash flows. The roll-forward of retained earnings appears in the statement of stockholders' equity.

Profitability: ROA

Return on Assets = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover

Medtronic (fiscal year, $ millions): net income $5,062; revenue $31,686; total assets $93,083 at the start of the year and $90,981 at the end.

  • ROA = Net income ÷ Average assets
  • Profit margin (PM) = Net income ÷ Sales
  • Asset turnover (AT) = Sales ÷ Average assets
  • ROA = PM × AT

Average assets = (beginning + ending) ÷ 2.

Example 1 — Average Total Assets

Compute Medtronic's average total assets, in $ millions.

$
Solution

($93,083 + $90,981) ÷ 2 = $92,032.

Average assets = $92,032 million

Example 2 — Return on Assets

Compute Medtronic's ROA as a percent, rounded to one decimal.

Need a hint?
Use average assets, not ending assets.
%
Solution

ROA = $5,062 ÷ $92,032 = 0.0550, or 5.5%.

ROA = 5.5%

Example 3 — Profit Margin

Compute Medtronic's profit margin as a percent.

%
Solution

PM = $5,062 ÷ $31,686 = 0.1598, or 16.0%.

Profit margin = 16.0%

Example 4 — Asset Turnover

Compute Medtronic's asset turnover, rounded to three decimals.

Solution

AT = $31,686 ÷ $92,032 = 0.344.

Asset turnover = 0.344

Check: 16.0% × 0.344 = 5.5%, which matches ROA.
Example 5 — Compare Two Companies

Boston Scientific has ROA of 2.2%, profit margin of 5.5% and asset turnover of 0.392. Compare it with Medtronic.

Solution

Medtronic's much stronger profit margin (16.0% vs. 5.5%) more than offsets Boston Scientific's slightly higher asset turnover (0.392 vs. 0.344), so Medtronic's ROA (5.5%) is more than double Boston Scientific's (2.2%).

Profitability: ROE

Return on Equity

Adobe Inc. ($ millions): net income $4,756; stockholders' equity $14,051 at the start of the year and $14,797 at the end.

ROE = Net income ÷ Average stockholders' equity. ROE measures the return to owners; ROA measures the return to all providers of financing.

Example 1 — Average Equity

Compute Adobe's average stockholders' equity, in $ millions.

$
Solution

($14,051 + $14,797) ÷ 2 = $14,424.

Average equity = $14,424 million

Example 2 — Return on Equity

Compute Adobe's ROE as a percent, rounded to one decimal.

%
Solution

ROE = $4,756 ÷ $14,424 = 0.3297, or 33.0%.

ROE = 33.0%

Example 3 — Effect of a Stock Repurchase

Adobe repurchased $6,550 million of its own stock during the year. Holding everything else constant, what happens?

Solution

A repurchase pays cash to owners and reduces equity, so the denominator of ROE falls. Net income falls only slightly (lost interest on the cash), so ROE usually rises. Without the repurchase, Adobe's average equity would have been about $17,699 million and ROE about 26.9%.

Business environment

Porter's Five Forces and Competitive Advantage

Financial results must be read in the context of the industry. Analysts use Porter's Five Forces and SWOT (internal strengths and weaknesses; external opportunities and threats).

  • Industry competition and threat of entry
  • Bargaining power of buyers and of suppliers
  • Threat of substitution
Example 1 — Identify the Force

Choose the force each situation illustrates.

Solution

Rivals in the same market create industry competition; easy switching gives buyers power; a sole source gives suppliers power; a different product that meets the same need is a substitute; a new firm joining the market is entry.

Example 2 — Source of Competitive Advantage

Choose the best description of each company's competitive advantage.

Solution

Apple's technology, design and legal protections differentiate its products and block entry. Walmart's size makes it a cost leader with power over suppliers. Uber faces low barriers to entry and a largely undifferentiated service.

Audits and governance

Audit Reports, Governance and Sarbanes-Oxley

Public companies must have their financial statements audited by an independent firm. The auditor reports to the board of directors (through its audit committee), not to management.

Example 1 — True or False?

Decide whether each statement is true or false.

Solution

Auditing relies on sampling and judgment, so the opinion is reasonable assurance. Management prepares the statements and the auditor expresses an opinion on them. Sarbanes-Oxley requires CEO and CFO certification, with fines and prison terms for false certification.

Wrap-up

Module 1 at a Glance

  • Assets = Liabilities + Equity; financing comes from nonowners (liabilities) and owners (equity).
  • The four statements: balance sheet (point in time), income statement, statement of stockholders' equity and statement of cash flows (periods of time).
  • ROA = Net income ÷ Average assets = Profit margin × Asset turnover; ROE = Net income ÷ Average equity.
  • Interpret results with Porter's Five Forces, SWOT, and the assurance provided by audits and governance.

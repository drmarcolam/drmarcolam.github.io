(c) True or false?

“Deferred revenue has increased steadily over the three-year period, which is of concern to analysts because higher liabilities generally indicate a weaker financial position.”

  1. False. Deferred revenue is settled by delivering software service, not by paying cash, so it is unlike debt. It represents revenue the company has already been paid for and has contracted to deliver, which makes its growth a positive signal about future revenue rather than a sign of weakness.
  2. True. Deferred revenue is a liability, and a company whose liabilities grow every year for three years is by definition in a weaker financial position at the end of the period.
  3. False. Deferred revenue did not in fact increase steadily over the three-year period, so the premise of the statement fails.
  4. True. Growing deferred revenue means the company is failing to deliver services it has already been paid for, which is why the obligation keeps accumulating.

(d) Cash received from customers this year

Start from revenue and adjust for the two balances that sit between revenue and cash. Cash arrives before revenue for subscriptions, so an increase in deferred revenue means cash came in that is not yet revenue. Cash arrives after revenue for billed work, so an increase in receivables means revenue was recorded for which no cash has arrived.

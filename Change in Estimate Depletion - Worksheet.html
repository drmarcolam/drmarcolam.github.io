Change in Estimate: Depletion

Units-of-production depletion and depreciation for a mineral mine, and the prospective effect of revising estimated ore reserves.

Problem

Spruce Pine Minerals Company

In 2027, Spruce Pine Minerals purchased land containing a mineral mine for $1,680,000. Additional costs of $680,000 were incurred to develop the mine. Geologists estimated that 250,000 tons of ore would be extracted. After the ore is removed, the land will have a resale value of $110,000.

To aid in the extraction, Spruce Pine built various structures and small storage buildings on the site at a cost of $157,500. These structures have a useful life of 10 years. The structures cannot be moved after the ore has been removed and will be left at the site. In addition, new equipment costing $73,000 was purchased and installed at the site. Spruce Pine does not plan to move the equipment to another site, but estimates that it can be sold at auction for $5,500 after the mining project is completed.

In 2027, 58,000 tons of ore were extracted and sold. In 2028, the estimate of total tons of ore in the mine was revised from 250,000 to 274,000. During 2028, 73,000 tons were extracted.

Required 1: Compute depletion of the mine and depreciation of the mining structures and equipment for 2027 and 2028. Spruce Pine uses the units-of-production method to determine depreciation on the mining structures and equipment.
Required 2: Compute the book value of the mineral mine, the structures, and the equipment as of December 31, 2028.

No prior-year amount is restated. The remaining cost at the beginning of the year of the revision is spread over the newly estimated remaining tons.

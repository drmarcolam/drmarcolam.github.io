The income statement and earnings quality

Income from continuing operations summarizes recurring, persistent performance. Format and classification help users assess earnings quality.

  • The income statement reports performance for a period. Income from continuing operations is the best base for predicting future results.
  • Single-step: total revenues and gains − total expenses and losses. Multiple-step: reports gross profit, operating income, then nonoperating items.
  • Multiple-step layout: Net sales − Cost of goods sold = Gross profit. Gross profit − Operating expenses (selling, G&A) = Operating income. ± Other income and expense (interest, gains and losses) = Income before taxes. − Income tax expense = Income from continuing operations.
  • Earnings quality is the ability of reported income to predict future results. Earnings management (income smoothing, “big bath” write-offs, aggressive revenue recognition, classifying operating expenses as nonoperating) reduces quality. Non-GAAP (pro forma) earnings exclude items; they must be reconciled to GAAP.

Discontinued operations, accounting changes, EPS, and comprehensive income

Items below continuing operations, accounting changes, per-share data, and OCI complete the picture of performance.

  • Discontinued operations: a component (or group of components) disposed of or held for sale that represents a strategic shift with a major effect on operations and financial results. Report separately, net of tax, after income from continuing operations: gain or loss from operations plus gain or loss on disposal.
  • Held for sale: write down to fair value less cost to sell if lower; stop depreciating.
  • Change in accounting principle: retrospective application (restate prior statements; adjust beginning retained earnings). Change in estimate: prospective. Error correction: restate prior periods as a prior period adjustment.
  • EPS = (net income − preferred dividends) ÷ weighted-average common shares. Report EPS for continuing operations, discontinued operations, and net income.
  • Comprehensive income = net income + other comprehensive income (OCI). OCI includes unrealized gains and losses on certain investments, foreign currency translation, and pension adjustments. Accumulated OCI is reported in equity.

Solution

A change in estimate is prospective. A change in principle is retrospective. An error means the prior statements were wrong.

Solution

Solution

The statement of cash flows

The statement of cash flows explains the change in cash by category and complements the accrual-based income statement.

  • The statement of cash flows reports cash receipts and payments in three categories: operating, investing, and financing. It helps assess the ability to generate future cash flows and pay obligations.
  • Operating: cash from customers, payments to suppliers and employees, interest and taxes. Investing: purchase and sale of long-term assets and investments, lending. Financing: issuing and repaying debt and stock, paying dividends.
  • Indirect method starts with net income: + depreciation and other noncash expenses, + decreases (− increases) in operating assets, + increases (− decreases) in operating liabilities, ± losses (gains) on investing/financing items.
  • Noncash investing and financing activities (for example, acquiring land by issuing a note) are disclosed separately.
  • Under U.S. GAAP, interest paid and received and taxes are operating. Under IFRS, interest and dividends paid can be operating or financing.

Profitability analysis and IFRS

DuPont analysis breaks return on equity into profitability, activity, and leverage.

  • Profit margin = net income ÷ net sales. Asset turnover = net sales ÷ average total assets. ROA = net income ÷ average total assets = profit margin × asset turnover.
  • ROE = net income ÷ average shareholders’ equity. DuPont: ROE = profit margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier, where equity multiplier = average total assets ÷ average equity.
  • Operating cash flow ratio and cash flow from operations to net income compare earnings with cash.
  • IFRS: the income statement may classify expenses by nature or function; extraordinary items are prohibited under both; IFRS allows interest to be classified differently on the cash flow statement.

Wrap-up: Chapter 4 at a glance

  • Income statement: single-step or multiple-step; income from continuing operations is the key measure for predicting future earnings; earnings management reduces earnings quality.
  • Below continuing operations: discontinued operations are reported net of tax. Changes in principle are retrospective; changes in estimate are prospective; errors are restated.
  • EPS uses weighted-average shares and deducts preferred dividends. Comprehensive income = net income + OCI.
  • Cash flows are classified as operating, investing, or financing. The indirect method reconciles net income to operating cash flow.
  • DuPont: ROE = profit margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier.

Initial cost of property, plant, and equipment

Capitalize every cost necessary to get an asset ready for its intended use, and no more.

  • Property, plant, and equipment (PP&E) are tangible, long-lived assets used in operations. Intangible assets lack physical substance. Natural resources are depleted as extracted.
  • Initial cost includes all expenditures necessary to get the asset in condition and location for its intended use: purchase price (net of discounts), sales taxes, freight, insurance in transit, installation, testing, legal fees, and similar costs. Later repairs, fines, and general overhead are expensed.
  • Land includes purchase price, closing costs, grading, demolition of old buildings (net of salvage), and similar. Land is not depreciated. Land improvements (parking lots, fences) have limited lives and are depreciated.
  • Lump-sum purchase: allocate the price to each asset in proportion to relative fair values.
  • Natural resources: include acquisition, exploration, development, and the present value of the asset retirement obligation (ARO).

Example 1 — Capitalize or expense?

Haywood Machining purchases a new machine. Decide how to treat each cost.
Need a hint?
Capitalize costs needed to acquire the asset and make it ready for use. Expense costs that arise after it is in use or that do not add to its value.

Solution

  • a–d. Capitalize. They are necessary to acquire the machine and ready it for use.
  • e. Expense. Fines are not a cost of acquiring the asset.
  • f. Expense. It is a cost after the asset is in use.
  • g. Expense. Ongoing insurance is a period cost.

Noncash acquisitions and exchanges

Assets acquired without a simple cash payment are recorded at fair value or the present value of what is given.

  • Deferred payment (noninterest-bearing note): record the asset at the cash price or the present value of the note at the market rate. The difference between the face amount and the present value is a discount, amortized to interest expense (carrying amount × market rate).
  • Equity securities: record the asset at the fair value of the shares (or of the asset if more clearly determinable). Donated assets: record at fair value and recognize revenue (a contribution).
  • Nonmonetary exchange with commercial substance: record the new asset at the fair value of the asset given (plus cash paid, minus cash received) and recognize the gain or loss (fair value − book value of old asset).
  • Lacks commercial substance: gains are deferred (new asset = book value of old asset + cash paid); losses are recognized immediately.
  • Fixed-asset turnover = net sales ÷ average net fixed assets.

Need a hint?

Solution

Self-constructed assets and capitalized interest

The cost of constructing an asset includes interest that could have been avoided if the expenditures had not been made.

  • Self-constructed assets include direct materials, direct labor, and allocated overhead, plus capitalized interest during construction.
  • Capitalized interest = avoidable interest, limited to actual interest incurred. Avoidable interest = weighted-average accumulated expenditures × interest rate. Use the rate on any specific construction loan first; apply the weighted-average rate on other debt to any excess.
  • Weighted-average accumulated expenditures = each expenditure × the fraction of the year it was outstanding.
  • Interest capitalization stops when the asset is substantially complete and ready for use.

Intangible assets and IFRS

Intangible assets are recorded at cost when purchased; internally developed intangibles are mostly expensed.

  • Purchased intangibles (patents, trademarks, franchises, copyrights, goodwill in a business combination) are capitalized at cost, including legal and registration fees. Internally developed intangibles: costs are generally expensed. Internally generated goodwill is never recorded.
  • Research and development (R&D) costs are expensed as incurred under U.S. GAAP. Legal costs to successfully defend a patent are capitalized.
  • Software: costs of software developed for sale are expensed until technological feasibility, then capitalized. Internal-use software costs are capitalized during the application development stage.
  • IFRS: research costs are expensed, but development costs are capitalized once specific criteria are met. IFRS also permits the revaluation model for PP&E (and some intangibles). U.S. GAAP requires historical cost.

Wrap-up: Chapter 10 at a glance

  • Cost includes all expenditures necessary to get an asset ready for use. Allocate lump-sum purchases by relative fair value.
  • Noncash acquisitions: use the present value of a noninterest-bearing note; fair value of shares or donated assets; fair value in exchanges with commercial substance (recognize gains and losses).
  • Capitalized interest = lesser of avoidable and actual interest, based on weighted-average accumulated expenditures.
  • Intangibles: capitalize purchased intangibles; expense R&D under U.S. GAAP. IFRS capitalizes development costs that meet criteria and permits revaluation.

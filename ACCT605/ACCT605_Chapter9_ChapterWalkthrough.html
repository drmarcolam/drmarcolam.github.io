Lower of cost and net realizable value

Inventory should not be reported above the amount we expect to recover from it.

  • Inventory is reported at the lower of cost and net realizable value (LCNRV) for FIFO and average cost. Net realizable value (NRV) = estimated selling price − estimated costs of completion and disposal.
  • Apply LCNRV to individual items, groups, or the total inventory (the method should be applied consistently). A write-down is recorded as a loss (or in cost of goods sold). The reduced amount becomes the new cost basis; recoveries are not recognized under U.S. GAAP.
  • LIFO and the retail inventory method use lower of cost or market (LCM). Market is the middle value of three amounts: replacement cost, ceiling (NRV), and floor (NRV − normal profit margin). Inventory is reported at the lower of cost and market.

Example 2 — Lower of cost or market (LIFO and retail)

Cashiers Hardware uses LIFO, so it applies lower of cost or market. For each item (per unit):
  • Item X: cost $100; replacement cost $85; NRV $95; normal profit margin $15; 1,000 units
  • Item Y: cost $60; replacement cost $40; NRV $52; normal profit margin $10; 500 units
Need a hint?
Market = median of (replacement cost, ceiling = NRV, floor = NRV − normal profit). Then take the lower of cost and market.

$

Solution

Item X: replacement cost $85; ceiling $95; floor = $95 − $15 = $80. The median is $85. LCM = lower of $100 and $85 = $85. Total: 1,000 × $85 = $85,000.

Item Y: replacement cost $40; ceiling $52; floor = $52 − $10 = $42. The median of $40, $52, $42 is $42. LCM = lower of $60 and $42 = $42. Total: 500 × $42 = $21,000.

Total LCM inventory: $85,000 + $21,000 = $106,000.

Estimating inventory: gross profit and retail methods

When counting is impractical, we estimate ending inventory and cost of goods sold using historical relationships.

  • The gross profit method estimates ending inventory when a count is impractical (fire, theft, interim reports). Estimated COGS = net sales × (1 − gross profit ratio). Estimated ending inventory = goods available − estimated COGS. Convert a markup on cost to a gross profit ratio on sales: GP% = markup ÷ (1 + markup).
  • The retail inventory method uses the relationship of cost to retail. Net markups increase retail; net markdowns decrease it.
  • Cost-to-retail percentage = cost of goods available ÷ retail of goods available. Conventional (lower of average cost or market): include net markups but exclude net markdowns from the denominator. Average cost: include both. FIFO: use current-period purchases only.
  • Ending inventory at retail = goods available at retail (after markups and markdowns) − net sales. Ending inventory at cost = ending retail × cost-to-retail percentage.
  • Dollar-value LIFO retail: deflate ending retail by the price index; any increase is a new layer, converted to cost using the current year’s cost-to-retail percentage.

Example 5 — Markup on cost versus gross profit on sales

Rutherford Rugs prices goods at 25% above cost. Net sales for the period are $500,000. Goods available for sale at cost total $460,000.
Need a hint?
A markup on cost of 25% is a gross profit ratio on sales of 0.25 ÷ 1.25.

Solution

GP% on sales = 0.25 ÷ 1.25 = 20%. COGS = $500,000 × 80% = $400,000 (check: $500,000 ÷ 1.25). Ending inventory = $460,000 − $400,000 = $60,000.

Changes in method, errors, and IFRS

Changes in inventory method and inventory errors affect more than one period.

  • Change in inventory method: generally a change in accounting principle, applied retrospectively (restate prior statements; adjust beginning retained earnings for the cumulative effect, net of tax). A change to LIFO is usually applied prospectively because earlier layers can’t be determined. Disclose the nature and justification.
  • Inventory errors: ending inventory is part of COGS in the current year and beginning inventory the next, so errors counterbalance over two years. An overstated ending inventory overstates income that year and understates it the next; retained earnings are correct at the end of the second year.
  • IFRS: LCNRV applies to all inventory (no LCM). Write-downs can be reversed (up to the original cost) if NRV recovers. U.S. GAAP prohibits reversal.

Example 9 — Inventory errors

Franklin Frames’ Year 1 ending inventory was overstated by $30,000. The error was not discovered until Year 3. Ignore income taxes.
Need a hint?
Ending inventory in Year 1 is beginning inventory in Year 2. Higher ending inventory means lower COGS and higher income.
$
$

Solution

  • Year 1: ending inventory ↑ $30,000 → COGS understated, net income overstated by $30,000.
  • Year 2: beginning inventory ↑ $30,000 → COGS overstated, net income understated by $30,000.
  • Retained earnings at the end of Year 2 are correct because the errors offset. Because the statements for Year 1 and 2 are wrong, prior statements shown for comparison are restated.

Wrap-up: Chapter 9 at a glance

  • LCNRV applies to FIFO and average cost; LCM applies to LIFO and retail. The write-down creates a new cost basis.
  • Gross profit method: estimated COGS = sales × (1 − GP%); estimated ending inventory = goods available − COGS.
  • Retail method: cost-to-retail percentage × ending inventory at retail. Conventional excludes markdowns from the denominator; FIFO uses only current purchases.
  • Method changes are generally retrospective; inventory errors counterbalance after two years.
  • IFRS allows reversal of inventory write-downs; U.S. GAAP does not.

Problem

Blue Ridge Courier Company

Blue Ridge Courier Company (BRC), an Asheville-based parcel delivery service, is considering outsourcing its Payroll Department to a payroll processing company for an annual fee of $220,400. An internally prepared report summarizes the Payroll Department's annual operating costs as follows.

Given data — current Payroll Department annual operating costs

ItemAmount
Supplies$30,400
Payroll clerks' salaries$120,400
Payroll supervisor's salary$58,400
Payroll employee training expenses$10,400
Depreciation of equipment$20,400
Allocated share of common building operating costs$15,400
Allocated share of common administrative overhead$28,400
Total annual operating cost$283,800

BRC currently rents overflow office space for $36,400 per year. If the Payroll Department is closed, the employees occupying the rented space could be brought in-house and the lease terminated with no penalty.

If the Payroll Department is outsourced, the payroll clerks will not be retained; however, the payroll supervisor would be transferred to BRC's Human Resource Management Department. As a result, BRC would discontinue its search for a new Human Resource Manager, for whom it expected to pay an annual salary of $56,400.

The Payroll Department's equipment would be transferred to other departments within the company to replace outdated equipment that would be recycled for zero salvage value.

Required:
  1. What is the financial advantage (disadvantage) of outsourcing the Payroll Department?

Financial advantage (disadvantage) analysis

Only costs and savings that actually change if the Payroll Department is outsourced are relevant. Depreciation, the allocated building costs, and the allocated administrative overhead do not change and are not part of the analysis; the payroll supervisor's own salary continues to be paid (just in a different department), but the salary BRC avoids paying to a new Human Resource Manager is a relevant savings.

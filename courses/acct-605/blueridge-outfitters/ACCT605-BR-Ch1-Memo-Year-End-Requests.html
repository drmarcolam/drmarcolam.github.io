The Year-End Requests

The CFO wants two year-end entries handled her way before the bank tests the loan covenant. Advise the controller: what do the facts support, and what should he do?

The situation

Blue Ridge Outfitters, Asheville, NC. Outdoor gear retailer with four stores. You are on the accounting staff; your reader is Luis Caldwell, Controller.

It is January 6. Mountain Credit Bank (MCB) will test the loan covenant on the audited December 31 statements, and the audit starts next week. Dana Whitfield, the CFO, has given Luis Caldwell, the controller, two instructions about the year-end books. Luis is uneasy about both. He asks you, a staff accountant, for a memo he can use before he answers her.

Where Blue Ridge stands

ItemAmount
Total liabilities$2,900,000
Total equity$1,500,000
Total liabilities divided by total equity1.93
Bank covenant limit (must be 2.00 or lower)2.00

These are the amounts before the events described below are recorded. Ignore income taxes. Round ratios to two decimals. Use only the facts in your own version: the other version differs on purpose, so another group may reach a different, equally well-supported conclusion.

The facts

Dana's two instructions

Instruction 1. “Do not record a loss on the Smoky Ridge Guides receivable. The court will not rule until March and a lot can change. We can deal with it next year.”

Instruction 2. “Record the Pisgah Trail Co. sale in December. They ordered it in December, so December is the right month.”

Neither instruction has been carried out yet. The books show the standing position above. Pisgah Trail Co.'s invoice is for $120,000; the goods cost Blue Ridge $72,000. Ignore sales tax.

Your version: A

Smoky Ridge Guides. Smoky Ridge owes Blue Ridge $150,000 for gear delivered in September. It filed for bankruptcy protection on December 18. Its attorney's letter, dated December 29, says unsecured creditors should expect to receive nothing.

Pisgah Trail Co. The goods left the Asheville warehouse on January 3. The terms are FOB shipping point, and a Pisgah Trail driver picked the goods up on January 3.

Smoky Ridge Guides. Smoky Ridge owes Blue Ridge $60,000 for gear delivered in September. It filed for bankruptcy protection on December 18. Its attorney's letter, dated December 29, says unsecured creditors should expect to receive about 25 cents on the dollar.

Pisgah Trail Co. The goods were delivered to Pisgah Trail's store and signed for on December 30. The terms are FOB destination. The invoice is dated January 2 only because the billing system was closed on December 31.

Your version is set by your Student ID. Do not use facts from the other version.

Questions to answer

  1. For each instruction, what is the accounting question, and what does GAAP require on the facts in your version? Which facts decide it?
  2. Which qualities of useful financial information (Concepts Statement No. 8) would Luis compromise by following each instruction, and which would he serve?
  3. Compute total liabilities divided by total equity (a) if Luis follows both instructions and (b) if the books follow GAAP on your facts. Does the covenant hold in (b)?
  4. What should Luis do? Consider his professional responsibilities, who relies on the statements, his alternatives (including a conversation with MCB), and how he should escalate if Dana insists.

What to submit (two-page memo)

A memo of about two pages (one file, Word or PDF). Use the headings To, From, Date, Re, then: Summary of your recommendation (3 to 4 sentences), Analysis (one short section per question), Calculations (a table), and Recommendation and next steps. Write for a reader who knows accounting but has not seen your work.

  • Cite the authority you rely on (textbook section or Codification reference) next to each conclusion.
  • Show your arithmetic in a table with a header row; do not paste a picture of a spreadsheet.
  • Use heading styles, real tables, and plain language so the memo is readable with a screen reader. Add alt text to any picture.

Authority to start with

  • Textbook Chapter 1: the conceptual framework, qualitative characteristics, and the discussion of ethics in accounting.
  • FASB Concepts Statement No. 8, Chapter 3 (relevance, faithful representation: complete, neutral, free from error).
  • ASC 606-10-25-23 and 25-30 (when control transfers) and Chapter 6 for the sale; ASC 326-20 and Chapter 7 for the receivable.
  • ASC 855-10 (subsequent events): is a December 18 bankruptcy a subsequent event at all?
  • IMA Statement of Ethical Professional Practice and the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct (integrity, objectivity, resolving ethical conflict).

Check paragraph numbers against the FASB Codification yourself before you cite them.

The Fleet, the Customer List and the Trademark

Change a fleet’s useful life, amortize a customer list, and test two intangibles for impairment: what does it do to income and the covenant?

The situation

Blue Ridge Outfitters, Asheville, NC. Outdoor gear retailer with four stores. You are on the accounting staff; your reader is The Board.

Three year-end items need a decision before the board meets: the delivery fleet's useful life, the customer list Blue Ridge bought from a competitor, and the Blue Ridge Trail Co. trademark. Your group prepares a five-slide briefing for the board and records a short presentation. Ignore income taxes.

The facts

Facts for every group

  • Delivery vans. Blue Ridge owns 8 vans that cost $480,000 in total, bought four years ago on January 1 and depreciated straight-line over 8 years with no salvage value. Tom Hensley now believes the vans will last 12 years in total (no salvage value). The staff has already recorded this year's depreciation at the old rate; it should be corrected.
  • Customer list. On January 1 of this year, Blue Ridge paid $240,000 for the customer list of River Valley Outfitters. The list is expected to produce benefits for 6 years. No amortization has been recorded yet.
  • Trademark. Blue Ridge carries the Blue Ridge Trail Co. trademark at $300,000. It can be renewed at little cost and has no foreseeable end to its useful life. Its carrying amount is unchanged since it was acquired.
  • The standing position above is the books before any of these corrections or entries.
Your version: A

Year-end tests. The trademark's fair value at December 31 is $270,000. The customer list is expected to generate $260,000 of undiscounted net cash inflows over its remaining life.

Year-end tests. The trademark's fair value at December 31 is $320,000. Several of the list's biggest customers have gone elsewhere: the list is expected to generate only $170,000 of undiscounted net cash inflows over its remaining life, and its fair value is $150,000.

Your version is set by your Student ID. Do not use facts from the other version.

Questions to answer

  1. Change in estimate: compute the book value of the vans at the start of this year, the new annual depreciation, and the correction to this year's depreciation. Is the change applied retroactively or prospectively?
  2. Customer list: is it finite-lived or indefinite-lived? Compute this year's amortization and the carrying amount at December 31 before any impairment test.
  3. Impairment: what test applies to each asset (the list and the trademark), and what does it show on your facts? Compute any impairment loss.
  4. Compute the total effect on net income and equity, and liabilities divided by equity. Does the covenant hold?
  5. What disclosures should accompany these items?

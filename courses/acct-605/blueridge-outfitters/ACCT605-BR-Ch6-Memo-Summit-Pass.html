The Summit Pass

A backpacking bundle with a tent, a sleeping bag, a year of gear care and loyalty points. How much of the $120,000 is December revenue?

The situation

Blue Ridge Outfitters, Asheville, NC. Outdoor gear retailer with four stores. You are on the accounting staff; your reader is Luis Caldwell, Controller.

In December, Maya Ledford launched the Summit Pass, a backpacking bundle sold online and in the stores. Blue Ridge sold 100 passes on December 1 for $1,200 each, $120,000 in all, collected in cash. The accounting staff recorded the entire $120,000 as sales revenue in December. Luis Caldwell is not sure that is right and asks you for a memo before the audit starts. Ignore the cost of goods sold, sales tax and income taxes.

The facts

What a Summit Pass includes

ComponentStand-alone selling priceNotes
Trailhead 2-person tent$700Delivered to the customer December 1
3-season sleeping bag$500Delivered to the customer December 1
Gear Care plan$300Repairs and seasonal tune-ups for 12 months, starting December 1
Loyalty points$50 face value × 80% expected redemption = $40Each pass earns points redeemable for $50 off a future purchase. Other customers do not receive this discount. No points were redeemed by December 31.

Blue Ridge sells the tent, the bag and the Gear Care plan separately at the prices shown. The plan's services are provided evenly over the twelve months.

The books before any correction show total liabilities of $2,900,000 and total equity of $1,500,000, including the $120,000 recorded as revenue.

Your version: A

Returns. Passes are final sale. Blue Ridge offers no right of return on the Summit Pass.

Returns. A customer may return a Summit Pass within 30 days for a full refund. Based on its experience with similar bundles, Blue Ridge expects 12% of passes to be returned. No pass has been returned as of December 31. Assume returned items could be resold at full cost, so ignore the cost of returned goods.

Your version is set by your Student ID. Do not use facts from the other version.

Questions to answer

  1. Identify the performance obligations in a Summit Pass. Which components are distinct, and are the loyalty points a separate obligation? Explain with the contract facts.
  2. Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations on a relative stand-alone selling price basis. Show the allocation for one pass and for all passes.
  3. How much revenue should Blue Ridge recognize in December, and what remains as a liability (and what kind) at December 31? Prepare the December 1 entry and the December 31 adjusting entry.
  4. Compute total liabilities divided by total equity after your correction and compare it with the covenant limit of 2.00.
  5. What should Luis tell Maya, who would like to keep all $120,000 as December revenue?

What to submit (two-page memo)

A memo of about two pages (one file, Word or PDF). Use the headings To, From, Date, Re, then: Summary of your recommendation (3 to 4 sentences), Analysis (one short section per question), Calculations (a table), and Recommendation and next steps. Write for a reader who knows accounting but has not seen your work.

  • Cite the authority you rely on (textbook section or Codification reference) next to each conclusion.
  • Show your arithmetic in a table with a header row; do not paste a picture of a spreadsheet.
  • Use heading styles, real tables, and plain language so the memo is readable with a screen reader. Add alt text to any picture.

Authority to start with

  • Textbook Chapter 6: the five-step revenue model, performance obligations, allocation, right of return, and options for additional goods.
  • ASC 606-10-25-14 through 25-22 (identifying performance obligations).
  • ASC 606-10-32-28 through 32-35 (allocating the transaction price) and 606-10-32-5 through 32-13 (variable consideration).
  • ASC 606-10-55-22 through 55-29 (rights of return) and 606-10-55-41 through 55-45 (customer options for additional goods; material rights).

Check paragraph numbers against the FASB Codification yourself before you cite them.

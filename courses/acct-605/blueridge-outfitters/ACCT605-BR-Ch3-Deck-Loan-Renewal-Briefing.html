The Loan Renewal Briefing

Mountain Credit Bank is reviewing Blue Ridge’s renewal. Classify the debt, handle a lawsuit and a warehouse fire, and brief the loan officer in five slides and one video.

The situation

Blue Ridge Outfitters, Asheville, NC. Outdoor gear retailer with four stores. You are on the accounting staff; your reader is Carolyn Reese, MCB.

Blue Ridge Outfitters is renewing its loan with Mountain Credit Bank. Carolyn Reese, a commercial loan officer at MCB, has asked for the December 31 balance sheet, a short explanation of how the debt is classified, and the note disclosures. Dana Whitfield wants the briefing to be clear and honest. Your group prepares the five-slide briefing and a short recorded presentation for Carolyn.

Facts for every group

  • Warehouse fire. On January 9, a fire at the Hendersonville warehouse destroyed about $240,000 of inventory. Insurance is expected to cover most of it. The financial statements will be issued on February 10.
Your version: A

The note due March 15. On January 22, Blue Ridge signed a three-year term agreement with MCB that refinances the $600,000 note. The agreement is not cancelable, no covenant is violated at the date of signing, and MCB is financially able to lend.

The customer lawsuit. A customer injured in the Boone store has sued. Blue Ridge's attorney says a loss is reasonably possible, in a range of $80,000 to $150,000, but not probable.

The note due March 15. Dana says MCB will “probably roll it over.” An MCB officer has said so verbally, and a term sheet exists, but nothing has been signed and no refinancing has taken place.

The customer lawsuit. A customer injured in the Boone store has sued. Blue Ridge's attorney says a loss is probable, in a range of $80,000 to $150,000, with no amount in the range more likely than any other.

Your version is set by your Student ID. Do not use facts from the other version.

Questions to answer

  1. Classify every liability as current or noncurrent on your facts, and give the reason for the two judgment calls (the note due March 15 and the lawsuit).
  2. Prepare the classified balance sheet totals: total current assets, total current liabilities, working capital, and total liabilities.
  3. Compute the current ratio and total liabilities divided by total equity. Does the covenant hold on your facts?
  4. Draft the disclosures MCB should see: the debt (including the note due March 15), the lawsuit, and the warehouse fire. Say whether each is recognized, disclosed only, or both.
  5. What should Blue Ridge tell Carolyn, and what should it ask of her?

Authority to start with

  • Textbook Chapter 3: classification of assets and liabilities, notes to the financial statements, subsequent events, and ratios.
  • ASC 210-10-45 (current assets and current liabilities).
  • ASC 470-10-45-14 (short-term obligations expected to be refinanced) and 470-10-45-11 (covenant violations).
  • ASC 450-20-25-2, 450-20-30-1 and 450-20-50 (loss contingencies: accrual, the low end of a range, disclosure).
  • ASC 855-10-25-3 and 855-10-50-2 (nonrecognized subsequent events).

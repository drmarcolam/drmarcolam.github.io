Core Concept: Fund financial statements provide the operational starting point for government-wide reporting, but governmental funds must be converted from the current financial resources focus and modified accrual basis to the economic resources measurement focus and full accrual basis of accounting. Government-wide statements present the financial position and operational activities of the government as a whole, split into Governmental Activities and Business-Type Activities, while completely excluding Fiduciary Funds.

Learning Objectives

  • LO 1: Differences in Accounting Measurement and Format: Describe the major differences in accounting measurements and format between government-wide and fund financial statements.
  • LO 2: Interfund and Internal Service Fund Reporting: Describe how interfund and Internal Service Fund (ISF) balances and activity are reported in government-wide financial statements.
  • LO 3: Capital Asset and Debt Conversion Adjustments: Convert fund statements to government-wide statements: capital asset and long-term debt adjustments.
  • LO 4: Revenue Conversion Adjustments: Convert fund statements to government-wide statements: revenue adjustments.
  • LO 5: Expense Conversion Adjustments: Convert fund statements to government-wide statements: expense adjustments.
  • LO 6: Government-Wide Statement Preparation and Reconciliation: Prepare government-wide statements and reconcile them with the fund financial statements.
  • LO 7: Infrastructure Asset Accounting Standards: Describe the accounting standards for special treatment of certain infrastructure assets, including the modified approach.

1. Government-Wide vs. Fund Financial Statements (LO 1)

Government-wide financial statements focus on the government as a whole, providing a long-term economic perspective. Unlike governmental fund statements—which use the current financial resources measurement focus and modified accrual basis—government-wide statements are prepared using the economic resources measurement focus and full accrual basis of accounting.

Statement Structure and Columns

The Government-Wide Statement of Net Position and Statement of Activities feature four main columns:

  • Governmental Activities: Consolidates all governmental funds plus Internal Service Funds that primarily serve governmental functions.
  • Business-Type Activities: Consolidates enterprise funds.
  • Total: Combines Governmental Activities and Business-Type Activities for the primary government.
  • Discretely Presented Component Units: Reports major legally separate component units in a separate column to preserve primary government readability.

Net Position Components: Net Position replaces Fund Balance and is presented in three categories: (1) Net Investment in Capital Assets (Capital Assets minus Accumulated Depreciation minus Outstanding Capital-Related Debt), (2) Restricted Net Position (constraints imposed externally by creditors/grantors or enabling legislation; cannot be negative), and (3) Unrestricted Net Position (residual balance).

Statement of Activities Format: Reports gross expenses by function or program, subtracts program revenues (Charges for Services, Operating Grants & Contributions, Capital Grants & Contributions) to derive Net Program Expense (Revenue). General revenues (all taxes, interest income, gains) are reported at the bottom to cover net program expenses.

2. Interfund & Internal Service Fund Reporting (LO 2)

To present a true picture of the government as a single economic entity, internal double-counting must be eliminated:

  • Interfund Receivables and Payables: Receivables/payables between individual governmental funds are eliminated. Receivables/payables between enterprise funds are eliminated. Any net balance between governmental funds and enterprise funds is reported as 'Internal Balances' on the Statement of Net Position (offsetting to zero in the Total column).
  • Interfund Transfers: Transfers between governmental funds and transfers between enterprise funds are eliminated. Transfers between governmental and enterprise activities are reported as 'Transfers—Internal Activities' on the Statement of Activities (offsetting to zero in the Total column).
  • Internal Service Funds (ISF): Because ISFs primarily serve governmental departments (e.g., central motor pool), ISF assets and liabilities are aggregated into Governmental Activities. ISF revenues and expenses are eliminated against the purchasing departments' expenditures to prevent doubling revenues/expenses. Any ISF net profit or loss is allocated back to reduce or increase the expenditures of the customer functions (e.g., General Government, Public Works).

3. Capital Asset & Long-Term Debt Adjustments (LO 3)

Governmental funds do not record capital assets or long-term liabilities on their balance sheets. Worksheet adjusting entries are required to bring these balances onto the government-wide statements.

Capital Asset Conversion Journal Entries

1. Record Beginning Capital Assets & Accumulated Depreciation:

Debit: Capital Assets (beginning balance)
Credit: Accumulated Depreciation (beginning balance)
Credit: Net Position (beginning balance equity adjustment)

2. Reclassify Current-Year Capital Outlay Expenditures:

Debit: Capital Assets (current-year acquisitions)
Credit: Expenditures—Capital Outlay (eliminates fund expenditure)

3. Record Current-Year Depreciation Expense:

Debit: Depreciation Expense
Credit: Accumulated Depreciation

Long-Term Debt Conversion Journal Entries

1. Record Beginning Long-Term Bonds Payable:

Debit: Net Position (beginning balance equity adjustment)
Credit: Bonds Payable (beginning balance)

2. Reclassify Debt Issuance Proceeds:

Debit: Other Financing Sources—Long-Term Debt Issued
Credit: Bonds Payable (records liability)

3. Reclassify Debt Principal Repayments:

Debit: Bonds Payable (reduces liability)
Credit: Expenditures—Bond Principal (eliminates fund expenditure)

4. Revenue Conversion Adjustments (LO 4)

Under modified accrual accounting, revenues are recognized only when they are measurable and available (collected within 60 days of year-end for property taxes). Under full accrual accounting, the 'availability' criterion is eliminated, requiring adjustments for deferred inflows:

  • Prior-Year Deferred Property Taxes Collected in Current Year: In governmental funds, prior-year deferred property taxes collected this year are credited to current-year property tax revenue. Under full accrual, this revenue belonged to the prior year. Adjustment: Debit Revenues—Property Taxes, Credit Net Position.
  • Current-Year Deferred Property Taxes (Unavailable): Under full accrual, property taxes levied for the current year are recognized as revenue regardless of when collected. Adjustment: Debit Deferred Inflows of Resources—Unavailable Property Taxes, Credit Revenues—Property Taxes.
  • Sales, Income Taxes, and Grants: Accrual basis adjustments are applied similarly. For expenditure-driven grants, revenue is recognized when allowable expenditures are incurred, so fund and government-wide revenue timing typically align.

5. Expense Conversion Adjustments (LO 5)

Expenses incurred but not due and payable are not recognized in governmental funds under modified accrual. Full accrual accounting requires accruing all incurred expenses:

  • Stub Period Interest on Long-Term Debt: Interest between the last payment date and fiscal year-end is accrued. Adjustment: Debit Net Position (for prior-year stub interest), Debit/Credit Interest Expense (for change in stub interest), Credit Accrued Interest Payable.
  • Expenses Incurred But Not Currently Due: Compensated absences, claims and judgments, landfill closure/postclosure costs, and pension/OPEB liabilities are accrued when incurred. Adjustment example for compensated absences: Debit Net Position (prior long-term liability), Debit Compensated Absences Expense (current year increase), Credit Compensated Absences Payable (ending long-term liability balance).
Summary: Five Basic Types of Conversion Adjustments
1. Capital Asset Adjustments (record capital assets, depreciation, outlays, asset sales)
2. Long-Term Debt Adjustments (record bonds payable, proceeds, principal repayments)
3. Revenue Adjustments (eliminate availability criterion, adjust deferred inflows)
4. Expense Adjustments (accrue stub interest, compensated absences, pension/OPEB liabilities)
5. Interfund Eliminations (eliminate interfund receivables/payables, transfers, and ISF profit/activity)

6. Statement Preparation & Reconciliations (LO 6)

To prepare government-wide statements, accountants use a six-column worksheet: Columns 1-2 aggregate preclosing trial balances of all governmental funds; Columns 3-4 record conversion adjustments (debits/credits); Columns 5-6 reflect adjusted balances for the Governmental Activities column.

Required Reconciliations: GASB requires two formal reconciliations presented either at the bottom of fund financial statements or in an accompanying schedule:

  • 1. Balance Sheet to Statement of Net Position Reconciliation: Reconciles Total Governmental Fund Balances to Net Position of Governmental Activities. Key reconciling items: + Capital assets (net of depreciation), + Accrual-basis revenue receivables (unavailable in funds), - Outstanding long-term bonds payable, - Accrued long-term liabilities (interest payable, compensated absences, pension/OPEB).
  • 2. Operating Statement to Statement of Activities Reconciliation: Reconciles Net Change in Governmental Fund Balances to Change in Net Position of Governmental Activities. Key reconciling items: + Capital outlay expenditures minus current depreciation expense, - Bond proceeds issued + Principal repayments made, + Reversal of deferred inflows to reflect accrual revenues, - Net changes in accrued long-term liabilities/expenses.

7. Infrastructure Assets & The Modified Approach (LO 7)

Infrastructure assets are long-lived, stationary capital assets preserved for significantly greater years than most capital assets (e.g., roads, bridges, tunnels, drainage systems, water/sewer networks, dams, lighting systems). Buildings are excluded unless they are an ancillary part of an infrastructure network.

Two Reporting Options for Infrastructure

  • Traditional Approach: Capitalize infrastructure at acquisition cost and depreciate over estimated useful life.
  • Modified Approach: Elect NOT to depreciate eligible infrastructure assets, provided two strict requirements are met:

Asset Management System: Maintain an inventory of eligible infrastructure assets, perform complete condition assessments at least once every three years, and estimate annual amounts required to maintain assets at an established condition level.

Documented Preservation Level: Provide documentation that infrastructure assets are being preserved at or above the condition level established and disclosed by the government.

Financial Reporting Consequences of Modified Approach

  • Depreciation: Infrastructure assets are NOT depreciated.
  • Preservation Expenditures: All routine maintenance and preservation expenditures are expensed in the year incurred.
  • Capitalization: Only additions or improvements that increase capacity or efficiency are capitalized.
  • Required Supplementary Information (RSI): Disclose condition rating results for the past 3 assessments and a 5-year comparison of estimated versus actual maintenance costs.

7. How are (a) interfund receivables and payables between two governmental funds, (b) a transfer from the General Fund to an Enterprise Fund, and (c) an Internal Service Fund's assets and liabilities handled in the government-wide statements?

Show answer
  • (a) Eliminated.
  • (b) Reported as Transfers—Internal Activities, offsetting to zero in the total column.
  • (c) Included in governmental activities, with ISF revenues and expenses eliminated against the departments' charges and any ISF profit or loss allocated back to the customer functions.

8. What two conditions must be met to use the modified approach for infrastructure, and what changes in the financial statements?

Show answer
  • The government must keep an asset management system (inventory, condition assessments at least every three years, estimated annual maintenance needed) and document that assets are preserved at or above the stated condition level.
  • Then eligible infrastructure is not depreciated, maintenance is expensed as incurred, and only additions or improvements that increase capacity or efficiency are capitalized. Required supplementary information discloses condition results and estimated versus actual maintenance.

1. Core Learning Objectives

Chapter Key Learning Outcomes & Mastery Goals:
▪ Distinguish General vs. Special Revenue Funds: Explain the operational scope of the General Fund versus the legally restricted resource constraints of Special Revenue Funds.
▪ Execute Short-Term Debt & TAN Transactions: Record tax anticipation note (TAN) borrowings, short-term liabilities, and interest expenditures under the modified accrual basis.
▪ Manage the Full Budgetary & Encumbrance Cycle: Demonstrate legal budget recording, purchase order commitments, actual expenditure processing, and year-end encumbrance reversals vs. carryovers.

▪ Prepare Governmental Financial Statements & Year-End Closing: Construct the Balance Sheet, Statement of Revenues, Expenditures, and Changes in Fund Balances, and perform closing entries for budgetary and actual accounts.

2. Nature of General & Special Revenue Funds

The General Fund and Special Revenue Funds serve as the primary operating funds for state and local government entities. Both funds utilize the modified accrual basis of accounting and focus on measuring current financial resources and inflows/outflows of spendable resources.

General Fund (GF)Accounts for all financial resources except those required to be accounted for in another fund. Supports basic day-to-day government operations.• Exactly ONE General Fund per government.

★ EXAM CORE PRINCIPLES: FUND STRUCTURE & MODIFIED ACCRUAL

▪ General Fund Cardinal Rule: A government can have ONLY ONE General Fund, but it may have zero, one, or multiple Special Revenue Funds.
▪ Revenue Recognition Criteria: Under modified accrual, revenue is recognized only when it is both MEASURABLE (dollar amount can be reasonably estimated) and AVAILABLE (collectible in current period or within 60 days after year-end to pay current bills).
▪ Expenditure Recognition Criteria: Expenditures are recognized when the related fund liability is incurred (claims against current financial resources).

3. Short-Term Financing & Anticipation Notes

Governmental cash inflows (e.g., annual property tax payments) are frequently lumpy, whereas cash outflows for salaries and operations occur evenly throughout the fiscal year. To bridge temporary cash flow shortages, governments issue short-term Tax Anticipation Notes (TANs), Revenue Anticipation Notes (RANs), or Bond Anticipation Notes (BANs).

4. The Budgetary & Encumbrance Accounting Cycle

Budgetary accounting integrates approved legal budgets directly into general ledger accounts. Encumbrance accounting prevents overspending by recording purchase commitments before actual cash disbursements occur.

Key Budgetary Accounts & Normal Balances

  • Estimated Revenues (Debit Normal Balance): Anticipated resource inflows for the fiscal year.
  • Appropriations (Credit Normal Balance): Legally authorized spending limits established by the legislative body.
  • Encumbrances (Debit Normal Balance): Purchase orders placed or contracts signed prior to receipt of goods/services.
  • Budgetary Fund Balance Reserved for Encumbrances (Credit Normal Balance): Offset account establishing commitment reservation.
  • Budgetary Fund Balance (Debit or Credit Plug): Equalizing debit/credit entry representing anticipated budget surplus (credit) or deficit (debit).

★ EXAM CRITICAL TRAP: ENCUMBRANCE VS EXPENDITURE REVERSAL

▪ Encumbrance Reversal Rule: When goods are received, ALWAYS reverse the encumbrance at the EXACT ORIGINAL encumbered amount ($20,000), even if the actual vendor invoice differs ($21,000). The actual invoice amount is recorded separately as Expenditures.
▪ Available Appropriation Formula: Available Appropriation = Total Budgeted Appropriation - Outstanding Encumbrances - Actual Year-to-Date Expenditures.

6. Governmental Financial Statements & Budgetary Comparison Schedule

Financial statements for General and Special Revenue Funds are prepared directly from preclosing trial balances. GASB requires two primary fund financial statements plus a Budgetary Comparison Schedule:

1. Balance Sheet: Reports current financial assets (Cash, Property Taxes Receivable), current liabilities (Accrued Salaries Payable), and classified Fund Balances (Nonspendable, Restricted, Committed, Assigned, Unassigned).

2. Statement of Revenues, Expenditures, and Changes in Fund Balance: Reports financial inflows (Revenues) and outflows (Expenditures). The bottom-line formula is:
Excess (Deficiency) of Revenues Over Expenditures + Beginning Fund Balance = Ending Fund Balance.

3. Budgetary Comparison Schedule: Required supplementary information (RSI) for the General Fund and major Special Revenue Funds. Features two mandatory variance columns:
• Column A: Variance with Original Budget — Over (Under) Final Budget
• Column B: Variance with Final Budget — Over (Under) Actual Amounts

7. Year-End Closing Journal Entries

At the end of the fiscal year, temporary accounts are closed. In governmental fund accounting, closing requires TWO DISTINCT JOURNAL ENTRIES:
1. Closing Budgetary Accounts (closing Estimated Revenues, Appropriations, and Budgetary FB against each other).
2. Closing Nominal Financial Accounts (closing actual Revenues and Expenditures into Unassigned Fund Balance).

★ EXAM WARNING: CLOSING ENTRY SEPARATION

▪ Two Closing Entries Mandatory: NEVER combine budgetary accounts (Estimated Revenues, Appropriations) with financial accounts (Revenues, Expenditures) in a single closing entry. Keep them strictly separated!
▪ Fund Balance vs Budgetary Fund Balance: Financial accounts close into actual 'Fund Balance' (an equity asset/liability balance sheet account). Budgetary accounts close into 'Budgetary Fund Balance' (reversing the opening budget entry).

8. Chapter 4 Key Terms & Exam Formula Summary

  • ✔ Modified Accrual Focus: Measures current financial resources (near-term cash flows). Long-term assets/liabilities are omitted from GF/SRF fund balance sheets.
  • ✔ Property Tax Accrual: Property taxes receivable recorded at levy date. Revenue recognized if collected within fiscal year or within 60 days post year-end.
  • ✔ Short-Term Borrowing (TANs/RANs): Recorded as a current liability ('Tax Anticipation Notes Payable') inside the fund, NOT as Other Financing Sources.
  • ✔ Encumbrance Reversal: Always reverse Encumbrance for the original encumbered amount. Record actual cost under Expenditures.
  • ✔ Budgetary Comparison Columns: Must include Original Budget, Final Budget, Actual Amounts, and TWO variance columns (Original vs Final, Final vs Actual).

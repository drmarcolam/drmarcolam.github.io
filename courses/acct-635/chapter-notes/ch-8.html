Key Course Takeaway: Fiduciary funds account for assets held by a government in a trustee or agency capacity for individuals, private organizations, or other governments. Fiduciary activities use the economic resources measurement focus and full accrual basis of accounting. Their resources cannot be used to support the government's own programs.

Learning Objectives

  • LO 1: Employer Government Pension and OPEB Accounting: Understand and apply pension and Other Postemployment Benefits (OPEB) accounting principles for government employers.
  • LO 2: Pension Trust Funds: Account for Pension Trust Fund transactions and prepare required fiduciary financial statements and supplementary schedules.
  • LO 3: Investment Trust Funds & Private-Purpose Trust Funds: Account for Investment Trust Fund and Private-Purpose Trust Fund transactions and prepare financial statements.
  • LO 4: Custodial Funds: Account for Custodial Fund transactions, including tax clearing mechanisms, and prepare financial statements.

1. Employer Pension and OPEB Accounting (LO 1)

Governmental employers provide two main categories of postemployment benefits to employees: pensions and Other Postemployment Benefits (OPEB) (such as retiree health care, life, dental, vision, hearing, and long-term care insurance).

Defined Benefit vs. Defined Contribution Plans

• Defined Benefit (DB) Plans: The employer promises a specified future benefit over retirement. The typical retiree benefit formula is:
Annual Pension Benefit = Final Average Salary × Years of Service × Benefit Multiplier %
The employer bears the investment, longevity, and inflation risks.

• Defined Contribution (DC) Plans: The employer contributes a set percentage of salary into individual employee accounts. The employee bears investment risks. Accounting is simpler: employers record an expense for agreed contributions, and a liability only if contributions remain unpaid at year-end.

Qualifying Trust Criteria for Net Liability Reporting

To report a Net Pension/OPEB Liability on the employer's financial statements, contributions must be held in a trust meeting three strict GASB characteristics:

  1. Contributions from the employer to the trust are irrevocable.
  2. Plan assets are dedicated exclusively to providing pension or OPEB benefits to retirees.
  3. Plan assets are legally protected from creditors of the employer, plan administrator, and participating employees.

Net Pension/OPEB Liability = Total Pension/OPEB Liability − Plan's Fiduciary Net Position
If a qualifying trust does not exist, the government must report the full Total Pension/OPEB Liability.

Deferred Outflows and Inflows of Resources

Actuarial changes and differences are reported as deferred inflows/outflows and amortized over specific periods:
• Experience Differences & Assumption Changes: Amortized over the average expected remaining service lives of active/inactive employees.
• Investment Return Differences: Differences between projected and actual investment earnings are amortized over exactly 5 years.

Fiduciary Financial Statements & Required Supplementary Information (RSI)

  1. Statement of Fiduciary Net Position: Reports assets, liabilities, and Net Position Restricted for Pensions.
  2. Statement of Changes in Fiduciary Net Position: Reports Additions (contributions, investment income) and Deductions (benefits, administrative costs, depreciation).
  3. Required Supplementary Schedules (10-Year Trend): Schedule of Changes in Net Pension Liability, Schedule of Contributions, and Schedule of Investment Returns.

3. Investment Trust Funds & Private-Purpose Trust Funds (LO 3)

• Investment Trust Funds: Used to report the external portion of investment pools held in qualifying trusts for other legally separate governments to achieve economies of scale. (The sponsoring government's own share is reported in its own operating funds).

• Private-Purpose Trust Funds: Used for trust structures where principal and/or earnings benefit specific individuals, private organizations, or other governments outside the primary reporting entity (e.g., escheat property, scholarship funds for citizens).

4. Custodial Funds (LO 4)

Custodial Funds account for fiduciary activities not administered through a qualifying trust agreement. They are commonly used as tax collection clearing mechanisms where a county or city collects property taxes on behalf of other external jurisdictions (school districts, levee districts).

CRITICAL EXAM RULE FOR CUSTODIAL FUNDS:
A government's OWN portion of collected taxes must be eliminated from the Custodial Fund financial statements! For example, when a city collects taxes for itself, a School Board, and a Levee District, the city's portion is transferred to its General Fund. Only property taxes receivable and net position held for OUTSIDE governments are reported on the Fiduciary Statement of Net Position.

SUMMARY CHEAT SHEET FOR FIDUCIARY FUNDS:
• Measurement Focus: Economic Resources Focus
• Basis of Accounting: Full Accrual Basis
• Terminology: 'Additions' (Revenues) & 'Deductions' (Expenses)
• Equity Account: Net Position Restricted for [Purpose]
• Primary Financial Statements: Statement of Fiduciary Net Position & Statement of Changes in Fiduciary Net Position

Practice Exercises

Do the pension trust entries and the tax clearing entries from scratch. Try each one first, then tap Show answer. The numbers are made up for practice.

1. A defined benefit plan pays final average salary × years of service × 2%. A retiree with a final average salary of $80,000 has 30 years of service. What is the annual benefit, and who bears the investment risk?

Show answer

$80,000 × 30 × 2% = $48,000 per year. The employer bears investment, longevity and inflation risk. In a defined contribution plan the employee bears it.

2. A plan has a total pension liability of $12,500,000 and fiduciary net position of $9,800,000. What does the employer report if (a) the plan is in a qualifying trust; (b) it is not?

Show answer
  • (a) Net pension liability = $12,500,000 − $9,800,000 = $2,700,000.
  • (b) Without a qualifying trust (irrevocable, dedicated only to benefits, protected from creditors) the government reports the full $12,500,000.

4. Why are benefit payments recognized only when due and payable, even though the fund uses full accrual?

Show answer

That is the stated exception for pension trust funds: benefits and refunds are recognized when due and payable under the plan terms. Everything else follows full accrual.

6. Which fiduciary fund type fits? (a) a city employees' retirement trust; (b) the outside governments' share of a county investment pool; (c) unclaimed property held for owners; (d) taxes collected for a school district under no trust agreement.

Show answer
  • (a) Pension (and other employee benefit) trust fund.
  • (b) Investment trust fund.
  • (c) Private-purpose trust fund.
  • (d) Custodial fund.

7. How are differences between expected and actual investment earnings on pension plan assets reported by the employer?

Show answer

As a deferred outflow or inflow of resources, amortized over exactly 5 years. Experience differences and assumption changes are amortized over the average remaining service lives of employees.

8. What measurement focus and basis do fiduciary funds use, and what are their terms for revenues and expenses?

Show answer

Economic resources focus and full accrual. Revenues are called additions and expenses are deductions. Equity is net position restricted for the specific purpose.

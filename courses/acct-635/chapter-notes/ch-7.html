1. Core Learning Objectives & Chapter Blueprint

Mastering Chapter 7 requires transitioning from the modified accrual basis used in governmental funds to the full accrual accounting model applied in proprietary funds. Upon completing this study guide, students will be able to:

  • LO 7-1: Accounting Measurement Focus & Basis: Distinguish the Economic Resources Measurement Focus and Full Accrual Basis of Accounting used in Proprietary Funds from the Current Financial Resources focus of Governmental Funds.
  • LO 7-2: Internal Service Fund (ISF) Operations: Account for Internal Service Funds (ISF), including capital contributions, operating billings to other departments, depreciation, flexible budgets, and year-end net position adjustments.
  • LO 7-3: Enterprise Fund (EF) Mandatory Criteria: Apply GASB's mandatory 3-criteria rule to determine when an Enterprise Fund must be established for business-type activities financing services through user charges.
  • LO 7-4: GASB Statement No. 9 Cash Flows: Prepare and analyze the Statement of Cash Flows for proprietary funds using the mandatory DIRECT method across four activity categories (Operating, Noncapital Financing, Capital & Related Financing, Investing).
  • LO 7-5: Net Position Classification Hierarchy: Calculate and present the three components of Proprietary Fund Net Position: Net Investment in Capital Assets, Restricted Net Position, and Unrestricted Net Position.
  • LO 7-6: GASB vs. FASB Accounting Differences: Differentiate GASB proprietary standards from FASB corporate accounting rules regarding infrastructure reporting, asset impairment, pensions, and GASB Statement No. 87 leases.

2. Measurement Focus and Accounting Basis for Proprietary Funds

Proprietary funds account for governmental activities that are business-like or commercial in nature. Because these funds recover their costs through user fees, charges, or internal cost allocations, they follow commercial accounting rules under GASB standards.

3. Internal Service Funds (ISF) Mechanics & Accounting Workflows

Internal Service Funds (ISFs) are established to finance and account for goods or services provided by one department or agency to other departments or agencies of the governmental unit, or to other governmental units, on a cost-reimbursement basis.

4. Enterprise Fund (EF) Mandatory Criteria & Revenue Bonds

Enterprise Funds account for operations that are financed and operated in a manner similar to private business enterprises, where the intent is that costs of providing goods or services to the general public be financed primarily through user charges.

Swipe the table sideways to see all columns.

GASB Mandatory CriteriaLegal & Accounting ConditionApplication Example
1. Revenue Debt PledgeOutstanding debt is secured solely by a pledge of net revenues from fees/charges.Municipal Water Utility revenue bonds backed by water billing revenue.
2. Cost Recovery LawsLaws/regulations require that costs (including capital) be recovered with fees.Public transit authority required by charter to cover 100% operating costs.
3. Pricing PoliciesPricing policies establish fees designed to recover costs, including capital/depreciation.City municipal golf course fees priced to fund operations and turf capital.

5. Statement of Cash Flows (GASB Statement No. 9 Standards)

GASB Statement No. 9 mandates specific rules for proprietary fund cash flow statements that differ substantially from FASB corporate standards (ASC 230).

1. Course Learning Objectives

This handout covers Chapter 13: Accounting for Nonprofit Organizations. Upon completing this unit, students in ACCT 635 will be able to:

  • LO 1: Understand the characteristics and varieties of nonprofit organizations and the single source of GAAP (FASB ASC Topic 958) that applies to them.
  • LO 2: Understand the objectives of nonprofit financial reporting, required financial statements, and the classification of revenues, net assets, and expenses.
  • LO 3: Record and make required disclosures for contribution revenue, unconditional promises to give (pledges), present value discounting, and pledge allowances.
  • LO 4: Recognize contributed services, nonfinancial assets, and collections, applying specialized skill criteria and FASB ASU 2020-07 disclosure mandates.
  • LO 5: Measure and report debt and equity investments at fair value under FASB rules and account for revocable and irrevocable split-interest agreements.
  • LO 6: Distinguish exchange transactions from nonexchange contributions under FASB ASC Topic 606, report functional expenses, and present required liquidity disclosures.
  • LO 7: Navigate nonprofit regulatory requirements, including IRS Form 990, Form 990-T (Unrelated Business Income Tax), and public charity support tests.
  • LO 8 & 9: Explain why nonprofits maintain internal fund accounting records and how those records are converted to GAAP-compliant financial reporting.

2. Characteristics of Nonprofits and Sources of GAAP

Defining Characteristics of Nonprofits

Nonprofit organizations differ fundamentally from commercial enterprises in three core operational aspects:

  • Non-reciprocal Resource Providers: Significant contributions are received from resource providers who do not expect proportionate economic returns.
  • Operating Focus: Entities operate to provide goods or social services rather than generate profits for equity investors.
  • Absence of Ownership Interests: No transferable ownership shares exist, meaning there are no equity holders to receive dividends or redeem stock.

Four Broad Categories of Nonprofits

  • 1. VHWO: Voluntary Health and Welfare Organizations (VHWO): Formed to provide voluntary social, health, and welfare services to society (e.g., American Cancer Society, Boys & Girls Clubs of America, Feeding America).
  • 2. Health Care: Health Care Organizations: Hospitals, clinics, and nursing homes (covered under FASB guidance and Chapter 14).
  • 3. Higher Education: Colleges and Universities: Higher education institutions (covered under Chapter 15).
  • 4. Other Nonprofits: Other Nonprofit Organizations: Membership associations, labor unions, homeowner associations, political parties, and PACs.

Nonprofits vs. Governmental Entities

While both governments and nonprofits provide public services without profit motives, governmental entities possess unique legal powers:

  • Governance: Officers are elected by voters or appointed by government officials.
  • Legal Authority: Power to enact tax levies and issue tax-exempt municipal debt.
  • Dissolution: A parent government can unilaterally dissolve the entity and assume its assets and liabilities.

Single Source of GAAP: FASB ASC Topic 958

Nonprofit GAAP is established solely by the Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) under Accounting Standards Codification (ASC) Topic 958 (Not-for-Profit Entities). Key recent Accounting Standards Updates (ASUs) include:

  • FASB ASU 2020-07: Requires separate line-item presentation and detailed note disclosures for contributed nonfinancial assets (in-kind gifts).
  • FASB ASU 2019-06 & 2021-03: Extends private company accounting alternatives for goodwill and intangible assets to nonprofit organizations.

3. Financial Reporting Objectives and Required Statements

Core Objectives of Nonprofit Financial Reporting

Nonprofit financial statements are designed to help donors, grantors, creditors, and members:

  • Make rational decisions regarding resource allocation to the organization.
  • Assess the services provided and the organization's ability to continue providing them.
  • Evaluate management stewardship and compliance with donor-imposed restrictions.

Measurement Focus and Accounting Basis

Nonprofits utilize the economic resources measurement focus and full accrual basis of accounting. Long-lived capital assets are capitalized and depreciated, and long-term debt is recognized on the balance sheet.

Required Financial Statements

A complete set of FASB ASC Topic 958 financial statements includes:

  • 1. Statement of Financial Position: Reports total assets, total liabilities, and net assets split into two required categories.
  • 2. Statement of Activities: Reports changes in net assets, revenues, gains, and expenses by function.
  • 3. Statement of Cash Flows: Reports cash flows from operating, investing, and financing activities (direct or indirect method).
  • 4. Functional Expense Information: Discloses expenses by natural classification (salaries, rent, depreciation) and functional classification (program vs. supporting).
  • 5. Liquidity Disclosures: Discloses financial assets available to meet operating cash needs within one year.

Classification of Net Assets

FASB standards require net assets to be presented in two primary classes:

  • Net Assets With Donor Restrictions: Subject to donor stipulations regarding purpose, timing, or permanent maintenance (endowments held in perpetuity).
  • Net Assets Without Donor Restrictions: Free of donor stipulations. Includes undesignated net assets and board-designated net assets.
Key Concept: Board-Designated Net Assets
Rule: Board designations (e.g., setting aside $100,000 for plant expansion) do NOT create donor restrictions. They remain part of Net Assets Without Donor Restrictions and are disclosed on the face or in notes.
Journal Entry: Debit: Net assets without donor restrictions ($100,000) / Credit: Net assets without donor restrictions—board designated ($100,000)

4. Accounting for Contributions and Pledges

Definition and Recognition of Contributions

A contribution is an unconditional transfer of cash or other assets from one entity to another without receiving equal value in exchange. Unconditional contributions are recognized immediately as revenue or gains at fair value in the period received.

Unconditional Promises to Give (Pledges)

Pledges represent written or oral agreements to contribute cash in the future. Because pledges depend only on the passage of time or demand by the receiver, they are recognized immediately as receivables and contribution revenue.

  • Short-Term Pledges (< 1 Year): Reported at net realizable value using an Allowance for Uncollectible Contributions.
  • Long-Term Pledges (> 1 Year): Must be discounted to present value using the nonprofit's average borrowing rate or investment return rate. Discount accretion over time is recorded as additional contribution revenue.
Example 1: Short-Term Pledge Entry
Scenario: Food for the Community receives a $100,000 pledge expected to yield $98,000 cash prior to year-end, with $2,000 uncollectible.
Journal Entry: Debit: Contributions Receivable ($100,000)
Credit: Allowance for Uncollectible Contributions ($2,000)
Credit: Contribution Revenue—Support Without Donor Restrictions ($98,000)
Example 2: Multi-Year Pledge Entry with Present Value Discount
Scenario: A donor promises $15,000 paid in 3 annual installments of $5,000 (first $5,000 received immediately; remaining two $5,000 payments due in 2028 and 2029). The present value of the two future $5,000 payments at 5% discount rate is $9,297.
Journal Entry: Debit: Cash ($5,000)
Debit: Contributions Receivable ($10,000)
Credit: Contribution Revenue—Without Donor Restrictions ($5,000)
Credit: Contribution Revenue—With Donor Restrictions (Time) ($9,297)
Credit: Discount on Contributions Receivable ($703)

Conditional Promises to Give

Conditional pledges bind the donor only upon the occurrence of a specified future and uncertain event (e.g., matching grant thresholds or building construction milestones). Conditional promises are NOT recognized as revenue until the conditions are substantially met. Cash received before conditions are met is credited to Refundable Advance (a liability).

Expiration of Donor Restrictions (Reclassifications)

When purpose or time restrictions are satisfied, net assets are reclassified on the Statement of Activities via 'Net Assets Released from Restrictions':

  • Reclassification Entry: Debit: Net Assets With Donor Restrictions—Reclassification Out ($5,000)
    Credit: Net Assets Without Donor Restrictions—Reclassification In ($5,000)

5. Contributed Services, Collections, and Nonfinancial Assets

Contributed Services Criteria

Under FASB ASC 958-605, contributed services are recognized as revenue and offsetting assets/expenses ONLY if they meet one of two strict conditions:

  1. They create or enhance nonfinancial assets (e.g., building construction or major renovation).
  2. They require specialized skills, are provided by individuals possessing those skills, and would typically need to be purchased if not donated (e.g., CPAs, attorneys, architects, electricians, nurses).
Key Rule: Skilled vs. Unskilled Volunteer Services
Rule: Unskilled volunteer labor (e.g., volunteers stocking food bank shelves or serving meals) does NOT meet specialized skill criteria. No journal entry is permitted, regardless of hours worked.
CPA Pro Bono Entry: Debit: Expenses—Accounting Services ($9,000) / Credit: Unrestricted Support—Contributed Services ($9,000) [90 hrs @ $100/hr]

Contributions to Collections

Nonprofits (e.g., museums) may elect NOT to capitalize donated works of art, historical treasures, or rare books if the collection items meet three mandatory criteria:

  1. Held for public exhibition, education, or research in furtherance of public service rather than financial gain.
  2. Protected, kept unencumbered, cared for, and preserved.
  3. Subject to an organizational policy requiring sale proceeds to be used solely to acquire other collection items or provide direct care of existing collections.

Presentation of Contributed Nonfinancial Assets (ASU 2020-07)

FASB ASU 2020-07 mandates that nonprofits present contributed nonfinancial assets as a separate line item on the Statement of Activities (separate from cash contributions) and disclose in notes: asset categories, valuation techniques, monetization vs. program utilization policies, and donor restrictions.

6. Investment Accounting and Split-Interest Agreements

Fair Value Measurement for Investments

Under FASB ASC 958-320, debt securities and equity securities with readily determinable fair values are reported at fair value on the Statement of Financial Position. Unrealized and realized gains and losses are recognized in the Statement of Activities.

FASB Fair Value Hierarchy:

  • Level 1: Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets.
  • Level 2: Observable inputs other than Level 1 prices (e.g., quoted prices for similar assets).
  • Level 3: Unobservable inputs reflecting the entity's own assumptions.

Split-Interest Agreements

Split-interest agreements represent trust arrangements where a nonprofit shares benefits with other beneficiaries:

  • Lead Interest: Nonprofit receives cash distributions during the trust term.
  • Remainder Interest: Nonprofit receives remaining trust principal at the end of the trust term.
  • Revocable Agreements: No contribution revenue recognized. Cash received is credited to Refundable Advance liability.
  • Irrevocable Agreements: Recognize contribution revenue and beneficial interest assets at present fair value when executed.

7. Exchange Revenue, Functional Expenses, and Liquidity

Exchange vs. Nonexchange Transactions (FASB ASC Topic 606)

Nonprofits often engage in exchange transactions where reciprocal value is exchanged (e.g., tuition, hospital patient care, bookstore sales, government service contracts). Nonprofits apply the FASB Topic 606 5-Step Model:

  1. Identify contract
  2. Identify performance obligations
  3. Determine price
  4. Allocate price
  5. Recognize revenue as obligations are satisfied.

Bifurcating Dual-Purpose Contracts

Contracts containing both exchange and contribution elements (e.g., annual membership dues that include a $20 newsletter and an $80 contribution) must be bifurcated into exchange revenue ($20) and contribution revenue ($80).

Functional Expense Classifications

Nonprofits must report expenses by functional classification:

  • Program Services: Activities directly tied to the primary mission (e.g., youth program operations).
  • Supporting Services: Management and general expenses, fundraising costs, and membership development.
Joint Cost Allocation & Key Performance Ratios
Rule: Fundraising costs that combine educational material with solicitation can be allocated between Program and Fundraising ONLY if three criteria are met: Purpose, Audience, and Content. Otherwise, 100% of the cost is charged to Fundraising.
Program Efficiency Ratio: Program Expenses / Total Expenses (e.g., Boys & Girls Clubs: $350,561 / $387,077 = 90.6%). Donors monitor this key benchmark.

Liquidity Disclosures

FASB requires a dedicated note disclosure detailing financial assets available to meet operating cash needs within one year of the balance sheet date, subtracting donor restrictions, board-designated endowments, and illiquid holdings.

8. Regulatory Environment, Form 990, and Fund Accounting

Tax Exemption under IRC Section 501(c)(3)

To obtain federal tax-exempt status, organizations file IRS Form 1023. Entities are categorized as:

  • Public Charities: Must pass the Public Support Test (at least 1/3 of support derived from public/government sources).
  • Private Foundations: Funded by a single donor/family; subject to strict investment and distribution rules.

IRS Annual Information Returns

  • Form 990: Annual information return required for most tax-exempt entities (discloses governance, key employee compensation, functional expenses, program accomplishments).
  • Form 990-T: Filed by tax-exempt organizations with $1,000 or more of Gross Unrelated Business Income (UBIT) from regularly conducted trades or businesses unrelated to their exempt purpose.

Internal Fund Accounting vs. GAAP Reporting

While GAAP prohibits fund accounting for external financial reporting, many nonprofits maintain internal funds (Unrestricted Current, Restricted Current, Endowment, Plant Funds). These internal fund balances are consolidated into the two GAAP net asset classes (With and Without Donor Restrictions) during year-end financial statement preparation.

6. A $150 membership includes a magazine valued at $30. How is it split?

Show answer

$30 is exchange revenue (Topic 606), recognized as the magazine is delivered, and $120 is a contribution. The contract is bifurcated into its exchange and contribution elements.

7. Program expenses are $780,000, management and general $120,000, and fundraising $100,000. What is the program expense ratio?

Show answer

$780,000 ÷ $1,000,000 = 78%. Donors watch this benchmark.

8. Classify: (a) a board sets aside $250,000 for plant expansion; (b) a donor gives $500,000 that must stay in an endowment forever; (c) a pledge payable only next year.

Show answer
  • (a) Without donor restrictions (board-designated). A board designation never creates a donor restriction.
  • (b) With donor restrictions (perpetual).
  • (c) With donor restrictions (time restriction).

9. Financial assets are $2,400,000. Of these, $600,000 is donor-restricted, $300,000 is board-designated, and $150,000 is illiquid. How much is available for general expenditure within one year?

Show answer

$2,400,000 − $600,000 − $300,000 − $150,000 = $1,350,000. This is the liquidity disclosure.

10. A donor transfers assets to a trust that pays income to the donor's spouse for life and then the remainder to the charity, and the donor can revoke it. When is revenue recognized?

Show answer

Not yet. For a revocable agreement, cash received is a Refundable Advance (liability). Revenue and a beneficial-interest asset at fair value are recognized when an irrevocable agreement is executed.

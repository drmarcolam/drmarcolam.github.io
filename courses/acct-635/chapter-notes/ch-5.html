1. Core Learning Objectives

Chapter Key Learning Outcomes & Mastery Goals:
▪ Apply Modified Accrual & Current Financial Resources Focus: Master the revenue availability criteria, the 60-day rule for property taxes, and the current financial resources accounting equation.
▪ Account for Property Tax Levies & Delinquencies: Record property tax levies, allowances for uncollectible taxes, tax appeals, delinquent tax reclassifications, and deferred inflow revenue recognition.
▪ Classify Revenue Recognition across Derived & Intergovernmental Categories: Distinguish revenue recognition timing for sales/income taxes, grant reimbursements, pass-through grants, and licenses/permits.
▪ Differentiate Interfund Transactions: Record the four distinct interfund categories—Interfund Loans, Interfund Services Provided/Used, Interfund Transfers, and Interfund Reimbursements—across both affected funds.
▪ Apply Inventory Accounting & GASB 54 Fund Balance Hierarchy: Compare the Consumption Method versus Purchases Method for inventory, and classify fund balance components across Nonspendable, Restricted, Committed, Assigned, and Unassigned categories.

2. Current Financial Resources Focus & Modified Accrual Principles

Governmental funds (General Fund, Special Revenue Funds, Capital Projects Funds, Debt Service Funds, and Permanent Funds) utilize the current financial resources measurement focus and the modified accrual basis of accounting. This framework emphasizes spendable financial assets and short-term liquidity rather than long-term economic capital or net income generation.

  • Focus on Spendable Resources: Governmental funds measure spendable financial assets (Cash, Investments, Taxes & Accounts Receivable) and short-term or matured long-term liabilities. Capital assets (buildings, equipment, land) and unmatured long-term liabilities (bonds payable, long-term notes) are excluded from governmental fund balance sheets.
  • Revenue Recognition Rule: Revenues are recognized only when they are both measurable and available. 'Available' means collectible within the current fiscal period or soon enough thereafter to be used to pay liabilities of the current period.
  • 60-Day Availability Rule: For property taxes, resources are considered available only if collected within the current fiscal year or within 60 days after the end of the fiscal year.
  • Expenditure Recognition Rule: Expenditures are recognized when liabilities are incurred and normally expected to be liquidated in a timely manner from current available financial resources.
  • Unmatured Long-Term Indebtedness Exception: Unmatured portions of bonds and notes payable represent general long-term liabilities and are NEVER reported as fund liabilities in governmental funds.

Governmental Funds Accounting Equation:
Financial Assets + Deferred Outflows of Resources = Short-Term Liabilities + Matured Long-Term Liabilities + Deferred Inflows of Resources + Fund Balance

DEFERRED OUTFLOWS VS. DEFERRED INFLOWS
Deferred Outflows of Resources represent a consumption of net assets applicable to a future reporting period (reported after assets).
Deferred Inflows of Resources represent an acquisition of net assets applicable to a future reporting period (reported after liabilities, e.g., unavailable property taxes collected >60 days post year-end).

3. Accounting for Property Tax Revenues and Receivables

Property taxes are classified as imposed nonexchange revenues. Revenue is recognized in the fiscal year for which taxes are levied, provided the funds are measurable and available (collected during the year or within 60 days after fiscal year-end).

  • No Bad Debt Expense: Governmental funds DO NOT report bad debt expense. Instead, Revenues—Property Taxes are credited at the NET estimated collectible amount at the time of levy.
  • Levy Journal Entry: Debit Property Taxes Receivable (Gross levy), Credit Allowance for Refunds & Uncollectible Taxes, Credit Revenues—Property Taxes (Net amount).
  • Tax Appeals: If taxpayers successfully appeal tax assessments, Allowance for Refunds & Uncollectible Taxes is debited, and Cash (if already paid) or Property Taxes Receivable (if unpaid) is credited.
  • Delinquent Reclassification: At year-end, uncollected receivables are transferred to Property Taxes Receivable—Delinquent, and the corresponding allowance is transferred to Allowance for Refunds & Uncollectible Taxes—Delinquent.
  • 60-Day Year-End Deferral: Unpaid delinquent taxes expected to be collected AFTER the first 60 days of the next fiscal year must be deferred: Debit Revenues—Property Taxes, Credit Deferred Inflow of Resources—Unavailable Property Taxes.
  • Subsequent Year Revenue Recognition: When deferred delinquent taxes are collected in subsequent periods beyond 60 days: Debit Cash, Credit Property Taxes Receivable—Delinquent AND Debit Deferred Inflow of Resources—Unavailable Property Taxes, Credit Revenues—Property Taxes.

Special Property Tax Considerations:

  • Early Payment Discounts: When discounts are offered for early payment, an Allowance for Discounts on Property Taxes is credited upon levy (reducing net revenue). If less discounts are taken than estimated, Revenues—Property Taxes is credited upon collection.
  • Interest & Penalties on Delinquent Taxes: Recognized as revenue when available (usually cash basis). If accrued, an Allowance for Uncollectible Interest & Penalties must be recorded.
  • Tax Liens & Property Tax Sales: If taxes remain unpaid, government enforces a legal lien (Dr. Tax Liens Receivable, Cr. Property Taxes Receivable—Delinquent). If property is sold at auction, government retains taxes owed + administrative costs; remainder goes to former owner.

4. Derived Tax Revenues, Intergovernmental Grants, and Nonexchange Revenues

Governmental revenues are categorized based on the underlying transaction characteristics into derived taxes, intergovernmental grants, imposed nonexchange revenues, and exchange revenues.

Revenue ClassificationKey ExamplesRevenue Recognition Timing & Rules
Derived Tax RevenuesGeneral Sales Taxes, Personal Income Taxes, Fuel TaxesRecognized when underlying exchange transaction occurs AND resources are available. Income taxes accrue based on post-year-end receipts and estimated refunds payable.
Intergovernmental GrantsExpenditure-Driven (Reimbursement) Grants, Pass-Through GrantsRecognized when eligibility requirements (qualifying expenditures incurred) AND time requirements are met. Cash received prior to meeting time rules = Deferred Inflow.
Imposed Nonexchange RevenuesProperty Taxes, Fines, Traffic Penalties, ForfeituresRecognized when enforceable legal claim arises AND resources are available. Net of estimated uncollectibles.
Miscellaneous Exchange RevenuesGolf/Pool fees, Inspection fees, Parking fees, Business LicensesRecognized on cash basis when cash is received. Business licenses are generally not susceptible to accrual.
SPECIAL GRANT & PILOT RULES
Pass-Through Grants: Recognized as revenues and expenditures in primary government funds EXCEPT when the primary government serves merely as a cash conduit (no administrative or financial involvement). In cash conduit cases, a Custodial Fund is used.
Payments in Lieu of Taxes (PILOTs): Cash received from tax-exempt entities (federal government, nonprofits) to cover lost property tax revenue. Recognized when eligibility requirements are met and resources are available.

5. Expenditure Accruals and Liability Recognition Rules

Under modified accrual accounting, expenditures are categorized into three operational groups, each following distinct liability recognition criteria:

7. Acquisition of Capital Assets and Supplies Inventory Accounting

In governmental funds, capital assets purchased are treated as capital outlays rather than capitalized assets. For supplies inventory and prepayments, governments select between two recognized accounting methods:

Inventory Accounting MethodPurchase & Usage Journal EntriesYear-End Balance Sheet & Fund Balance Treatment
Consumption Method
(Asset-based approach)		Purchase: Dr. Supplies Inventory, Cr. Vouchers Payable.
Usage: Dr. Expenditures—Supplies, Cr. Supplies Inventory.		Views inventory as a financial asset. Year-end inventory balance requires a corresponding Nonspendable Fund Balance classification.
Purchases Method
(Expenditure-based approach)		Purchase: Dr. Expenditures—Supplies, Cr. Vouchers Payable.
Usage: No entry during the year.		Does not view inventory as financial asset. At year-end, if inventory is material: Dr. Supplies Inventory, Cr. Nonspendable Fund Balance.

Capital Asset Transactions in Governmental Funds:

  • Acquisition / Outlay: Dr. Expenditures—Capital Outlay, Cr. Cash / Vouchers Payable. (Depreciation is NOT recorded in governmental funds).
  • Sale / Disposal Proceeds: Dr. Cash, Cr. Other Financing Sources—Proceeds from Sale of General Capital Assets. (No gain or loss is calculated in governmental funds).

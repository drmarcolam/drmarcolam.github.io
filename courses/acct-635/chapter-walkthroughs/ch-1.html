Standard setters

Which Board Writes the Rules?

Laurel Gap is a small mountain town. Its accountants must know which standard setter governs each organization they deal with.

  • GASB sets GAAP for state and local governments, including public universities and utilities.
  • FASB sets GAAP for businesses and private nonprofits (ASC Topic 958).
  • FASAB sets standards for the federal government.
Example 1 — Match the Entity to Its Standard Setter

Choose the board that issues GAAP for each organization.

Need a hint?
Ask two questions: is it governmental, and if not, is it a nonprofit or a business?
Solution
  • Town and library: a state or local government and its departments follow GASB.
  • Food pantry: a private nonprofit follows FASB ASC Topic 958.
  • USDA: federal agencies follow FASAB.
  • State university: public universities are governmental, so GASB applies.
  • Marina: a business follows FASB GAAP.
Example 2 — Rank the Guidance

A town has no GASB Statement on a transaction. Place each source in the GAAP hierarchy.

Solution

Category A (official GASB Statements) is most authoritative. Category B includes Technical Bulletins, Implementation Guides and cleared AICPA literature. If neither speaks to the transaction, accountants may use nonauthoritative sources such as FASB pronouncements by analogy and GASB Concept Statements.

Governmental status

Is It a Governmental Entity?

A nonprofit corporation or a statutory body is governmental if it has at least one of four characteristics.

  1. Officers are popularly elected, or a controlling majority of the governing body is appointed or approved by government officials.
  2. Power to enact or enforce a tax levy.
  3. Power to issue directly debt whose interest is tax-exempt.
  4. Another government can unilaterally dissolve it and take its net assets.
Example 1 — Governmental or Not?

Decide for each organization.

Solution
  • Government-appointed majority: criterion 1, so governmental.
  • Tax-exempt debt power: criterion 3, so governmental.
  • Receiving a grant does not make an organization governmental. No criterion is met.
  • Unilateral dissolution by another government: criterion 4.
  • A donor-governed foundation meets none of the criteria.
Interperiod equity

Did Current Citizens Pay for Current Services?

The GASB's key reporting objective is interperiod equity: whether current-year revenues were enough to pay for current-year services, or whether the cost was pushed to future taxpayers.

Example 1 — Measure the Shortfall

Laurel Gap's current-year revenues were $3,600,000. The cost of services provided this year was $3,850,000. By how much did revenues fall short of the cost of current services?

Need a hint?
Subtract revenues from the cost of services.
$
Solution

$3,850,000 − $3,600,000 = $250,000.

Shortfall = $250,000. Because revenues fell short, interperiod equity was not achieved this year: the gap shifts to future taxpayers.

Example 2 — Who Pays?

Does each practice shift the cost of current services to future taxpayers?

Solution

Borrowing for routine operating costs and underfunding long-term obligations both defer the cost. Paying from current taxes and fees keeps the cost with the people who received the service.

Reporting frameworks

Comparing Reporting Frameworks

Entity-wide statements are required of every type of entity, but fund reporting and modified accrual statements are special to state and local governments.

  • Federal: entity-wide, budgetary reporting, accrual basis. No funds.
  • State and local: entity-wide and fund statements, budgetary reporting, accrual and modified accrual.
  • Businesses and nonprofits: entity-wide accrual statements only.
Example 1 — Who Does What?

Choose the entity type that matches each description.

Solution

Only state and local governments prepare fund-level statements on a modified accrual basis alongside budgetary reporting.

Example 2 — Board Membership

The Financial Accounting Foundation (FAF) appoints the full-time members of both the FASB and the GASB. How many full-time members does each board have?

Solution

The FAF appoints seven full-time members to each board, oversees their budgets and protects their independence.

Each board has 7 full-time members.

Example 3 — Nonprofit or Business?

Choose which description fits a nonprofit and which fits a for-profit business.

Solution

Nonprofits receive nonreciprocal contributions, exist to provide services, and have no ownership interests. A business budget is a flexible management tool, while a government budget carries the force of law.

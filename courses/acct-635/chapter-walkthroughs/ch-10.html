Capital assets

Capital Asset Adjustments

Governmental funds expense capital outlay. For government-wide statements, Laurel Gap had $520,000 of capital outlay and $340,000 of depreciation this year.

Example 1 — Capitalize the Outlay

Choose Debit or Credit for each account in the conversion entry.

What is the net effect of capitalizing the outlay and recording depreciation?

$
Solution

+$520,000 − $340,000 = +$180,000.

Net effect = +$180,000

Revenue and expense

Revenue and Expense Adjustments

Full accrual has no availability test, so unavailable taxes become revenue. Expenses incurred but not due are accrued.

Example 1 — Net Revenue Adjustment

This year's unavailable property taxes are $40,000. Last year's deferred taxes of $25,000 were collected this year. What is the net change in revenue for government-wide statements?

$
Solution

+$40,000 now recognized, −$25,000 that belonged to last year = +$15,000.

Net revenue adjustment = +$15,000

Example 2 — Stub Interest

Bonds of $3,000,000 bear 4.8% interest paid February 1 and August 1. How much interest is accrued at December 31 (August 1 to December 31)?

$
Solution

$3,000,000 × 4.8% × 5/12 = $60,000.

Accrued interest = $60,000

Example 3 — Interest Expense Adjustment

Last year's accrued interest was $55,000. How much additional interest expense is recorded this year?

$
Solution

$60,000 − $55,000 = $5,000. The $55,000 is debited to beginning net position.

Additional expense = $5,000

Reconciliation

Reconciling the Balance Sheet to Net Position

Total governmental fund balances are $2,900,000. Capital assets, net, are $12,400,000. Bonds payable are $6,500,000, accrued interest payable $60,000, compensated absences $190,000, and unavailable property taxes (a deferred inflow) $40,000.

Example 1 — Net Position of Governmental Activities

What is net position of governmental activities?

$
Solution

$2,900,000 + $12,400,000 − $6,500,000 − $60,000 − $190,000 + $40,000 = $8,590,000.

Net position = $8,590,000

Example 2 — Net Investment in Capital Assets

Of the $6,500,000 of bonds, $500,000 of proceeds has not yet been spent on capital assets. What is net investment in capital assets?

$
Solution

Subtract only debt used for the assets: $12,400,000 − ($6,500,000 − $500,000) = $6,400,000. The unspent proceeds affect restricted or unrestricted net position instead.

Net investment = $6,400,000

Eliminations and infrastructure

Interfund Eliminations and the Modified Approach

Internal activity is removed so the government is shown as a single entity.

Example 1 — How Is It Handled?

Choose how each item is reported in the government-wide statements.

Solution

Transfers between governmental and business-type activities are reported as internal activities that net to zero in the total column.

Example 2 — Modified Approach

Which statements describe the modified approach for infrastructure?

Solution

The modified approach replaces depreciation with an asset management system, regular condition assessments and documented preservation at or above a stated level.

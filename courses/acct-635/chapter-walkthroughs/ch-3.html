Property tax rates

Setting the Millage Rate

Property taxes require annual legal action to set the levy and the millage rate. The town of Laurel Gap needs revenue and expects some taxes to go uncollected.

  • Required levy = needed revenue ÷ expected collection percentage.
  • Net assessed value = total assessed value − exemptions.
  • Millage rate = levy ÷ net assessed value (expressed per $1,000 as mills).
Example 1 — Required Levy

Laurel Gap needs $5,200,000 of property tax revenue and expects to collect 97% of what it levies. What levy is required? (Round to the nearest dollar.)

Need a hint?
Divide by the collection percentage so the levy covers expected uncollectibles.
$
Solution

$5,200,000 ÷ 0.97 = $5,360,825.

Required levy = $5,360,825

Example 2 — Net Assessed Value

Total assessed value is $980,000,000 and exemptions total $120,000,000. What is net assessed value?

$
Solution

$980,000,000 − $120,000,000 = $860,000,000.

Net assessed value = $860,000,000

Example 3 — Millage Rate

What is the millage rate in mills (dollars per $1,000 of net assessed value) using a levy of $5,360,825? (Round to three decimals.)

mills
Solution

$5,360,825 ÷ $860,000,000 = 0.006234; × 1,000 = 6.234 mills.

Millage rate = 6.234 mills

Example 4 — A Taxpayer's Bill

Using 6.234 mills, what is the tax on a property with a net assessed value of $175,000?

$
Solution

($175,000 ÷ 1,000) × 6.234 = $1,090.87.

Tax bill = $1,090.87

Cash forecasting

Borrowing Against Future Taxes

Tax receipts are lumpy, but spending is steady. A tax anticipation note (TAN) bridges the gap.

Example 1 — How Much to Borrow?

A General Fund starts a quarter with $18,000 of cash, expects $60,000 of receipts and $115,000 of disbursements, and must keep a $12,000 minimum balance. How much must it borrow on a TAN?

$
Solution

Cash available = $18,000 + $60,000 = $78,000.
Need = $115,000 + $12,000 minimum − $78,000 = $49,000.

TAN borrowing = $49,000

Example 2 — Interest on the TAN

The $49,000 note is outstanding for 4 months at 5% annual interest. What is the interest? (Round to the nearest dollar.)

$
Solution

$49,000 × 5% × 4/12 = $816.67, or $817.

Interest = $817

Budgetary entries

Recording the Budget

Governments record the adopted budget in the accounts to control spending. Estimated revenues are a debit, appropriations a credit, and the difference goes to Budgetary Fund Balance.

Example 1 — Adopt the Budget

Choose Debit or Credit for each account in the entry that records a budget with expected surplus.

Solution

Adopt the budget:

Budget

    Appropriations4,180,000

Estimated revenues exceed appropriations by $120,000, so the budgeted surplus is credited to Budgetary Fund Balance.

Example 2 — Budgeted Surplus

Estimated revenues are $4,300,000 and appropriations are $4,180,000. What amount is credited to Budgetary Fund Balance?

$
Solution

$4,300,000 − $4,180,000 = $120,000.

Budgetary Fund Balance credit = $120,000

Example 3 — Interchange or Revision?

Is each change a budgetary interchange (total unchanged) or a revision (total changes)?

Solution

An interchange moves authority between appropriations (Dr one, Cr another). A revision changes the total budget and affects Budgetary Fund Balance or estimated revenues.

Encumbrances

The Purchase Order Cycle

An encumbrance commits appropriation authority when a purchase order is issued. When the goods arrive, reverse the exact encumbered amount and record the actual invoice as an expenditure.

Example 1 — Issue the Purchase Order

Choose Debit or Credit for each account when a purchase order is issued.

Solution

Issue a purchase order:

PO
Issue a purchase order		Encumbrances18,500
    Budgetary Fund Balance Reserved for Encumbrances18,500

The encumbrance is a budgetary commitment, not a liability.

Example 2 — Reversal

A purchase order for $18,500 is filled by goods with an invoice of $18,200. What amount is the encumbrance reversed for?

$
Solution

Always reverse the original encumbered amount, $18,500, even though the invoice differs.

Reversal = $18,500

Example 3 — Expenditure

What amount is recorded as the expenditure?

$
Solution

The actual invoice amount, $18,200, is recorded as the expenditure and a liability.

Expenditure = $18,200

Example 4 — Available Appropriation

An appropriation is $60,000. Outstanding encumbrances are $12,500 and expenditures to date are $31,200. How much appropriation is still available?

$
Solution

$60,000 − $12,500 − $31,200 = $16,300.

Available = $16,300

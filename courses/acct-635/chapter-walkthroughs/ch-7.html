ISF or EF?

Internal Service Fund or Enterprise Fund?

Both are proprietary funds that use the economic resources focus and full accrual. The difference is who the customers are.

An Enterprise Fund is required for a significant activity if any of these is true: debt is secured only by the activity's net revenues, laws require cost recovery through fees, or pricing policies are meant to recover costs.

Example 1 — Choose the Fund

Which fund fits each activity?

Solution

Internal customers: Internal Service Fund. Outside customers who pay fees: Enterprise Fund.

Example 2 — Is an Enterprise Fund Required?

Does each situation require an Enterprise Fund?

Solution

Any one of the three criteria (revenue bond debt, legal cost recovery, or cost-recovery pricing policy) requires an Enterprise Fund for a significant activity.

GASB vs. FASB

How GASB and FASB Differ

Governments and businesses report some of the same transactions differently.

Example 1 — GASB or FASB?

Which standard setter's rules fit each statement?

Solution

GASB: modified approach, short-/long-term leases, fiduciary reporting, deferral of pension differences, deferred outflows/inflows. FASB: finance/operating leases, other comprehensive income, no modified approach.

Proprietary leases

Leases in a Proprietary Fund

A utility signs a 3-year equipment lease with $50,000 payments due at the start of each year, at 6%.

A proprietary fund uses full accrual, so it records the intangible right-to-use asset and the lease payable, then amortization and interest expense.

Example 1 — Present Value

The present value is $50,000 × (1 + 1/1.06 + 1/1.06²). What is it? (Round to the nearest dollar.)

$
Solution

$50,000 × (1 + 0.943396 + 0.889996) = $141,670.

Lease payable and intangible asset = $141,670

Example 2 — Annual Amortization

What amortization expense is recorded each year (straight-line over the 3-year term)?

$
Solution

$141,670 ÷ 3 = $47,223.

Amortization = $47,223

Example 3 — Year-1 Interest Expense

After the first $50,000 payment, the liability is $91,670. What interest expense accrues for the year?

$
Solution

$91,670 × 6% = $5,500. A proprietary fund accrues it at year-end.

Interest expense = $5,500

Multi-year pledges

Discounting Long-Term Pledges

A donor pledges $25,000: $5,000 now and $10,000 at the end of each of the next two years. The pantry uses an 8% rate.

Pledges due after more than a year are recorded at present value. The discount is accreted into additional contribution revenue over time.

Example 1 — Present Value

What is the present value of the two future payments? (Round to the nearest dollar.)

$
Solution

$10,000 ÷ 1.08 + $10,000 ÷ 1.08² = $17,833.

Present value = $17,833

Example 2 — Discount

The two payments total $20,000. What is the discount on contributions receivable?

$
Solution

$20,000 − $17,833 = $2,167.

Discount = $2,167

Example 3 — Year-1 Accretion

What revenue is recorded from accreting the discount in year 1? (Round to the nearest dollar.)

$
Solution

$17,833 × 8% = $1,427.

Accretion = $1,427

Conditional promises

Conditions and Restrictions

A conditional promise is not revenue until the condition is substantially met. Cash received first is a Refundable Advance.

Example 1 — Cash Before the Condition Is Met

A matching grant of $50,000 depends on the pantry raising $50,000 from others. The grantor sends $20,000 early. Choose Debit or Credit.

Solution

It is a liability until the match is raised.

Contributed services

Which Services Are Recorded?

Contributed services are recorded only if they create or enhance nonfinancial assets, or require specialized skills and would otherwise be purchased.

Example 1 — Record or Not?

Decide for each service.

Solution

Unskilled labor is never recorded, regardless of the hours. Skilled services and services that enhance nonfinancial assets are.

Example 2 — Value of the CPA's Time

The CPA donates 45 hours at a fair value of $140/hour. What amount is recorded?

$
Solution

45 × $140 = $6,300.

Contributed services = $6,300

Exchange vs. contribution

Splitting a Membership

A $200 annual membership includes benefits (a magazine and a discount) with a fair value of $45.

Example 1 — Contribution Portion

How much is contribution revenue?

$
Solution

$200 − $45 = $155. The $45 is exchange revenue under Topic 606.

Contribution = $155

Net assets and ratios

Net Assets, Efficiency and Liquidity

Net assets are shown in two classes: with and without donor restrictions.

Example 1 — Classify the Net Assets

Choose the class.

Solution

A board designation never creates a donor restriction. Time and perpetual restrictions are donor restrictions.

Example 2 — Program Efficiency Ratio

Program expenses are $640,000, management and general $95,000, and fundraising $65,000. What percent of total expenses are program expenses?

%
Solution

$640,000 ÷ $800,000 = 80%.

Program ratio = 80%

Example 3 — Available Within One Year

Financial assets are $1,800,000. Donor-restricted $450,000, board-designated $200,000 and illiquid $120,000 amounts are not available. How much is available?

$
Solution

$1,800,000 − $450,000 − $200,000 − $120,000 = $1,030,000.

Available = $1,030,000

Example 4 — Revocable or Irrevocable?

When is revenue recognized for a split-interest agreement?

Solution

For a revocable agreement, cash is a Refundable Advance. When an irrevocable agreement is executed, the charity records revenue and a beneficial-interest asset at fair value.

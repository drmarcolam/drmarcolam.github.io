Fund types

Which Fund Does It Belong In?

A fund is a self-balancing set of accounts segregated for specific activities. Governments use 11 fund types in three categories: governmental (5), proprietary (2) and fiduciary (4).

  • Governmental: General, Special Revenue, Capital Projects, Debt Service, Permanent.
  • Proprietary: Enterprise, Internal Service.
  • Fiduciary: Pension Trust, Investment Trust, Private-Purpose Trust, Custodial.
Example 1 — Governmental Funds

Choose the fund for each situation.

Need a hint?
Think about the purpose of the resources, not the source.
Solution

The General Fund is the residual fund. Special Revenue Funds hold restricted or committed revenue sources, Capital Projects Funds hold resources for capital outlays, Debt Service Funds pay general long-term debt, and Permanent Funds preserve principal.

Example 2 — Proprietary and Fiduciary Funds

Choose the fund for each situation.

Solution

Enterprise Funds serve outside customers; Internal Service Funds serve other departments. Fiduciary funds hold assets for others and cannot support the government's own programs.

Measurement focus

Measurement Focus and Basis of Accounting

Each fund category measures different things and recognizes transactions at different times.

Example 1 — Match the Category

Choose the measurement focus for each fund category.

Solution

Governmental funds track spendable financial resources (modified accrual). Proprietary and fiduciary funds track all economic resources (full accrual).

Example 2 — On the Governmental Fund Balance Sheet?

Would each item appear on a governmental fund balance sheet?

Solution

Governmental fund balance sheets include financial assets and short-term or matured liabilities only. Capital assets and unmatured long-term debt are reported in the government-wide statements.

Recognition

Same Transaction, Different Fund

Laurel Gap records the same events in a governmental fund (modified accrual) and in its Enterprise Fund (full accrual).

  • Governmental funds report expenditures and expense capital assets in full when acquired.
  • Enterprise funds report expenses, capitalize assets and record depreciation.
  • Under modified accrual, revenue must be both measurable and available (collected within the period or 60 days after).
Example 1 — Salaries

The General Fund paid $380,000 of salaries during the year and owes $15,000 more for work already done at year-end. How much salary expenditure does the General Fund report?

$
Solution

Paid $380,000 + accrued $15,000 = $395,000. Salaries are normally paid soon, so the liability is accrued under modified accrual.

Salary expenditure = $395,000

Example 2 — Equipment in an Enterprise Fund

An Enterprise Fund buys equipment for $120,000 with a 5-year life on the first day of the year. Using straight-line depreciation with no salvage value, how much depreciation expense does it record for the year?

$
Solution

$120,000 ÷ 5 years = $24,000 per year. A governmental fund, by contrast, would report the full $120,000 as a capital outlay expenditure.

Depreciation expense = $24,000

Example 3 — Debt Payment

A payment of $62,000 includes $50,000 of principal and $12,000 of interest. How much expense does an Enterprise Fund record?

$
Solution

Only interest is an expense in a full accrual fund. The $50,000 principal payment reduces the liability. A Debt Service Fund would record the whole $62,000 as a debt service expenditure.

Interest expense = $12,000

Example 4 — Property Tax Revenue

The General Fund levies $800,000 of property taxes and expects $16,000 to be uncollectible. Of the $784,000 expected, $740,000 arrives during the year, $32,000 within 60 days after year-end, and $12,000 after that. How much property tax revenue does the fund report under modified accrual?

Need a hint?
The 60-day window is the availability test.
$
Solution

Only amounts collected within the year or the 60 days after are available: $740,000 + $32,000 = $772,000. The remaining $12,000 is a deferred inflow of resources. Under full accrual, revenue would be $784,000.

Modified accrual revenue = $772,000

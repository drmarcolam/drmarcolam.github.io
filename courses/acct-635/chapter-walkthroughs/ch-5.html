Property taxes

Following a Levy from Start to Finish

The Town of Laurel Gap levies $1,800,000 of property taxes and estimates $36,000 will be uncollectible.

  • Governmental funds record no bad debt expense. Revenue is recorded net of estimated uncollectibles.
  • Unpaid taxes become delinquent at year-end.
  • Delinquent taxes not expected to be collected within 60 days are deferred.
Example 1 — Record the Levy

Choose Debit or Credit for each account in the levy entry.

Solution

Levy:

1
Levy		Property Taxes Receivable1,800,000
    Allowance for Refunds and Uncollectible Taxes36,000
    Revenues—Property Taxes1,764,000

Revenue is credited at the net collectible amount.

Example 2 — Revenue

How much revenue is recorded at the time of the levy?

$
Solution

$1,800,000 − $36,000 = $1,764,000.

Revenue = $1,764,000

Example 3 — Delinquent Taxes

During the year the town collects $1,650,000, and appeals reduce taxes by $14,000. How much of the gross receivable is declared delinquent at year-end?

$
Solution

$1,800,000 − $1,650,000 − $14,000 = $136,000.

Delinquent receivable = $136,000

Example 4 — Net Delinquent Taxes

The allowance was $36,000 and the appeals used $14,000 of it. What is the net delinquent taxes receivable?

$
Solution

Remaining allowance = $36,000 − $14,000 = $22,000. Net = $136,000 − $22,000 = $114,000.

Net delinquent = $114,000

Example 5 — Deferral

Only $90,000 of the net delinquent amount is expected within 60 days after year-end. How much is deferred as an unavailable (deferred inflow) amount?

$
Solution

$114,000 − $90,000 = $24,000. Entry: Dr Revenues—Property Taxes, Cr Deferred Inflow of Resources—Unavailable Property Taxes.

Deferred inflow = $24,000

Interfund activity

The Four Types of Interfund Transactions

Interfund activity between funds of the same government falls into four types.

  • Services provided and used: like an arm's-length sale.
  • Transfers: one-way shifts with no repayment.
  • Loans: short-term (Due to/from) or long-term (Advance to/from).
  • Reimbursements: repayment for expenditures initially paid by another fund.
Example 1 — Name the Type

Classify each transaction.

Solution

A long-term loan requires the lending General Fund to reclassify an equal amount of fund balance from Unassigned to Nonspendable, because the receivable is not a spendable resource.

Example 2 — Reclassify for a Long-Term Loan

The General Fund lends $100,000 for three years. Choose Debit or Credit for each account in the fund balance reclassification.

    Nonspendable Fund Balance100,000

The cash left the fund, so that amount can no longer be spent.

Inventory

Consumption Method vs. Purchases Method

A fund buys $55,000 of supplies and uses $49,500 during the year.

  • Consumption method: record the purchase as an asset, then expenditures as supplies are used.
  • Purchases method: record the full purchase as an expenditure; add inventory at year-end only if it is material.
Example 1 — Consumption Method Expenditure

How much supplies expenditure does the fund report under the consumption method?

$
Solution

Only the $49,500 used is expended. The remaining $5,500 stays as an asset with a matching Nonspendable fund balance.

Expenditure = $49,500

Example 2 — Purchases Method Expenditure

How much supplies expenditure does the fund report under the purchases method?

$
Solution

The full purchase is an expenditure when bought: $55,000.

Expenditure = $55,000

Example 3 — Nonspendable Fund Balance

How much Nonspendable fund balance is reported for inventory at year-end?

$
Solution

$55,000 − $49,500 = $5,500 on hand under either method.

Nonspendable = $5,500

Expenditure accruals

How Much Is Accrued?

At year-end, employees have earned $64,000 of vacation pay, of which $8,000 has matured (a retiree is owed it). The fund also owes $31,000 of salaries for work already performed.

Example 1 — Total Accrual

How much is accrued as a fund liability?

$
Solution

Salaries are accrued in full ($31,000). For compensated absences, only the matured portion ($8,000) is accrued. Total = $39,000. The rest of the vacation liability appears only in the government-wide statements.

Accrued = $39,000

Fund balance

GASB 54 Fund Balance Classifications

Fund balance is classified by how strongly the spending of those resources is constrained.

Example 1 — Classify the Fund Balance

Choose the classification.

Solution

Nonspendable: not in spendable form. Restricted: external constraint. Committed: formal action by the highest decision-making body. Assigned: intent only. Unassigned: residual (only the General Fund can report a positive amount).

Example 2 — Total Fund Balance

A General Fund balance sheet shows assets of $410,000, liabilities of $96,000 and deferred inflows of resources of $24,000. What is total fund balance?

$
Solution

$410,000 − $96,000 − $24,000 = $290,000.

Fund balance = $290,000

General vs. special revenue

General Fund or Special Revenue Fund?

Pinecrest County runs a General Fund and several Special Revenue Funds. A government has one General Fund, but zero or many Special Revenue Funds.

Example 1 — Choose the Fund

Where does each activity belong?

Solution

The General Fund holds everything not required in another fund. Special Revenue Funds need a substantial restricted or committed revenue source.

Short-term borrowing

Borrowing on a Tax Anticipation Note

Pinecrest borrows $450,000 from a bank on a TAN to cover costs before property taxes arrive.

Short-term notes are recorded as a liability in the fund (Tax Anticipation Notes Payable), not as an other financing source. Interest is an expenditure.

Encumbrance cycle

Purchase Orders and Available Appropriations

Pinecrest issues a purchase order for $32,000 of equipment. The invoice arrives for $33,100.

Example 1 — Encumbrance Reversal

For what amount is the encumbrance reversed?

$
Solution

The original encumbered amount: $32,000.

Reversal = $32,000

Example 2 — Expenditure

What expenditure is recorded?

$
Solution

The actual invoice: $33,100.

Expenditure = $33,100

Example 3 — Available Appropriation

Appropriations total $150,000. Another open purchase order is $40,000, and expenditures to date are $62,500. What is the available appropriation?

$
Solution

$150,000 − $40,000 − $62,500 = $47,500.

Available = $47,500

Revenues and payroll

Property Taxes, Collections and Salaries

Pinecrest levies $900,000 of property taxes. During the year it collects $780,000 of taxes, $22,000 of licenses and $14,000 of fines, and pays $510,000 of salaries with $14,000 still owed at year-end.

Example 1 — Cash Received

How much cash was received from taxes, licenses and fines?

$
Solution

$780,000 + $22,000 + $14,000 = $816,000.

Cash = $816,000

Example 2 — Taxes Receivable

What is the year-end balance of Property Taxes Receivable?

$
Solution

$900,000 levied − $780,000 collected = $120,000.

Receivable = $120,000

Example 3 — Salary Expenditure

What salary expenditure is reported?

$
Solution

$510,000 paid + $14,000 accrued = $524,000.

Salary expenditure = $524,000

Year-end closing

The Two Closing Entries

Preclosing balances: Estimated Revenues $1,200,000, Appropriations $1,240,000, Budgetary Fund Balance $40,000 (debit), Revenues $1,215,000, Expenditures $1,172,000.

Governmental funds use two closing entries: one for the budgetary accounts and one for the actual operating accounts. Never combine them.

Example 2 — Closing Actual Accounts

Revenues are $1,215,000 and expenditures are $1,172,000. What amount is credited to Fund Balance in the second closing entry?

$
Solution

$1,215,000 − $1,172,000 = $43,000 excess of revenues over expenditures.

Credit to Fund Balance = $43,000

Example 3 — Ending Fund Balance

Beginning fund balance was $85,000. What is ending fund balance?

$
Solution

$85,000 + $43,000 = $128,000.

Ending fund balance = $128,000

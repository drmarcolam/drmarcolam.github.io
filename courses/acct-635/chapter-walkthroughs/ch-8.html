Employer accounting

Defined Benefit Plans and the Net Pension Liability

In a defined benefit plan the employer promises a specified benefit and bears the investment risk. In a defined contribution plan the employee bears the risk.

Net pension liability = total pension liability − plan fiduciary net position, but only if assets are in a trust that is irrevocable, dedicated to the benefits, and legally protected from creditors.

Example 1 — Annual Benefit

A retiree has a final average salary of $72,000, 25 years of service and a 1.8% multiplier. What is the annual benefit?

$
Solution

$72,000 × 25 × 1.8% = $32,400.

Annual benefit = $32,400

Example 2 — Net Pension Liability

The total pension liability is $8,400,000 and the plan's fiduciary net position is $6,150,000, held in a qualifying trust. What net pension liability does the employer report?

$
Solution

$8,400,000 − $6,150,000 = $2,250,000. Without a qualifying trust, the employer would report the full $8,400,000.

Net pension liability = $2,250,000

Example 3 — Who Bears the Risk?

Choose the party that bears investment risk.

Solution

The employer bears investment, longevity and inflation risk in a defined benefit plan.

Example 4 — Investment Return Deferral

Actual investment earnings exceeded the projection by $150,000. The difference is amortized over 5 years. What is the annual amortization?

$
Solution

$150,000 ÷ 5 = $30,000. The excess is a deferred inflow of resources.

Annual amortization = $30,000

Pension trust

A Pension Trust Fund for the Year

The Laurel Gap Pension Trust Fund records: employer contributions due $410,000, member contributions due $160,000, benefits paid $330,000, investment income $70,000, a $95,000 increase in fair value, and administrative costs of $18,000 owed to the General Fund.

Fiduciary funds use full accrual, except that benefits and refunds are recognized when due and payable. Revenues are additions and expenses are deductions.

$

$

Other fiduciary funds

Choosing the Fiduciary Fund

Fiduciary funds hold resources for people and organizations outside the government.

Example 1 — Which Fiduciary Fund?

Choose the fund type.

Solution

Pension and OPEB trusts hold employee benefit assets; investment trusts hold outside governments' pool shares; private-purpose trusts benefit outsiders; custodial funds are for other fiduciary activity.

Custodial funds

Collecting Taxes for Other Governments

The county levies $2,000,000 for itself, $900,000 for the School Board and $300,000 for the Fire District. It collects 95% of each.

The county's own portion is eliminated from the Custodial Fund statements. Only taxes held for outside governments are reported there.

Example 1 — Total Collected

How much is collected in total?

$
Solution

($2,000,000 + $900,000 + $300,000) × 95% = $3,040,000.

Total collected = $3,040,000

Example 2 — Owed to Outside Governments

How much is owed to the School Board and Fire District?

$
Solution

($900,000 + $300,000) × 95% = $1,140,000.

Owed to outsiders = $1,140,000

Example 3 — Where Does the County's Share Go?

Where is the county's own portion reported?

Solution

It is owed to the General Fund and is not reported as a Custodial Fund balance for outsiders.

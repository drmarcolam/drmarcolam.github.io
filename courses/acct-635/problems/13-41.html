Journal Entries for Contributions to a VHWO — Feed the Needy

Feed the Needy (FTN), a voluntary health and welfare organization based in Sylva, North Carolina, has three programs: (a) providing meals for the needy in its own facility (Service to Needy program); (b) providing meals for senior citizens through a contract with the Jackson County Department on Aging (Service to Seniors program); and (c) distributing food to other facilities in the area that serve the needy (Food Distribution program). FTN maintains a warehouse to store donated food and has cooking and serving facilities. It does not use fund accounting. Prepare journal entries to record the following transactions and events, which occurred during its fiscal year 2026 (which was the calendar year).

Item 1

Annual fundraising drive

On January 10, 2026, in response to its annual fundraising drive to finance day-to-day operations, FTN received $150,000 of cash donations and $400,000 of promises to donate cash. Based on experience, FTN expected to collect 80 percent of the promises made to donate cash, all of which are to be used to finance its activities during the current fiscal year.

Required: Prepare (1) the entry to record the cash donations and (2) the entry to record the promises to give, at their net realizable value.

Item 3

Conditional promise to give

To meet its expanding operations, FTN determined that it needed to add storage facilities at an estimated cost of $400,000. Dr. Ted Golfit, a noted research engineer, advised FTN that he would contribute $200,000, provided FTN raised an equal amount from other contributions.

No journal entry is required. Dr. Golfit's promise is a conditional promise to give — it depends on FTN satisfying a barrier (raising a matching amount) that has not yet occurred. Conditional promises are not recognized as contribution revenue (or as a receivable) until the condition is substantially met; until then, FTN only discloses the conditional promise in the notes to its financial statements.

FTN received a $20,000 advance payment from the county.

Item 11

Donated volunteer time

Local residents donated 1,000 hours of their time serving food at FTN's facility. If the residents had not donated their time, FTN would have paid $8 an hour for the services.

No journal entry is required. Serving food does not require the kind of specialized skill (like law, medicine, accounting, or carpentry) that GAAP requires before donated services can be recognized. Because this general volunteer labor does not meet the recognition criteria, it is not recorded — it would only be disclosed, if material, in the notes to the financial statements.

Capital Projects Fund — City of Hendersonville

Journal entries (including budgetary entries and encumbrances), a pre-closing trial balance, closing entries, a statement of revenues, expenditures, and changes in fund balance, a balance sheet, and the entries needed to reestablish the budget and encumbrances for the following fiscal year — for a city hall complex financed with general obligation bonds and a federal construction grant.

Before you start: record every entry in the Capital Projects Fund's general journal, using budgetary and control accounts only. Enter all dollar amounts in whole dollars (e.g., 25,000,000), and use the account dropdown to select the exact account title for each line. A deduction on the statement of revenues, expenditures, and changes in fund balance (a discount on bonds issued, a transfer out) should be entered as a negative number, e.g. -10,000,000 or (10,000,000). The Cash account may end the year with a credit (overdrawn) balance — that is not an error; it results from investing most of the bond proceeds while still paying two large progress billings and the year's bond interest out of a smaller cash pool. Retainage on the construction contract is a flat 5 percent throughout.
Background

City of Hendersonville, North Carolina

The following transactions occurred in the City of Hendersonville, North Carolina, during the fiscal year July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027. The city council approved the construction of a city hall complex and recorded the following budget (assume the budget is recorded in the accounts and encumbrance accounting is used):

Budgeted resourcesAmount
Federal construction grant (expenditure-driven)$25,000,000
General obligation bonds authorized (face amount)$100,000,000
Estimated investment revenue$4,000,000
Total estimated revenues and other financing sources$129,000,000
Budgeted appropriationAmount
Total appropriation for the city hall complex$120,000,000

A few days later the city signed a construction contract for the complex, at the full appropriated amount, with Jackson Construction; the contract carries a 5 percent retainage clause and its price includes the architects' fee. The buildings will be financed by the federal grant, the bond issue, and investment revenue earned during construction.

Requirement (c)

Closing entries

The general obligation bond proceeds and the federal grant revenue are restricted by the debt covenant and by the federal grant agreement for construction of the city hall complex. Neither the debt covenant nor the grant agreement says anything about how investment earnings on that money should be used. Based on authority granted to it by the city council, Hendersonville's management has decided to use investment earnings for construction of the city hall complex or, if not needed for construction, for debt service.

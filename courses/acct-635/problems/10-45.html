Background

City of Brevard, North Carolina

The City of Brevard, a small North Carolina city, is planning to issue its first set of GAAP financial statements for the year ended December 31, 2026. To prepare for the transition, the city comptroller wants to have a government-wide statement of net position as of January 1, 2026, the beginning of the year. The available information includes extracts from the governmental funds portion of the balance sheet prepared as of December 31, 2025, together with other data necessary to prepare a government-wide statement of net position for governmental activities, using the economic resources measurement focus and accrual basis of accounting.

The balance sheet for the combined governmental funds as of December 31, 2025, is as follows:

The following additional information is available as of December 31, 2025:

Capital assets: Brevard's capital asset records show that the total acquisition value of the assets in use as of December 31, 2025, is $8,400,000. Estimated accumulated depreciation on the assets is $4,600,000.

Bonds payable: Brevard has outstanding bonds payable of $2,600,000 as of December 31, 2025. Of this amount, the principal due to be paid during the calendar year 2026 is $150,000. Analysis of the outstanding bonds shows that all of the debt was used to finance the acquisition of capital assets.

Interest on long-term debt: In its governmental fund statements, Brevard recognizes interest on bonds payable when it is due and payable. It does not accrue interest for year-end stub periods that will be paid early in the following year. Stub-period interest as of December 31, 2025, was $20,000.

Property taxes: Brevard expects that all of its property taxes receivable will be collected in 2026. Property taxes of $18,000 were deferred because Brevard did not expect to collect them during the first 60 days of 2026.

Other expenses: Employees may accumulate vacation pay, subject to certain limits, that they may receive in cash on retirement. Governmental fund expenditures of $14,000 include $2,000 that the city will pay early in 2026 to retired employees. Other employees have accumulated vacation pay of $42,000 that Brevard expects to pay when they retire in future years. No accrual has been made for this amount.

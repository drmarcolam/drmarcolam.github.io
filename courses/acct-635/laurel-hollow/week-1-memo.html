Week 1 Memo: Council Briefing

Read the memo and the weekly outcomes. The assignment in Canvas explains the deliverable, the two format options and the rubric.

ToStaff Accountant
FromFinance Director, City of Laurel Hollow
ReCouncil briefing: why the city keeps separate funds

Welcome to Laurel Hollow. Our fiscal year began on July 1, and two new members joined the council this month. At their orientation they asked a fair question: “Why does the city have so many separate sets of books? A business shows one bottom line. Why can’t we?”

I would like to answer in writing before the next council meeting, and I would like you to write that answer. It will go to all seven council members, and it may be read by residents and by the local newspaper. Nobody on the council is an accountant. They are smart and busy, and they will decide how the city spends public money, so they need to understand what our statements are telling them.

Please prepare a memo to the council that does four things:

  1. Opens with a direct answer to their question.
  2. Explains, in plain language, why governments report differently from businesses.
  3. Shows where each of our major activities lives, using the fund map (twelve activities, listed in the assignment).
  4. Gives a short preview of the two sets of statements we publish, so they know what they will see at the audit presentation.

I also want you to meet one of our community partners. Pathways Together, a local nonprofit, serves families across the city, and we fund part of their work. Two of the twelve activities involve them, and you will hear much more about them in the weeks ahead.

Take the time to get this right. This is the first document the council will see from our office this year, and I expect it to read like a professional work product, not a class exercise. If you would rather explain it out loud, you may instead record a short video briefing with slides, as long as it covers the same ground.

Welcome aboard.

By the end of this week you can

  1. Explain in plain language three reasons governments report differently from businesses: accountability to citizens, interperiod equity, and the budget as legal authority.
  2. Identify the main standard setters and describe how GAAP for governments is organized, including awareness that states such as North Carolina add their own requirements.
  3. Classify each of Laurel Hollow’s twelve activities by fund category and type, and state the measurement focus and basis of accounting for each category.
  4. Distinguish the government-wide statements from the fund statements at a preview level.

Time and flow

About 15 hours: intro slides, walkthrough and chapter summary, Gate Quiz, the memo (plan on 5 to 6 hours), and a short reflection. Do the walkthrough and Gate Quiz first, because the memo uses what they teach. If you score below 80% on the Gate Quiz, choose one remedial path, then take the Mastery Quiz. The higher of the two quiz scores counts.

CPA relevance: fund classification, measurement focus and basis of accounting appear under State and Local Government Concepts in the FAR section (check the current AICPA blueprint).

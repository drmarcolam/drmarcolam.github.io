Discontinuing a Segment

Deciding whether to drop a money-losing segment by isolating the contribution margin that would be lost against the fixed costs that would actually be avoided.

Problem

Highland Craft Furniture

Highland Craft Furniture, owned by Blue Ridge Furnishings Group, builds both standard and custom size furniture at its workshop in Hickory, North Carolina, in the foothills of the Blue Ridge Mountains. Assume that the contribution income statements for both segments follow.

Contribution income statements

  Standard Custom Total

    Required: If the Custom segment is discontinued, what will be the net effect on the profit of the company?

    Work through it in steps

    Only costs that would actually go away matter. Unavoidable fixed costs are simply reallocated (often to the remaining Standard segment) and do not change total company profit — they are not part of this decision.

    ItemAmount
    (a) Contribution margin lost if Custom is dropped
    (b) Fixed costs avoided (saved) if Custom is dropped
    (c) Net effect on company profit — amount
    (c) Net effect on company profit — direction
    (d) Recommendation

    The direction and the recommendation always agree: if dropping the segment would decrease company profit, management should keep it; if dropping it would increase company profit, management should discontinue it.

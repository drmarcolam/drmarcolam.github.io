Problem

Blue Ridge Pharmaceuticals and Nantahala Therapeutics

Following are selected income statement and balance sheet data from two pharmaceutical companies for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2027 ($ millions).

Blue Ridge Pharmaceuticals, Inc. of Asheville develops and sells patent-protected branded medicines. Nantahala Therapeutics Ltd. of Bryson City manufactures mostly generic equivalents of drugs whose patents have expired.

Blue Ridge’s gross profit as a percentage of sales is considerably higher than Nantahala’s. Which aspect of its business best explains that?

  1. Blue Ridge sells a far larger volume of product, and higher volume mechanically produces a higher gross profit percentage.
  2. Blue Ridge’s patents give it exclusive rights to sell its medicines, so it can price well above manufacturing cost; Nantahala competes in generics, where rivals sell the same molecule and price falls toward cost.
  3. Blue Ridge holds a larger proportion of long-term assets, which reduces its cost of goods sold.
  4. Blue Ridge pays less income tax, which raises its gross profit.

Blue Ridge has chosen to fund itself with a lower proportion of equity, and a higher proportion of debt, than Nantahala. How does that capital structure decision affect the evaluation of the relative riskiness of these two companies?

  1. Blue Ridge carries the greater financial risk: a larger share of its assets is funded by creditors, whose interest and principal must be paid on schedule whatever the year brings, while Nantahala’s heavier reliance on equity leaves it more room to absorb a bad year.
  2. Blue Ridge is riskier simply because it is the larger company; scale is what drives financial risk.
  3. Nantahala is riskier because a greater share of its assets is current rather than long-term.
  4. Capital structure has no bearing on riskiness, since both balance sheets balance by construction.

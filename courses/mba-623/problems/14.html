Identifying Operating Income Components

Sorting an income statement into the results of running the business and the results of financing and investing — then asking which of the operating items an analyst should expect to see again next year.

How to answer: classify every line as operating (it arises from running the business), nonoperating (it arises from how the company invests spare cash or finances itself), or neither for income tax, which is levied on the combined result. Problem

Whiteside Medical Devices, Inc.

Following is the income statement information from Whiteside Medical Devices, Inc., a surgical instrument manufacturer in Highlands. Identify the components that we would consider operating.

WHITESIDE MEDICAL DEVICES, INC. — Income Statement · $ thousands
 Current year
Required: (a) Classify each line of the income statement as operating, nonoperating, or neither. (b) Confirm the operating and nonoperating subtotals. (c) Identify which of the operating charges an analyst would treat as transitory rather than persistent.

The test for operating is not whether an item is unusual or unwelcome — it is whether it arises from the business the company is in. A litigation settlement over a product is an operating cost of making and selling that product, however much management wishes it were not. Interest and securities gains, by contrast, come from the company’s cash and its capital structure, not from its devices. For any gain or loss, the question to ask is which asset produced it: one earned on an asset the company uses to make and sell its products belongs with operations, wherever the statement happens to print it.

Multiple-Product Break-Even Analysis

Weighting each product's contribution margin by its place in the sales mix to find the break-even point for a store carrying several products.

Rounding convention: do not round intermediate calculations. Carry the package contribution margin to the nearest cent and the break-even point in packages to two decimal places. Because a partial hockey stick can't be sold, round the final answer up to the next whole stick.
Problem

Blue Ridge Sporting Goods

Assume Blue Ridge Sporting Goods, an Asheville, North Carolina retailer, sells three lines of youth hockey sticks: Pisgah, Cataloochee, and Chimney Rock. Presented is information for the store's three products.

PisgahCataloocheeChimney Rock
Unit selling price$180$120$100
Unit variable costs$120$75$60
Unit contribution margin$60$45$40

With monthly fixed costs of $150,000, the store sells two Pisgah sticks for each Cataloochee, and three Cataloochee for each Chimney Rock.

Required: Determine the number of Cataloochee sticks sold at the monthly break-even point.

(a) Set up the sales mix ("package")

Start from the least-often-sold product (Chimney Rock = 1) and build up: Cataloochee sells three-for-one against Chimney Rock, and Pisgah sells two-for-one against Cataloochee. Enter the whole number of each stick contained in one package.

(b) Compute the package contribution margin

Multiply each product's unit contribution margin by its units per package, then sum the three products.

(c) Solve for the break-even point

Break-even packages = fixed costs ÷ package contribution margin. Then multiply by the Cataloochee units per package to get the number of Cataloochee sticks.

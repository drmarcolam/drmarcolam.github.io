Variable and Absorption Costing — Waynesville Woolens, Incorporated

A first-quarter loss under variable costing, a bank loan riding on the numbers, and a friend who insists absorption costing would tell a better story. Work out whether the friend is right, then see what happens in the second quarter when sales outrun production.

Before you start: Waynesville Woolens produced the same number of units in both quarters, so its absorption costing unit product cost is the same in Quarter 1 and Quarter 2. Quarter 1 builds inventory (production exceeds sales); Quarter 2 sells that entire built-up inventory plus everything produced that quarter, so ending inventory returns to zero. Problem

Waynesville Woolens, Incorporated

Elena Ruiz opened Waynesville Woolens, Incorporated, a small manufacturer of wool camp blankets, at the beginning of the year. A friend who had just finished a managerial accounting course prepared the variable costing income statement below for the first quarter, and Elena is discouraged by the loss — especially since she hoped to use the statement to support a bank loan. Another friend, a CPA, insists the company should be using absorption costing and believes it would show a profit instead.

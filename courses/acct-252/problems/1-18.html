Cost objects: two cost objects are in play here — the Outdoor Apparel Department and the Asheville Store that contains it. A cost can be direct to the store while still being indirect to the department (and every cost that is direct to the department is automatically direct to the store as well). Problem

Blue Ridge Department Stores, Inc.

The following cost data pertain to the operations of Blue Ridge Department Stores, Inc., for the month of July.

The Asheville Store is one of many stores owned and operated by the company. The Outdoor Apparel Department is one of many departments at the Asheville Store. The company's central warehouse serves all of the company's stores.

Required:
  1. What are the total direct costs of the Outdoor Apparel Department?
  2. What are the total direct costs of the Asheville Store?
  3. Of the Outdoor Apparel Department's direct costs, how much is variable with respect to total departmental sales?

(a) Classify each cost

For each cost, indicate whether it is direct (D) or indirect (I) with respect to the Outdoor Apparel Department, and separately with respect to the Asheville Store. Ask: could this cost be traced to that cost object without any allocation?

CostAmountDirect to Department?Direct to Store?

A cost that is indirect to the department can still be direct to the store — store-level costs such as the store manager's salary or store utilities are traceable to the Asheville Store as a whole, but not to any one department inside it. The central warehouse lease and the two corporate-level costs are indirect to both, since they are shared across many stores or the entire company.

(b) Compute the required totals

Use your classifications above. A cost object's total direct cost is the sum of every cost you marked "direct" to that object.

Problem 11-7

Cullowhee Mining Company

In —, Cullowhee Mining Company purchased land containing a mineral mine for —. Additional costs of — were incurred to develop the mine. Geologists estimated that — tons of ore would be extracted. After the ore is removed, the land will have a resale value of —.

To aid extraction, Cullowhee built structures and small storage buildings at the site at a cost of —. These structures have a useful life of 10 years but cannot be moved after the ore is removed and will be left at the site. New equipment costing — was also purchased and installed; it will not be moved to another site, but can be sold at auction for — after the mining project is completed.

In —, — tons of ore were extracted and sold. In —, the estimate of total tons of ore in the mine was revised from — to —. During —, — tons were extracted, of which — tons were sold.

Cullowhee uses the units-of-production method for the mining structures and equipment as well, tied to the same estimated tons used for depletion. Depletion and depreciation follow the tons extracted, not the tons sold.

Requirement 1: Compute depletion of the mine and depreciation of the mining facilities and equipment for both years.

Year one — on the original estimate

The mine’s depletable base is the land and development cost less what the land will still be worth when the ore is gone. The equipment has a residual value; the structures do not, because they are abandoned on site.

Requirement 2: Compute the book value of the mineral mine, the structures and the equipment at the end of the second year.

Careful with the mine: its book value is the whole capitalised cost — purchase price plus development — less the depletion recorded. The land’s residual value was taken out to compute the rate, not written off the asset.

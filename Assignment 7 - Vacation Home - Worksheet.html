Reference: vacation home rules (§280A)

Kyle’s personal use is more than 14 days and more than 10% of the rental days, so the condo is a residence with significant rental use: the expenses are split between rental and personal use, and the rental deductions cannot exceed the gross rental income.

Allocating the expenses — Tax Court method
  • Mortgage interest and property taxes are allocated by rental days ÷ 365, because they accrue over the whole year.
  • All other expenses are allocated by rental days ÷ total days of use (rental + personal). Days the condo sat vacant are ignored.
  • An expense that relates only to the rental activity — the management fee — is not allocated at all; it is 100% rental.
  • Under the IRS method every expense, including interest and taxes, would use rental days ÷ total days of use.

Tax Court ratio = rental days ÷ 365   Rental-use ratio = rental days ÷ days used

The three tiers and the gross-income limit
  • Tier 1 — the direct rental expense (management fee) and the rental share of mortgage interest and property taxes.
  • Tier 2 — the rental share of the operating expenses (insurance, cleaning, utilities), deductible only to the extent of the rental income that remains.
  • Tier 3 — the rental share of depreciation, deductible only to the extent of the balance left after tiers 1 and 2.
  • The rental deductions may never exceed the gross rental income, so the net rental income cannot be negative; the disallowed amounts carry forward.
Itemized deductions and the 2026 limits
  • The personal share of the condo’s mortgage interest and property taxes joins the interest and taxes on her personal residence on Schedule A.
  • The deduction for state and local taxes (property taxes plus state income taxes) is limited to $40,400 for 2026.
  • The 2026 standard deduction for a single filer is $16,100; Kyle deducts the greater of that or her itemized deductions.
